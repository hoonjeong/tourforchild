🏰 Mont-Saint-Michel

The tidal island church where the sea gallops in twice a day

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, on the coast of Normandy in France, a lonely rock rose up out of a wide sandy flat. Twice every day the sea came rushing in to visit that rock, then slowly pulled back out again. One night, a bishop named Aubert dreamed that the Archangel Michael appeared and asked him to build a church right on top of that rock. Aubert didn't quite believe it—so, legend says, the archangel poked a hole clean through the bishop's skull with one finger to make him listen! (You can still see that very skull today.) Aubert got the message, and in the year 708, he began to build.

Over the next several hundred years, monks and builders kept adding more: a church on top of a church, stairs on top of stairs, growing higher and higher like a mountain made of stone. People started calling it 'Mont-Saint-Michel'—Saint Michael's Mountain—and pilgrims picked their way carefully across the dangerous, muddy sand just to pray there.

That sea was never something to laugh at. Twice a day, it rushed across the wide flats so fast that people said it moved 'like a galloping horse'—one of the biggest tides anywhere in Europe. Mont-Saint-Michel was so hard to reach and so hard to attack that during a war between France and England that lasted more than a hundred years, French soldiers held on and the enemy never once managed to capture it.

Today, Mont-Saint-Michel is no ordinary mountain—it's a UNESCO World Heritage treasure, topped with a golden statue of the Archangel Michael shining out over the bay. For a while, a long causeway connected it to land but stopped the sea from flowing all the way around. So France built a brand-new bridge that lets the tide slip freely around the mount again. Now, whenever high tide comes home, Mont-Saint-Michel turns into a true island once more.

📍 Places to visit

📍 The Abbey of Mont-Saint-Michel

At the very top of the mount sits the abbey church, crowned with a shining gold statue of the Archangel Michael. Climb the same steps Bishop Aubert once climbed, and you'll feel like you're standing over the whole bay.

📍 La Merveille ("The Marvel")

A tall stack of Gothic halls, cloisters, and staircases built onto the north side of the abbey. Monks once walked here in quiet, between prayers—now you can walk across the very same stone floors.

📍 Grande Rue (the village's only street)

The mount's one and only street, winding steeply up between houses built right into the rock. Pilgrims and villagers have climbed this exact lane for over a thousand years.

📍 The New Bridge (Passerelle)

This bridge lets the tide flow freely all the way around the mount again, just as nature intended. Stand here as the sea rushes in, and you might just believe the old story about a galloping horse.
