🧜 Warsaw

The city rebuilt brick by brick from an artist's paintings

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, legend says a mermaid named Syrenka swam up the Vistula River and fell so in love with the riverbank that she decided to stay forever. A fisherman tried to catch her, but her enchanting song won over the local people, who promised to protect her. In return, she promised to protect their village — and that little village grew into Warsaw. Even today, a statue of the mermaid stands guard in the middle of the Old Town, sword and shield raised.

For hundreds of years, Warsaw grew into a grand capital, filled with cobblestone squares, castles, and colorful houses. But during a terrible war less than 100 years ago, the city was almost completely flattened — nine out of ten buildings in the Old Town were destroyed. When the fighting finally ended, visitors could hardly find a single street corner still standing the way it used to be.

Then something incredible happened. Long before the war, a painter named Bernardo Bellotto had painted Warsaw's streets in stunning, careful detail — every window, every rooftop, every brick. So when it came time to rebuild, architects pulled out these old paintings like a giant instruction manual and built the Old Town back exactly as it once looked, brick by brick.

Today, when you walk through Warsaw's Old Town, you're actually walking through a 'new' old city — one so faithfully rebuilt that UNESCO named it a World Heritage Site, not for being ancient, but for the incredible story of how it came back. Right beside it rise shiny modern skyscrapers, so Warsaw is a city where the past was painted back to life, standing shoulder to shoulder with the future.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Old Town Market Square

This lively square is the heart of the Old Town — and where the mermaid statue Syrenka stands guard with her sword raised. Colorful merchant houses circle the square, all rebuilt to look exactly as they did centuries ago, using those old paintings as a guide.

📍 Royal Castle

For centuries, Poland's kings lived and ruled from this castle overlooking the Vistula River. It was almost completely destroyed in the war, then patiently rebuilt room by room — even its golden clock tower rings out over the Old Town again today.

📍 Palace of Culture and Science

This towering skyscraper right in the city center was a surprise gift from a neighboring country in the 1950s, and Varsovians still have very mixed feelings about it! You can zoom up to an observation deck near the top for a bird's-eye view of the whole rebuilt city.

📍 Holy Cross Church

Look for a small pillar inside this beautiful church — it holds the heart of composer Frédéric Chopin, brought home after he died far away in France. Musicians and fans still visit to say hello to the heart of one of Poland's most beloved sons.
