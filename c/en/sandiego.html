🐼 San Diego

The sunny bay where California began — now home to pandas, sea lions, and a giant warship museum

📖 The Story

Long ago, before California was even called California, a wide bay on the edge of the Pacific was home to the Kumeyaay people. Then in 1542, a Portuguese captain named Juan Cabrillo sailed his little ship, the San Salvador, up from Mexico and dropped anchor in this calm blue bay. He was the first European to ever set foot on what is now the west coast of the United States — and that's exactly why people call San Diego 'the birthplace of California.'

More than 200 years later, in 1769, Spanish priests picked this same bay to build California's very first mission, San Diego de Alcalá. It was only the beginning — 21 missions in all went up along the coast like beads on a string, each one built about one day's walk from the next.

Today San Diego is famous for something else entirely: its weather. The sun shines almost every single day, the air stays a cozy 70 degrees, and people call it 'America's Finest City.' That sunshine helped build one of the world's most amazing zoos, where giant pandas moved back in 2024 after years away, and playful sea lions bark and nap right on the sidewalks of nearby La Jolla.

And out in the bay floats a giant gray ship called the USS Midway — once the largest ship on Earth, now a floating museum you can climb all over. Stand on its flight deck, look at the fighter jets, and you can feel just how far this little bay has come: from one explorer's ship in 1542 to a Navy giant today.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Cabrillo National Monument

This clifftop park marks the very spot where Juan Cabrillo sailed into San Diego Bay in 1542. Climb up to the old lighthouse and look out over the same blue water he saw first — on a clear day you might even spot whales swimming by.

📍 San Diego Zoo

One of the most famous zoos on Earth, tucked inside beautiful Balboa Park. Giant pandas moved back in 2024 after years away, and it was the first place outside Australia to ever raise a baby koala. Thanks to the sunshine, animals from all over the world love this climate almost as much as people do.

📍 USS Midway Museum

This gray giant was once the largest ship in the world and served the Navy for 47 years. Now you can walk its decks, sit in a real fighter jet cockpit, and imagine 4,500 sailors living aboard all at once — like a floating city.

📍 La Jolla Cove

A little beach cove where wild sea lions and harbor seals nap right on the sand and rocks, barking and sunbathing just feet from visitors. Waves have carved sea caves into the cliffs here too — some you can even walk into at low tide.
