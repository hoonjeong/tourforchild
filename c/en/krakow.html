🏰 Kraków

The dragon-slaying old capital where a trumpet call stops mid-note, every single hour

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, on a hill above a bend in the Vistula River in southern Poland, a king named Krak built a castle. A town grew up around it, and people named the city after him — Kraków. For hundreds of years it was Poland's royal capital, and its old town still looks almost exactly like it did back in the days of knights and dragons.

But legend says the hill wasn't always so safe. A fire-breathing dragon lived in a cave beneath the castle, and it loved gobbling up sheep — and sometimes people, too! No knight could ever beat it, until a clever young shoemaker named Skuba had a brilliant idea. He stuffed a sheepskin full of sulfur and left it outside the dragon's cave. The dragon swallowed it whole, and its belly began to burn like fire! It drank and drank from the river to cool the burning down — until it had drunk so much that it floated right off its feet, drifted away downstream, and was never seen in Kraków again.

Kraków has another amazing secret: unlike so many old European cities, it was barely touched by the terrible fighting of World War II. So today its cobblestone square, tall church towers, and colorful houses are almost all real, original medieval buildings — not rebuilt copies.

Every single hour, day and night, a trumpeter climbs the tower of St. Mary's Basilica and plays a tune called the hejnał — but it always stops suddenly, right in the middle of a note. Why? Centuries ago, a trumpeter spotted invaders approaching and blew the alarm to warn the city, until an arrow stopped him mid-note. Kraków has played the song unfinished ever since, as a little salute to a brave watchman.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Rynek Główny (Main Market Square)

One of Europe's biggest medieval squares. Merchants have sold their goods here for over 700 years, and it's still packed with flower stalls and horse carriages that make it feel like stepping back in time.

📍 St. Mary's Basilica

The church where a trumpeter climbs the tower every hour to play the hejnał. Stand in the square and listen for the exact moment the tune stops short.

📍 Wawel Hill — Castle & Dragon's Den

Home to Poland's kings and, legend says, a fire-breathing dragon's cave. A metal dragon statue still guards the entrance — and it actually breathes real fire!

📍 Wieliczka Salt Mine

A mysterious mine where workers carved an entire chapel deep underground — out of salt. The floors, statues, even the chandeliers are all made of salt.
