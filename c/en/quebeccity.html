🏰 Quebec City

North America's only walled city — a fairy-tale castle on a cliff above the river

📖 The Story

Long ago, in 1608, a French explorer named Samuel de Champlain sailed up a great river called the St. Lawrence and stopped where the water narrowed and tall cliffs rose above the shore. He built a small fort there and called it "Kebec" — an Indigenous word meaning "where the river narrows." That tiny fort grew into Quebec City, one of the oldest European-founded cities in all of North America.

Because Quebec sat on a cliff overlooking the river, it was a perfect spot to defend — and for hundreds of years, soldiers built thick stone walls all around the town. French soldiers, British soldiers, and colonists all wanted control of this river gateway, and the walls were tested more than once. Quebec is still the only walled city left north of Mexico — you can literally walk along the top of its old ramparts today.

Perched right on the cliff's edge stands a building that looks like it popped out of a fairy tale: the Château Frontenac, with copper-green turrets and towers. It was built more than a hundred years ago as a grand hotel for train travelers, and today it's said to be the most photographed hotel on Earth. Below it, narrow cobblestone lanes wind through "Old Quebec," lined with stone houses so charming that visitors sometimes forget they're still in North America.

Every winter, Quebec City throws one of the biggest snow parties in the world — Winter Carnival — complete with a giant snowman mascot named Bonhomme, ice sculptures taller than houses, and daring canoe races across the icy river. Old Quebec's stone walls and towers are now a UNESCO World Heritage Site, protected forever so kids like you can still walk through 400 years of history in a single afternoon.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Château Frontenac

This castle-like hotel sits right where Champlain's first fort once stood, high on the cliff above the river. Its green copper roof and towers make it look like something out of a storybook, and you can admire it for free just by strolling below.

📍 The Citadelle of Quebec

A star-shaped fort still guards the top of the cliff, and real Canadian soldiers are stationed there today. Every summer morning, you can watch soldiers in tall furry hats march in the Changing of the Guard.

📍 Petit-Champlain District

This is one of the oldest shopping streets in all of North America, tucked at the bottom of the cliff. A cute little funicular car climbs the rock face to connect it to the upper town, so you can ride instead of climbing all those stairs!

📍 Plains of Abraham

Now a big, peaceful park for picnics and sledding, this grassy field was once where a famous battle helped decide who would rule the city. Today kids fly kites here instead, right where history once changed forever.
