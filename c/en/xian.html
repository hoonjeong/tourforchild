🗿 Xi'an

The ancient capital where a secret army of 8,000 clay soldiers has stood guard for 2,000 years

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, the city we now call Xi'an was known as Chang'an, and it was the starting point of the Silk Road. Camel caravans loaded with silk, tea, and spices set off from here on a journey of thousands of kilometers, all the way to distant Rome. More than a dozen dynasties made this city their capital, and Chang'an became one of the biggest, busiest cities on Earth.

Even earlier than that, there was a boy emperor who united China into one country for the very first time: Qin Shi Huang. But Qin Shi Huang was terrified of dying, so he gave an incredible order. He commanded that an entire army made of clay be built to guard his tomb forever! Thousands of life-sized soldiers, horses, and chariots were shaped, each one with its own face, hairstyle, and armor.

This clay army lay hidden underground, completely unknown, for more than 2,000 years. Then, in 1974, some farmers were digging a well in a dry field near Xi'an when their shovels struck something hard—broken pieces of pottery. Without meaning to, they had discovered the buried army! Archaeologists dug carefully and found row after row of soldiers, still standing exactly where they'd been placed 2,000 years before.

Today, if you go there, you can see thousands of these clay soldiers, every face different, standing shoulder to shoulder—still guarding an emperor who has been gone for over two thousand years. And the massive ancient wall that once circled the whole city still stands today, too. You can even rent a bike and ride all the way around the top of it!

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The Terracotta Army has more than 8,000 soldiers, and no two faces are exactly alike.
  • 2It was found completely by accident in 1974, when farmers digging a well hit ancient pottery.
  • 3The soldiers were originally painted in bright colors, but most of the paint faded away within minutes of meeting fresh air after 2,000 years underground.
  • 4Xi'an's city wall is one of the largest and best-preserved ancient walls in the world—about 14 kilometers around, and wide enough on top to drive a car.
  • 5Xi'an was the starting point of the famous Silk Road, where camel caravans set off on journeys of over 6,000 kilometers to reach Europe.
  • 6The Big Wild Goose Pagoda was built to store scriptures a monk named Xuanzang carried home from a 17-year journey to India—the same trip that inspired the famous tale 'Journey to the West.'

📍 Places to visit

📍 Terracotta Army Museum

This is where the buried army was first found. Step inside the giant excavation pits and you'll see thousands of soldiers still lined up in formation, exactly as they were 2,000 years ago.

📍 Xi'an City Wall

Built over 600 years ago, this is one of China's oldest and most complete city walls. You can rent a bike and pedal all the way around the top of it.

📍 Big Wild Goose Pagoda

A tall brick pagoda built to hold Buddhist scriptures a monk named Xuanzang brought back from India. His real journey later inspired one of China's most famous adventure tales.

📍 Bell Tower

A beautiful tower standing right at the center of Xi'an. Its huge bell once rang out the time every morning. Climb to the top and you can see the whole city, ringed by its ancient wall.
