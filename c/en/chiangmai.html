🐘 Chiang Mai

The 700-year-old mountain capital where a white elephant chose the temple's spot

📖 The Story

Long ago, in 1296, a king named Mengrai went looking for the perfect place to build his new capital. He saw good signs everywhere — white deer bounding through the grass, white mice with babies scurrying by the river — so he decided this spot was lucky. He named his city Chiang Mai, which means 'New City,' and made it the capital of a kingdom called Lanna, the 'Land of a Million Rice Fields.'

To protect his new city, the king built a square moat and thick brick walls around it, and remnants of both still stand today, right in the middle of modern Chiang Mai. But the city needed something even more special watching over it — a holy relic to bless it.

The story goes that a monk had a piece of the Buddha's bone, so shiny it seemed to glow. To find where it belonged, they strapped it onto a white elephant and let the animal wander wherever it wished. The elephant climbed straight up a steep mountain, trumpeted three times, turned in a circle, and lay down. That very spot became Wat Phra That Doi Suthep, a golden temple that still gleams above the city today.

Every November, Chiang Mai lights up in an even more magical way. Thousands of paper lanterns are released together into the night sky for the Yi Peng festival, drifting up like a river of stars. Combined with the floating lights of Loi Krathong on the river below, the whole valley glows — a 700-year-old city still celebrating the same wonder that made a king choose it long ago.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Wat Phra That Doi Suthep

This golden temple sits high on a mountain overlooking the whole city — exactly where the legendary white elephant lay down. Climb (or ride the cable car up) 300-plus naga-flanked steps to reach the glittering chedi at the top.

📍 Wat Chedi Luang

A massive, part-crumbled brick chedi towers over the old city — it once held the Emerald Buddha until an earthquake shook its top off in 1545. Even broken, it's one of the tallest, most awe-inspiring structures in northern Thailand.

📍 Wat Phra Singh

One of Chiang Mai's most beautiful and beloved temples, home to a revered golden Buddha statue called Phra Singh. Its buildings glitter with gold leaf, colorful glass mosaics, and carved wooden gables.

📍 Tha Phae Gate

A rebuilt brick gate in the old city walls, once a main entrance for traders, elephants, and pilgrims arriving in Chiang Mai. Today it's a lively meeting spot where the Sunday Walking Street market fills the road with food, music, and lanterns.
