⛵ Split

The Roman emperor's palace people still call home today

📖 The Story

Long ago, a Roman emperor named Diocletian, tired from ruling a huge empire, decided it was finally time to retire. He picked a sparkling spot on the Adriatic coast near his hometown and gave one big order: 'Build me a palace to live in.' What rose from the ground wasn't just a fancy house—it was a massive palace with thick stone walls, temples, and even his own future tomb.

The palace was finished around the year 305, and it was so huge it felt like its own tiny walled town, with four grand gates and a wide courtyard called the Peristyle, ringed by tall marble columns. Diocletian spent his last years there, gazing out at the glittering sea he loved so much.

Years later, when the Roman Empire began to crumble and a nearby city was attacked, terrified families ran to the safest place they could find: this sturdy old palace. They didn't just hide there for a night—they built their houses right into the ancient stone walls, turned grand hallways into everyday streets, and even turned the emperor's own tomb into a church.

Here's the wildest part: people never left! Today, 1,700 years later, real families still live, run shops, and hang their laundry inside Diocletian's old palace walls. Walk through Split's Old Town and you're not peeking at a museum behind glass—you're strolling right through the middle of a living neighborhood, built inside an emperor's own home.

📍 Places to visit

📍 The Peristyle

The grand courtyard that was the palace's beating heart. Standing among the tall marble columns, it's easy to picture Roman soldiers lining up and people bowing to the emperor. It's still where Split locals love to meet up today.

📍 Cathedral of Saint Domnius

This was once Emperor Diocletian's own tomb—and now it's a cathedral. Funny twist of history: the emperor who made life hard for Christians ended up with a church for his final resting place! Climb the bell tower for a sweeping view of Split and the glittering sea.

📍 The Egyptian Sphinx

Carved from black stone, this sphinx is over 3,500 years old—the oldest thing in the whole palace. It sailed all the way from ancient Egypt long, long ago. It's been quietly guarding the square ever since, like a real-life time traveler.

📍 Bačvice Beach

This shallow, sandy beach is the birthplace of picigin, a splashy ball game where a circle of players keeps a tiny ball from ever touching the water. It's been a beloved local pastime for well over a century.
