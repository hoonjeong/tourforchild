🧜 Copenhagen

The fairy-tale harbor city where a tiny mermaid and a magic garden dream came true

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, Viking sailors needed a safe harbor to trade goods along the coast of Denmark. They found a cozy bay and built a little port town there. They called it "Køpmannæhavn"—which means "merchants' harbor"—and over hundreds of years, that name grew into "Copenhagen." Ships loaded with fish, wool, and amber came and went, and the harbor never stopped growing.

Along that same harbor, sailors built tall, narrow houses in every color you can imagine—red, yellow, blue, green—so they could spot their own home from far out at sea. That colorful street is called Nyhavn, and hundreds of years later, a storyteller named Hans Christian Andersen lived right there. He filled notebooks with fairy tales about mermaids, tin soldiers, and ugly ducklings that children all over the world still read today.

One of his tales was about a little mermaid who longed to walk on land. Copenhagen loved her so much that in 1913 they placed a bronze statue of her on a rock right in the harbor, gazing out to sea. She's much smaller than people expect—barely as tall as a six-year-old—but millions of visitors come to say hello every year.

Not far away, something even more magical opened in 1843: Tivoli Gardens, twinkling with lights, roller coasters, and fireworks. It became one of the oldest amusement parks still running in the whole world, and it was so wonderful that a young American named Walt Disney visited and went home dreaming up his own magic kingdom. Today Copenhagen zips along on thousands of bicycles, its harbor sparkles at night, and every corner still feels like it's tucked inside one of Andersen's fairy tales.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Nyhavn

This candy-colored canal was once packed with sailors, pubs, and tattoo parlors. Hans Christian Andersen lived in three different houses right along this street while he wrote his fairy tales.

📍 The Little Mermaid statue

A small bronze mermaid sits on a rock, forever gazing out to sea. She was inspired by Andersen's story, and even though pranksters have knocked her head off more than once, she always gets fixed and put right back.

📍 Tivoli Gardens

One of the oldest amusement parks in the world still spins its rides today, just as it did in 1843. Walt Disney loved it so much on a visit that it helped inspire the idea for his own Disneyland.

📍 Rosenborg Castle

Deep inside this fairy-tale castle sit the real Danish crown jewels, sparkling behind glass. Royal Guards in tall black fur hats march from the palace to Rosenborg and back every single day to keep watch.
