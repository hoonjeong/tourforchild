🏝️ Phuket

The pearl of the Andaman Sea, where an old ocean legend once saved thousands of lives

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, sailors crossing the Andaman Sea knew a lush green island rising from the water—Phuket. Beneath its jungled hills lay something valuable: shining tin. And along its shores lived families called the Moken, who spent almost their whole lives on boats, reading the ocean like a storybook.

When word of Phuket's tin got out, ships came from China, Portugal, and beyond. Traders built grand houses with colorful shutters and columned porches—a mix of Chinese and European styles found nowhere else quite like this. Their busy streets grew into what we now call Phuket Old Town, still painted in bright pastel colors today.

Then in 2004, something enormous happened. The ocean pulled back farther than anyone had ever seen, exposing the sea floor. Most tourists walked out to look, curious. But Moken elders remembered the old story of 'laboon,' the wave that eats people, passed down for generations. They shouted for everyone to run to high ground—and saved countless lives moments before a giant tsunami wave struck.

Phuket rebuilt, and today it's Thailand's largest island, famous for beautiful beaches, a giant white Buddha watching over it from a hilltop, and nearby bays full of towering limestone rocks rising straight out of the sea. But locals never forgot: sometimes the oldest stories carry the smartest warnings.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Phuket is Thailand's largest island—so big it has its own mountains, waterfalls, and even a rainforest inside it.
  • 2The 'sea gypsy' Moken people traditionally live almost their entire lives on boats, and their children can see clearly underwater almost twice as well as most people!
  • 3During the 2004 tsunami, the Moken village of Ban Tung Wa lost no lives at all—elders remembered the old 'laboon' wave legend and led everyone to high ground in time.
  • 4The Big Buddha on Phuket's hilltop stands 45 meters tall and is covered in gleaming white Burmese marble.
  • 5Phuket Old Town's rainbow-colored buildings mix Chinese and European styles into a look found almost nowhere else in the world.
  • 6Nearby James Bond Island is a tall limestone rock that leans out of the sea as if it's about to topple—and it became famous after appearing in a 007 movie.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Phuket Old Town

Stroll down streets lined with rainbow-colored mansions built long ago by tin-trading families. Peek inside and you'll often find a hidden courtyard letting in sunlight and fresh air. It's the best place to see how Chinese and European styles blended into something wonderfully new.

📍 Big Buddha Phuket

This gleaming white marble statue sits atop a hill and stands taller than a 15-story building. Climb up and you'll get one of the best views on the whole island, with the sea stretching out on both sides.

📍 Wat Chalong Temple

Phuket's most important temple, famous for its towering golden stupa holding a fragment of Buddha relics. Locals still light strings of firecrackers here for good luck—a tradition that goes back generations.

📍 Phang Nga Bay & James Bond Island

A short boat ride from Phuket, this bay is dotted with giant limestone cliffs rising straight out of turquoise water. One tall, leaning rock became world-famous after appearing in a 007 movie, and everyone still calls it 'James Bond Island.'
