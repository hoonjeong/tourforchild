♨️ Yellowstone

The world's first national park — where the ground breathes fire and rainbow pools bubble

📖 The Story

Imagine a giant, sleepy dragon snoozing right under your feet — but instead of breathing fire, it huffs hot water into the sky. That's Yellowstone! Steamy geysers hiss, rainbow-colored pools bubble, and mud pots plop like giant bowls of oatmeal. All of this happens because Yellowstone sits on top of one of the biggest volcanoes on Earth — and it's still very much alive.

Long ago, when explorers first came back from Yellowstone and told people about water shooting into the sky and mud that boiled and popped, almost nobody believed them! It sounded too wild to be true. But in 1872, the United States decided to protect this amazing land forever — making Yellowstone the very first national park in the whole world.

The star of the park is Old Faithful, a geyser so reliable it blasts a fountain of hot water dozens of meters high about every ninety minutes, like clockwork. Nearby, Grand Prismatic Spring glows in fiery orange, sunny yellow, and deep blue — not from paint, but from tiny living creatures called microbes that love to live in super-hot water.

Yellowstone isn't just fire and water — it's also a wild, wild home for animals. Huge, shaggy bison wander right across the roads, grizzly bears fish in rivers, and howling wolves roam the forests. Wolves actually disappeared from the park for almost 70 years — until people brought them back in 1995, and the whole park slowly grew wilder and healthier again.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Yellowstone became the world's very first national park in 1872 — before that, no country had ever protected a place just because it was wonderful.
  • 2There are more geysers in Yellowstone than anywhere else on Earth — over 500, more than half of all the geysers in the whole world!
  • 3Old Faithful shoots hot water up to 55 meters high — about as tall as a 15-story building — roughly every 90 minutes.
  • 4Grand Prismatic Spring's rainbow colors come from trillions of tiny microbes that love living in scalding-hot water, not from paint or dye.
  • 5The ground at Yellowstone actually rises and falls a little every year, like the giant volcano underneath is slowly breathing.
  • 6Wolves vanished from Yellowstone for almost 70 years. When they came back in 1995, they helped rivers and forests grow healthier too!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Old Faithful

This famous geyser blasts hot water dozens of meters into the air roughly every 90 minutes, almost like it's keeping its own clock. Crowds gather on benches around it just to watch the sky-high splash.

📍 Grand Prismatic Spring

The largest hot spring in the U.S., ringed in blazing orange, yellow, and green like a giant eye staring up at the sky. Those colors come from heat-loving microbes, not paint — the hotter the water, the different the color.

📍 Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone

A river carved this colorful, steep-walled canyon, and the thundering Lower Falls tumbles down twice as high as Niagara Falls. The canyon's yellow-streaked rock is actually where the park got its name!

📍 Mammoth Hot Springs

Hot water bubbling up from underground carries minerals that harden into wavy, white stone terraces — like a frozen waterfall made of rock. The terraces keep slowly growing and changing shape every single day.
