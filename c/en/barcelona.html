⛪ Barcelona

Where 2,000-year-old Roman walls meet Gaudí's still-unfinished dream of a church

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, on the sunny coast of Spain, a small Roman town was born by the Mediterranean Sea. Legend says the hero Hercules himself sailed there first, and centuries later the Romans built walls around it and called it Barcino. Today that same spot is Barcelona, and if you wander its oldest streets you can still touch stones that are almost 2,000 years old.

Long after the Romans left, an amazing builder named Antoni Gaudí fell in love with this city. He didn't like straight lines and flat walls—he thought nature never made anything that boring. So he built houses that curve like waves, roofs that ripple like dragon scales, and towers covered in sparkling broken tiles, as if a rainbow had shattered across the rooftops.

Gaudí's biggest dream was a giant church called the Sagrada Família. He started building it in 1882, but he knew he'd never see it finished in his lifetime. "My client is not in a hurry," he liked to say, because he was building it for God, one stone at a time. More than 140 years later, builders are STILL working on it—and it's finally set to be completed in 2026, exactly 100 years after Gaudí died.

Today Barcelona mixes all its ages together like a treasure chest: ancient Roman walls, Gaudí's dreamy towers, a golden beach built for the 1992 Olympics, and a soccer stadium that roars with 99,000 fans. Walk down one street and you time-travel through 2,000 years—all in a single afternoon!

  • 6FC Barcelona's Camp Nou is one of the biggest football stadiums on Earth—it holds about 99,000 cheering fans, almost as many people as a whole small town!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Sagrada Família

Gaudí's dream church has been rising since 1882 and is only now, in 2026, finally being finished. Step inside and look up—the columns branch out like a stone forest, and colored light pours through the windows like a rainbow.

📍 Park Güell

This hilltop park was meant to be a fancy neighborhood, but almost nobody wanted to live there—so it became a wonderland instead. Wander past a mosaic dragon, wavy benches, and stone columns shaped like a forest, with all of Barcelona sparkling below.

📍 Casa Batlló

Gaudí gave this house a roof like a dragon's scaly back and balconies shaped like skull-masks, turning an ordinary street into a fairy tale. Locals nicknamed it the "House of Bones" because its front looks almost alive.

📍 Roman Walls of Barri Gòtic

Tucked between shops and cafés are real stone walls and watchtowers the Romans built almost 2,000 years ago, back when this whole city was just a small fort called Barcino. Touch the ancient stone and you're touching Barcelona's very first bricks.
