🌲 Seattle

The city a fire rebuilt one story higher—with a hidden underground town below its sidewalks

📖 The Story

Long before skyscrapers, the hills and shores around what is now Seattle were home to the Duwamish people for thousands of years. Settlers arrived in the 1850s and named their growing town after a Duwamish leader, Chief Seattle. Ringed by water, forests, and a sleeping volcano called Mount Rainier, the little logging town grew fast.

But one June day in 1889, a pot of glue caught fire in a woodworking shop, and flames raced through the wooden buildings nearby. In just a few hours, almost the whole downtown burned down! Amazingly, nobody was killed, and the people of Seattle decided to rebuild bigger and stronger instead of giving up.

When they rebuilt, engineers raised the streets one whole story higher to solve a flooding problem near the water. But shopkeepers reopened at the old street level before the new roads were even finished, so for years people climbed down ladders to walk on 'basement' sidewalks that used to be the street. Today you can still explore that hidden underground city.

Seventy years later, Seattle wanted to show the world it was a city of the future, so for the 1962 World's Fair, builders raised the towering Space Needle in less than a year. Ever since, its saucer-shaped top has watched over a city famous for foggy mornings, flying fish at the market, and the very first Starbucks coffee cup. From the Space Needle you can even spot Mount Rainier glowing pink at sunset, like a giant sleepy volcano watching over it all.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The Space Needle was built in less than a year for the 1962 World's Fair, and its elevators zoom up at about 16 km/h (10 mph).
  • 2The Great Seattle Fire of 1889 burned down more than 25 city blocks—but amazingly, nobody died.
  • 3Because the streets were rebuilt one story higher, the old sidewalks became hidden tunnels you can still tour today: Seattle Underground.
  • 4At Pike Place Market, fishmongers toss whole fish through the air before wrapping them—it's become a famous show!
  • 5The very first Starbucks store ever opened near Pike Place Market back in 1971.
  • 6Seattle is nicknamed the 'Emerald City' for its evergreen forests, and on clear days you can spot Mount Rainier, a real volcano, right from downtown.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Space Needle

This 184-meter tower was built in under a year for the 1962 World's Fair, to show off a 'city of the future.' Ride the elevator up for a spinning restaurant and views for miles around.

📍 Pike Place Market

One of America's oldest farmers markets, running since 1907. Watch fishmongers toss salmon through the air, then stop by the very first Starbucks storefront nearby.

📍 Seattle Underground (Pioneer Square)

After the 1889 fire, streets here were rebuilt a whole story higher. Take a tour and walk right through the old storefronts and sidewalks that got buried below the new city.

📍 Kerry Park

A little hilltop park with the best postcard view of Seattle's skyline—and, on clear days, Mount Rainier glowing pink at sunset behind it all.
