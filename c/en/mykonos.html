🌊 Mykonos

The blindingly white island where giants turned to stone and windmills race the wind

📖 The Story

Picture a Greek island so white it looks like sugar cubes stacked up by the sea. That's Mykonos. Every house is painted snow-white, with doors and windows in bright blue, and narrow lanes twist like a maze between the buildings. People built it that way long ago to confuse pirates and block the wild wind. On a little hill above town, a row of windmills spins slowly against the sky.

Long, long ago, Greek legends said giants once fought the gods on this very island. The hero Hercules chased the giants across Mykonos and turned them to stone right where they fell. Look around today and you'll still see huge, strange boulders scattered across the island—locals say those rocks are really the giants, frozen forever.

Mykonos wasn't always full of visitors. For hundreds of years it was a quiet fishing island, and its windmills ground wheat brought by sailing ships, using the same strong winds that once scared off pirates. Down by the harbor, sea captains built houses right at the water's edge—so close that waves splash their balconies. People still call that pretty row of houses 'Little Venice.'

Just a short boat ride away floats the tiny island of Delos, where the ancient Greeks believed the twin gods Apollo and Artemis were born. Nobody is even allowed to sleep there overnight—it's that sacred! Back in Mykonos town, keep an eye out for Petros, the island's famous pelican, who waddles through the streets like he owns the place. Mykonos may look small, but it's packed with giants, gods, and one very confident bird.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Mykonos has 16 old windmills; sailors and farmers used their strong sails to grind wheat, powered by fierce winds called the meltemi.
  • 2Every house is painted blinding white on purpose—the lime wash reflects the hot sun, keeps homes cool, and even helps kill germs.
  • 3In Little Venice, houses sit so close to the sea that during storms, waves crash right up against their balconies.
  • 4Mykonos's official mascot is a pelican named Petros, rescued by a fisherman in the 1950s—today his pelican friends still stroll the streets like local celebrities.
  • 5Nearby Delos is considered one of the most sacred places in Greek mythology, believed to be the birthplace of the gods Apollo and Artemis—no one is allowed to be born or die there even today.
  • 6The giant boulders scattered around Mykonos are said in legend to be petrified giants, turned to stone by Hercules during a battle with the gods.

📍 Places to visit

📍 The Windmills of Kato Mili

These whitewashed windmills have stood on the hill above town for centuries, built to catch the island's famous wind and grind wheat into flour. Their giant wooden sails still turn today, and at sunset they glow gold above the sea.

📍 Little Venice (Alefkandra)

Colorful wooden balconies hang right over the water here, built long ago by sea captains and merchants. At night the windows light up and reflect on the waves, making it one of the prettiest sunset spots on the island.

📍 Panagia Paraportiani Church

This snow-white church looks like a sandcastle melted into wavy curves. It's actually five tiny churches built at different times and joined into one—no two Mykonos travelers agree on which side is the 'front'!

📍 Delos

This small, uninhabited island just off the coast was one of the most sacred places in the ancient Greek world, said to be where Apollo and Artemis were born. Today you can wander among 2,000-year-old temples, statues, and marble lions, all in silence.
