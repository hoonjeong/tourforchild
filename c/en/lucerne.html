🌉 Lucerne

The lakeside Swiss town with a 700-year-old wooden bridge and a lion carved into a mountain

📖 The Story

Picture a sparkling blue lake ringed by snow-capped mountains, and right where a green river spills out of it sits a tiny old town. That's Lucerne, in the very middle of Switzerland. Wooden houses lean over the water, boats bob at the docks, and if you look up, jagged peaks seem to touch the sky. For hundreds of years, this has been one of the most beautiful spots in the Alps.

Right across the river stands a long wooden bridge with a red roof, zigzagging over the water like a covered hallway. It's called the Chapel Bridge, and people built it way back in 1333 — which makes it the oldest covered wooden bridge still standing anywhere in Europe! Inside the roof, painters once filled more than a hundred wooden panels with pictures telling the story of Lucerne and its saints. Sadly, a fire one night in 1993 burned most of the bridge and its paintings — but the townspeople rebuilt it almost overnight, plank by plank, because they loved it too much to let it go.

Not far away, tucked in a quiet park, there's something even more surprising: a giant lion, carved straight into the face of a real rock cliff. Nobody built this lion out of blocks — sculptors chiseled it right into the mountain itself, over 200 years ago. It remembers brave soldiers from Lucerne who died protecting a king far away in France. The lion looks so tired and sad, lying there with a broken spear, that a famous writer once called it the most moving piece of stone in the whole world.

And when you're ready for a real adventure, a train waits to climb the steepest mountain railway track on Earth, straight up craggy Mount Pilatus. Long ago, people whispered that a dragon lived in caves near its peak — or even that a ghost haunted the summit! Nowadays you just climb aboard, and the little train grinds slowly upward until suddenly the whole lake, the town, and a hundred snowy peaks spread out beneath you like a giant map.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The Chapel Bridge was built in 1333, making it the oldest wooden covered bridge still standing in Europe.
  • 2Attached to the bridge is an eight-sided stone Water Tower that's actually even older — over the centuries it's been used as a prison, a treasury, and a watchtower.
  • 3A fire one night in 1993 destroyed most of the bridge and its painted panels, but the whole town pitched in and rebuilt it almost overnight.
  • 4The Lion Monument isn't built from blocks at all — it's carved directly into one giant slab of natural rock.
  • 5The writer Mark Twain visited and called it 'the most mournful and moving piece of rock in the world.'
  • 6The train up Mount Pilatus is the steepest cogwheel railway on Earth, climbing slopes so steep it feels like riding up a wall!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Chapel Bridge & Water Tower

This is the very bridge from the story — walk across its worn wooden planks and look up at the painted panels overhead. At one end stands the stubby stone Water Tower, which has guarded the river for over 700 years.

📍 Lion Monument

Tucked in a quiet garden, this sleeping stone lion is carved straight into a real cliff face. Look closely and you'll spot the broken spear still stuck in its side.

📍 Mount Pilatus

Ride the world's steepest cogwheel train up this legendary mountain, where people once said a dragon or a ghost lived near the top. At the summit, the view stretches over dozens of snow-capped Alps.

📍 Musegg Wall

This long stone wall with nine towers once wrapped around the whole town to protect it. You can still climb up and walk along parts of it, peeking out through the same windows guards used centuries ago.
