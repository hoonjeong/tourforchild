🍊 Valencia

The city that moved an entire river and turned its bed into a giant's playground

📖 The Story

Long ago, a Roman city grew on Spain's sunny Mediterranean coast, where orange trees bloomed and a river called the Turia flowed straight through the middle of town. Fishing boats bobbed near the beach, and just south of the city, a huge, shallow lake called the Albufera rippled with wind-blown rice paddies. This lively place became Valencia—and one day it would invent one of the world's most famous dishes.

But the same river that gave Valencia life could also turn dangerous. In October 1957, days of pounding rain sent the Turia roaring over its banks in a terrifying flood. Water rushed through streets and shops, and the city knew right away: this river could never be allowed to flood the city again.

So the people of Valencia did something bold—they built the river a brand-new path outside the city and left its old bed completely dry. Instead of paving over it with a highway, they turned the empty riverbed into a giant green park, 9 kilometers long! Today kids climb and slide across a giant sculpture named Gulliver, lying in the grass just like in the storybook, while nearby, gleaming white buildings shaped like eyes, shells, and skeletons rise where the river used to flow.

Those white buildings make up the City of Arts and Sciences, home to Europe's biggest aquarium and a planetarium shaped like a giant blinking eye. And Valencia has plenty more surprises: it's the true birthplace of paella, cooked with rice from the Albufera, and every March it explodes with the Fallas festival, when giant statues glow all night before going up in fireworks and flame.

📍 Places to visit

📍 City of Arts and Sciences

Dazzling white buildings rise right where the Turia river used to flow. Inside the eye-shaped Hemisfèric you can watch movies on a giant curved screen, and next door the Oceanogràfic—Europe's biggest aquarium—is home to sharks, dolphins, and beluga whales.

📍 Gulliver Park

A giant sculpture of Gulliver lies stretched out in the grass, just like in the storybook where tiny people tied him down. But here, kids don't tie him up—they climb all over him and slide down ropes and ladders built into his giant clothes.

📍 La Lonja de la Seda (Silk Exchange)

Five hundred years ago, silk merchants sealed their deals inside this grand Gothic hall, its twisting stone columns spiraling up to the ceiling like giant palm trees. It's so beautiful and important that UNESCO named it a World Heritage Site.

📍 Albufera Natural Park

A wide, shallow lagoon spreads out just south of the city, ringed by rice paddies that shimmer like a golden mirror at sunset. This is where Valencia's most famous dish, paella, was born—cooked with rice grown right in this water.
