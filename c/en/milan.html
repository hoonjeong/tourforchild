👗 Milan

The city with a 600-year cathedral and a painting that survived a war

📖 The Story

Picture a church so big and so fancy that builders worked on it for almost 600 years — not just one lifetime, but generation after generation, from great-great-great-grandparents all the way down to their great-great-great-grandchildren. That's the Duomo di Milano, the giant marble cathedral standing right in the middle of Milan, a busy city in northern Italy. Workers started carving its walls in 1386, and they kept adding spires and statues for centuries after that.

Milan didn't have mountains full of marble nearby, so boats had to bring huge blocks of stone all the way from quarries near Lake Maggiore. The clever trick? Milan built canals called the Navigli, and barges floated the heavy marble right into the heart of the city. Look closely at the roof today and you'll spot 135 spires and more than 3,400 statues, each one carved by hand.

While the cathedral was still growing, a curious painter named Leonardo da Vinci moved to Milan. In a quiet dining hall at a monastery, he painted a picture of Jesus having dinner with his friends — a picture we now call The Last Supper. Leonardo tried a brand-new painting technique on the wall, but it started peeling almost right away, so people have been carefully saving it ever since.

Then came a scary night in 1943, when bombs fell on Milan during a war and smashed the roof right next to that dining hall. Somehow, almost like magic, the wall holding The Last Supper stayed standing while everything around it crumbled into dust. Today you can still stand in that same room, look up at Leonardo's painting, and feel like you've traveled back more than 500 years.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Building the Duomo took almost 600 years — construction began in 1386 and the very last bronze door wasn't finished until 1965!
  • 2A golden statue called La Madonnina stands on the cathedral's tallest spire, and for centuries Milan had an unwritten rule that no building could stand taller than her.
  • 3Leonardo painted The Last Supper straight onto a dry wall instead of using real fresco technique, which is exactly why it started flaking almost right after he finished it.
  • 4During World War II, sandbags were piled up in front of the wall with The Last Supper — and when a bomb destroyed the roof next to it in 1943, that one wall stayed standing.
  • 5The Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, a giant glass-roofed arcade near the Duomo, is considered one of the world's oldest shopping malls — and its floor has a mosaic bull that people spin around on their heel for good luck.
  • 6Leonardo da Vinci helped design clever lock gates for Milan's canals, the Navigli, so boats could move up and down between different water levels — and that same basic design is still used in canals around the world today.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Duomo di Milano

This giant marble cathedral took almost 600 years to build and now has 135 spires and thousands of statues on its roof. Climb up top and you can walk right among the stone statues, with the golden Madonnina shining above you and all of Milan spread out below.

📍 Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II

A grand shopping arcade with a giant glass-and-iron dome, built way back in the 1860s. Find the floor mosaic of a bull and spin around three times on your heel right on a very sensitive spot — locals say it brings good luck!

📍 Santa Maria delle Grazie (The Last Supper)

This quiet monastery has the wall where Leonardo da Vinci painted The Last Supper over 500 years ago. Sandbags saved that very wall when bombs destroyed the building right next to it in 1943, so you can still stand in the same room and look up at Leonardo's masterpiece today.

📍 The Navigli Canals

Milan's old canal district, once used to float huge blocks of marble into the city for the Duomo. Leonardo da Vinci himself helped design the clever lock gates that let boats travel between different water levels — walk along the water today and you'll see cafes and bridges where barges used to pass.
