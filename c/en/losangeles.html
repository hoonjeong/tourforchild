🎬 Los Angeles

A tiny farm village that grew into the world's dream factory—with mammoths buried underneath

📖 The Story

Long before movie stars and freeways, Los Angeles began as a tiny farming village. In 1781, 44 settlers—families of Spanish, African, and Indigenous heritage—walked into a valley by a river and built a small pueblo. They named it after Our Lady, Queen of the Angels—"Los Angeles" simply means "The Angels" in Spanish. Back then it was just adobe houses and orchards, nothing like the giant city you know today.

For over a hundred years, Los Angeles grew slowly. Then in the early 1900s, filmmakers making the first movies started arriving from the East Coast. They loved LA's sunshine, which shone almost every single day of the year, and the amazing mix of scenery nearby—ocean, desert, mountains, and forest, all within a short drive. Soon studios popped up, and a giant sign reading "HOLLYWOODLAND" was built on a hillside to advertise new houses for sale.

That sign later lost its last four letters and became the famous HOLLYWOOD sign you can still see today, watching over a city that makes movies and shows for the whole planet. But LA holds an even older secret hidden right in the middle of the city: black pools of sticky tar that have bubbled up from underground for tens of thousands of years, trapping woolly mammoths and saber-toothed cats like a prehistoric snare.

Scientists have pulled millions of ancient bones out of that sticky tar, and you can still watch it bubble today. So Los Angeles is a strange and wonderful mix: a small farm village that grew into a dream factory for the world, sitting right on top of an ice-age time capsule. Beaches, mountains, movie magic, and mammoths—all in one city!

📍 Places to visit

📍 La Brea Tar Pits

Right in the middle of the city, black tar still bubbles up from the ground just like it has for tens of thousands of years. Scientists have pulled more than 3.5 million ice-age bones from this sticky trap, including whole saber-toothed cat skeletons. Watch the tar bubble for real, then meet the ancient animals inside the museum next door.

📍 Griffith Observatory & the Hollywood Sign

From this hilltop observatory you get the best view of the famous HOLLYWOOD sign, which once just advertised houses for sale. Step inside and you can look through real telescopes and see a show under a giant dome of stars. On a clear night, the whole twinkling city stretches out below you.

📍 El Pueblo de Los Angeles (Olvera Street)

This is the exact spot where 44 settlers built the very first Los Angeles back in 1781. Today it's a lively historic marketplace called Olvera Street, filled with colorful stalls, music, and delicious food. Walking here feels like stepping into the city's very first birthday party.

📍 TCL Chinese Theatre & Hollywood Walk of Fame

Outside this famous movie theater, hundreds of stars have pressed their hands, feet—even paw prints!—into wet cement. Right outside on the sidewalk, more than 2,700 pink stars are set into the ground, each one honoring someone who made movies, music, or TV shows. See if you can find your favorite!
