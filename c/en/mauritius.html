🏝️ Mauritius

The tiny island where a strange, wonderful bird once lived — and rainbow-colored sand still glitters

📖 The Story

Far out in the Indian Ocean, east of Africa, a volcano once pushed a tiny island up from the deep sea. No people ever lived there at first—just birds, bats, and giant tortoises who had the whole place to themselves for millions of years. One very special bird waddled through the forest: big, round, and friendly, with tiny wings it never needed, because on this safe island nothing hunted it. Sailors later gave it a funny name—the dodo.

About 400 years ago, sailors finally landed on this island and named it Mauritius. The dodo had never seen a human before and had no idea to be afraid, so it simply walked right up to say hello. Sadly, within less than a hundred years, the trusting dodo disappeared forever—and today it's the most famous example in the world of an animal that vanished too soon. Mauritius never forgot her: the dodo is still the island's proud mascot, printed on coins, flags, and t-shirts everywhere you look.

But Mauritius has more magic hiding in its ground. In a place called Chamarel, sand dunes come in seven different colors—pink, purple, brown, red, violet, green, and yellow—all naturally, with no paint at all! And near a mountain called Le Morne, if you fly overhead, the ocean plays a trick on your eyes: sand and mud sliding off an underwater cliff make it look exactly like a giant waterfall pouring beneath the sea.

Today, people whose families came from Africa, India, China, and Europe all call Mauritius home, mixing their languages, foods, and music into one joyful island culture, dancing to swaying sega rhythms on the beach at sunset. Giant tortoises now roam protected nature islands again, munching grass just like their ancestors did long before anyone else arrived. From its rainbow earth to its friendly tortoises, Mauritius proves that even a tiny dot on the map can hold enormous wonder.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The dodo bird lived only on Mauritius and nowhere else on Earth—it's the most famous example of an extinct animal in the whole world.
  • 2The sand dunes at Chamarel come in seven natural colors—and even after heavy tropical rain, the colors never mix together!
  • 3Near Le Morne, sand slipping off an underwater cliff creates an optical illusion so convincing it looks like a giant waterfall pouring into the sea.
  • 4Le Morne Brabant mountain is a UNESCO World Heritage Site—its steep cliffs once sheltered escaped slaves seeking freedom, so it's now a symbol of liberty.
  • 5Giant tortoises roam freely on protected islands like Île aux Aigrettes, and some of them are over 100 years old!
  • 6The Mauritian flag has four colors, and people on the island speak Creole, English, French, and more, all mixed into one friendly culture.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Chamarel Seven Colored Earths

A field of sand dunes glowing in seven different colors, all completely natural. Kids can walk along a boardwalk right above the rainbow dunes and try to spot every single color.

📍 Le Morne Brabant

A dramatic mountain rising straight out of the turquoise sea, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Fly above its coast and you might spot the famous 'underwater waterfall' illusion in the water below.

📍 Black River Gorges National Park

Mauritius's biggest wild forest, home to rare birds found nowhere else, like the pink pigeon and the Mauritius kestrel. Trails wind past waterfalls and deep green valleys where the island's original wilderness still survives.

📍 Île aux Aigrettes

A tiny protected island just off the coast where giant tortoises roam free and rare native plants grow the way they did before people ever arrived. It's like stepping back in time to Mauritius before the dodo disappeared.
