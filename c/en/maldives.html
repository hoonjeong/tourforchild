🏝️ Maldives

1,192 tiny coral islands where the sea glows with stars

📖 The Story

Imagine an ocean so huge that a whole country could almost hide inside it—invisible from a plane except for tiny sprinkles of green and white on endless blue. That's the Maldives: about 1,192 little coral islands scattered across the Indian Ocean, grouped into 26 ring-shaped clusters called atolls. Only around 200 of those islands have people living on them at all.

Long, long ago, volcanoes rose up from the seafloor and then slowly began to sink back down. Tiny coral creatures kept building their rocky homes around the top of each one, ring after ring, for millions of years. Eventually every volcano sank completely out of sight—but the necklace-shaped reef the corals had built stayed poking just above the waves. That's why every Maldivian island is flat, sandy, and hugged by a turquoise lagoon.

Here's something wild: the Maldives is the flattest, lowest country on Earth. Most of it sits just about 1.5 meters above the sea—roughly as high as a kitchen counter! The country worries so much about the rising sea that in 2009 its president and ministers once strapped on scuba tanks and held an entire government meeting underwater, just to show the whole world what could be lost.

But the Maldives isn't just flat—it's full of magic. At night, on some beaches, the waves glow electric blue with tiny sparkling creatures, as if the ocean were spilling over with stars. By day, gentle giant whale sharks and manta rays glide through crystal-clear water, close enough to feel like they're waving hello. No wonder people call it a slice of paradise.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Malé (the Capital)

The beating heart of the Maldives, packed onto a tiny island. Tens of thousands of people live side by side here, making it one of the most densely populated islands on Earth. Wander its narrow streets and you'll find colorful buildings squeezed in at every turn.

📍 Vaadhoo Island

A beach that glows softly blue after dark. Tiny plankton light up whenever the waves stir them, making the shoreline look like it's sprinkled with stars—people call it the 'Sea of Stars.'

📍 Hanifaru Bay

A magical bay ringed by coral reef. At certain times of year, plankton gather here in huge clouds, drawing hundreds of whale sharks and manta rays to feed together—one of the greatest ocean spectacles on the planet.

📍 Kurumba Island (Maldives' First Resort)

In 1972, the Maldives' very first resort opened its doors right here, and the world discovered this island paradise. Every dreamy overwater bungalow you see across the Maldives today traces its story back to this one island.
