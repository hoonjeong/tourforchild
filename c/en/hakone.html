🇯🇵 Japan

♨️ Hakone

A steaming volcano, a dragon's lake, and Mount Fuji—all in one mountain town

📖 The Story

Long ago, if you wanted to travel between Edo (old Tokyo) and Kyoto, you had to cross a wall of steep, misty mountains called Hakone. Samurai, merchants, and pilgrims all walked the same narrow road, resting under a long avenue of towering cedar trees planted just for them. At the top of the pass stood a strict checkpoint where guards checked every single traveler—especially watching for hidden swords and women sneaking through.

But Hakone hides a secret that's much older and much hotter. Thousands of years ago, a volcano here didn't just erupt once—it collapsed and shook the whole mountain, blocking a river and creating a deep, clear crater lake called Lake Ashi. Locals once believed a fierce nine-headed dragon lived beneath its waters, terrorizing villages, until a wise monk tamed it and turned it into the lake's guardian spirit.

To honor that dragon-turned-guardian, people built Hakone Shrine right on the shore—and set a bright red torii gate standing in the water itself, so it looks like it's floating when the lake is calm. On clear days, if you stand facing the gate, snowy Mount Fuji rises perfectly behind it, like the whole scene was made for a postcard.

And that angry volcano never really went to sleep—it just simmers now in a smoky valley called Owakudani, 'the Great Boiling Valley,' where steam still hisses out of the rocks. Visitors boil eggs right in the sulfur springs until the shells turn jet black, and everyone lines up to eat one, because legend says each black egg you eat adds seven years to your life.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Owakudani Valley

A smoky volcanic valley where the very eruption that shaped Hakone still hisses with hot steam. Try a jet-black boiled egg here—legend says it adds seven years to your life. A ropeway glides right over the steaming vents for a bird's-eye view.

📍 Lake Ashi & Hakone Shrine's Floating Torii

This deep blue lake formed when an ancient volcanic collapse blocked a river. A bright red torii gate stands right in the water, seeming to float—with Mount Fuji rising perfectly behind it on clear days. Pirate-ship-style sightseeing boats cruise right past the gate.

📍 Hakone Checkpoint (Hakone Sekisho)

A rebuilt Edo-period checkpoint where guards once inspected every traveler crossing the mountains. Try on samurai gear and peek into the watchtower where guards checked for hidden weapons. It's a fun step back into the days of the old Tokaido road.

📍 Hakone Open-Air Museum

Japan's first outdoor sculpture museum, where giant artworks stand among the misty mountains. Kids can climb inside a tower made entirely of stained glass, and there's even a foot-bath hot spring to soak tired toes while looking at art.
