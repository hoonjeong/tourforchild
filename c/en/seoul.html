🏯 Seoul

The city where a 600-year-old palace stands shoulder to shoulder with shining skyscrapers

📖 The Story

Long, long ago—more than 600 years back—a Korean king went looking for the perfect spot to build a brand-new capital. He found a valley wrapped in mountains like a folding screen, with a wide river flowing in front. He believed this land would protect the whole kingdom, so he named it Hanyang. Right in the middle, he built a magnificent palace called Gyeongbokgung, 'the palace greatly blessed by heaven.'

But the city's story wasn't always smooth. Long ago, invading soldiers set fire to the beautiful palace, and it lay in ruins for many, many years. Still, the people never gave up. They built a huge stone wall around the whole city and raised four grand gates facing north, south, east, and west, opening and closing them every morning and evening to the beat of drums and the ring of a giant bell.

As time passed, Gyeongbokgung rose again, even more dazzling than before—with a golden throne hall and a graceful pavilion that seems to float on a pond. Even today, every single day, guards dressed in bright red and blue drum and blow horns just as they did hundreds of years ago, performing the royal changing-of-the-guard ceremony for everyone to watch.

Today, Seoul is a place where the very old and the very new live side by side. Rows of curved-tile hanok roofs sit right next to shimmering glass skyscrapers. Climb up high and look down, and you'll see a giant city hugging the wide Han River, still watched over by those same old mountains. Turn down just one street, and you can step straight out of a 600-year-old palace and into the future.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Gyeongbokgung Palace

The grandest, most beautiful palace, built 600 years ago by the king who founded the Joseon dynasty. Don't miss the golden throne hall, Geunjeongjeon, and Gyeonghoeru, a pavilion that seems to float on a pond. And be sure to catch the royal guard-changing ceremony held every day!

📍 Bukchon Hanok Village

A charming neighborhood packed with hanok, traditional Korean houses topped with curved tile roofs. Wander its alleys and you'll spot tall modern buildings peeking up behind them—proof that old and new really do stand side by side here.

📍 Namsan Mountain & N Seoul Tower

Long ago, signal fires were lit on this mountain's peak to send messages across the whole kingdom. Today, climb the tower built on top and you can see all of Seoul at once—from the old palaces to the shining skyscrapers.

📍 Changdeokgung Secret Garden

A secret garden once reserved only for the king and queen to stroll through. Huge trees over 300 years old and lovely ponds remain just as they were, which is why it's been named a UNESCO World Heritage site.
