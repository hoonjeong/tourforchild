🇯🇵 Japan

🏯 Nagoya

The city where two golden fish guard a castle, and a legendary sword sleeps in a shrine

📖 The Story

Picture a castle with a roof so sparkly you can spot it from far away. That's Nagoya Castle, and instead of a flag on top, it wears two giant golden fish-tiger creatures called shachihoko, each taller than a giraffe and covered in real gold. Long ago, a powerful family named Tokugawa built this castle to guard their land, and they wanted everyone in Japan to know how rich and strong they were—so they topped it with gold.

But castles aren't the only treasure in Nagoya. Deep inside a quiet, ancient shrine called Atsuta, hidden from every visitor's eyes, sits one of Japan's most sacred objects: a legendary sword named Kusanagi. Old stories say a hero once used this very sword to cut through raging flames and escape alive. Nobody alive today has ever actually seen the sword, but people have come to pray here for more than 1,900 years.

Nagoya has another kind of magic too—the kind made in factories. Over a hundred years ago, a clever inventor named Sakichi built a weaving loom that could stop itself the instant a thread snapped. His son took that same 'figure it out and build it better' spirit and used it to make something totally different: cars. That company became Toyota, one of the biggest carmakers on Earth—and it all started with machines for weaving cloth!

These days, Nagoya keeps breaking records. Just outside the city, engineers tested a train that doesn't even touch the tracks—it floats on magnets and rockets along faster than 600 kilometers an hour, faster than any train has ever gone. Between the golden fish, the secret sword, and trains that fly, Nagoya is a city built on wonder, then and now.

  • 2When the castle burned down in World War II, the golden shachihoko fell with it. Some melted gold fragments were later recovered and are now on display inside the castle.
  • 3Atsuta Shrine is one of Japan's most important shrines, said to hold the legendary sword Kusanagi—one of Japan's three sacred imperial treasures. But it's kept hidden and shown to no one.
  • 4Toyota's very first invention wasn't a car—it was a weaving loom! Built in 1924, it could stop itself the instant a thread snapped.
  • 5In 2015, a maglev train tested near Nagoya hit 603 kilometers per hour—the fastest a train has ever traveled on rails.
  • 6Nagoya's famous hitsumabushi lets you eat the same grilled eel rice bowl three different ways—plain, with toppings, and finally poured over with hot broth like a soup!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Nagoya Castle

A castle with two giant golden shachihoko perched on its roof. Built by the powerful Tokugawa family, it burned down during the war but was rebuilt—and recovered fragments of the original gold are on display inside.

📍 Atsuta Shrine

One of Japan's oldest and most sacred shrines, said to secretly guard the legendary sword Kusanagi. Walk through its cool, towering forest of ancient trees and feel 1,900 years of history.

📍 SCMAGLEV and Railway Park

See the real maglev train that once rocketed to 603 km/h, the fastest ever recorded on rails. Kids can try train-driving simulators and watch trains float on magnets up close.

📍 Toyota Commemorative Museum of Industry and Technology

Step into the very building where Sakichi Toyoda's self-stopping looms once clattered away. Watch old machines still weaving cloth today and see how a loom company turned into a car empire.
