🏄 Gold Coast

Where an ancient volcano's green mountains meet golden, glittering waves

📖 The Story

On Australia's east coast there's a city where the sand really does look like gold under the sun — that's exactly why people call it the Gold Coast! Its beach stretches for about 70 kilometers, echoing all day with the cheers of surfers riding the waves. Long ago, real-estate sellers loved joking that this stretch of coast was 'worth its weight in gold,' and the nickname stuck so well that in 1958 it became the city's true, official name!

But the real treasure here isn't buried under the sand — it's hiding in the green mountains behind the beach. Millions of years ago, a colossal volcano erupted right where those hills now stand, one of the biggest volcanoes the Earth has ever known. Over millions of years, wind and rain wore the volcano down, carving deep valleys, tumbling waterfalls, and a rainforest so old it has trees related to ones from Antarctica.

Climb into a hidden cave here at night, and tiny blue-green lights glow all around you — but they're not fairy lights, they're glow-worms! Out on the ocean, giant humpback whales swim past every winter, often with a baby whale gliding close beside its mother, heading toward warmer waters up north.

Today the Gold Coast mixes all this natural wonder with pure fun: roller coasters racing by, wildlife parks full of kangaroos and lorikeets, and a golden tower called Q1 that once was the tallest place anyone could live in the whole world. Surfers ride some of the longest, smoothest waves on the planet just offshore. It's a place where an ancient volcano, glowing caves, giant whales, and thrill rides all share the very same golden coastline.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The name 'Gold Coast' began as a cheeky real-estate nickname in the 1950s — there's no actual gold buried in the sand, just golden sunshine!
  • 2Mount Warning (Wollumbin), in the hills behind the Gold Coast, is the eroded core of an ancient volcano that may once have been one of the biggest volcanoes on Earth.
  • 3In hidden rainforest caves nearby, tiny glow-worms light up the dark like a starry sky — they're really glowing to lure in prey to eat.
  • 4Every winter, humpback whales swim right past Gold Coast beaches with their calves, on a journey of over 10,000 kilometers.
  • 5The Q1 tower in Surfers Paradise was once the tallest residential building in the world — 78 floors shaped like an Olympic torch.
  • 6Since 1965, Gold Coast 'Meter Maids' in gold outfits have strolled the streets popping coins into parking meters for lucky visitors.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Surfers Paradise Beach

The golden heart of the Gold Coast, where the very nickname 'Gold Coast' and its surfing culture were born. Waves roll in almost every day of the year right beside the skyscrapers — the perfect spot to see why people fell in love with this coastline.

📍 SkyPoint Q1 Observation Deck

Ride an elevator to the top of Q1, once the tallest residential tower on the planet, shaped to look like an Olympic torch. From the deck you can spot the whole golden coastline — beaches, hinterland mountains, and the ancient volcano's rim — all at once.

📍 Natural Bridge, Springbrook National Park

A waterfall pours straight through a hole in an ancient volcanic rock roof into a hidden cave — and at night, that cave twinkles with hundreds of glowing glow-worms. This whole rainforest sits on the rim of the giant volcano that shaped the Gold Coast millions of years ago.

📍 Currumbin Wildlife Sanctuary

Every morning, wild rainbow lorikeets swoop in by the hundreds to be hand-fed right here — a rush of color and noisy chatter you'll never forget. It's also a wildlife hospital where injured kangaroos, koalas, and other Aussie animals are cared for and released back to the wild.
