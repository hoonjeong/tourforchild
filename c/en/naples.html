🍕 Naples

The seaside city where pizza was born—and a whole hidden world sleeps underground

📖 The Story

Long, long ago—about 2,500 years back—Greek sailors sailed into a sparkling blue bay in southern Italy and built a brand-new town. They called it Neapolis, which simply means 'new city.' Behind it loomed a tall, quiet mountain named Vesuvius, and in front of it stretched the sea, dotted with fishing boats.

Over the centuries, Neapolis grew into Naples—a big, buzzing city where Greeks, Romans, and later kings and queens all left their mark. Narrow streets filled with laundry lines, church bells, and the smell of something delicious always cooking. Naples became one of the largest, liveliest cities in all of Europe.

And here's the tastiest part: pizza was born right here! In 1889, a Naples baker made a pizza with red tomatoes, white mozzarella, and green basil—the same colors as the Italian flag—to honor a visiting queen named Margherita. People still eat that very pizza today, named after her.

But Naples hides a secret below the streets too. Dig down, and you'll find an entire second city—ancient tunnels, wells, and quarries carved by hand thousands of years ago. Up above, kids play in sunny piazzas; down below, a hidden world waits in the dark, with Vesuvius still watching over it all.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Castel dell'Ovo (Egg Castle)

A castle built right on a tiny island in the bay. Legend says the Roman poet Virgil hid a magic egg inside its walls—if it ever breaks, disaster will strike Naples! Today you can walk along its walls and look straight out at Vesuvius across the water.

📍 Naples Underground (Napoli Sotterranea)

Beneath the busy streets lies a whole hidden world—ancient Greek and Roman tunnels, wells, and quarries carved by hand over 2,000 years ago. During World War II, families even hid down here to stay safe, surrounded by candlelight and cool stone.

📍 Via San Gregorio Armeno

A whole street where artisans have handcrafted tiny nativity-scene figures for centuries—not just shepherds and kings, but tiny chefs, soccer players, and even famous people! It smells of fresh-cut wood, paint, and a little bit of Christmas all year round.

📍 Naples Cathedral (Duomo di San Gennaro)

This grand cathedral is home to a small glass vial believed to hold the blood of Naples' patron saint, Gennaro. Several times a year, crowds gather to watch and cheer as the dark, solid blood is said to turn to liquid again.
