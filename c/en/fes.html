🕌 Fes

The ancient city where donkeys deliver the mail and the world's oldest university still holds class

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, in a green valley in Morocco, two little towns grew up on either side of a river and grew until they became one great city: Fes. Camel caravans loaded with gold, salt, and spices came rumbling through, making Fes one of the busiest crossroads in the world. But the thing that made Fes truly famous wasn't gold — it was books.

More than 1,100 years ago, a clever young woman named Fatima al-Fihri used the money her father left her to build a mosque with a school attached. She named it Al-Qarawiyyin. Students came from near and far to study there, and it kept teaching new students every single year after that — so many years, in fact, that today it holds the record for the oldest university in the world that has never stopped running.

Around that ancient school grew the walled old town called Fes el Bali, a maze so tangled it has more than 9,000 narrow lanes. They're so skinny that cars have never been able to squeeze through — so donkeys and mules still trot down them every day, piled high with everything from gas canisters to fresh-baked bread, like little furry delivery trucks.

Deep inside that maze, potters and leather-workers still dye hides by hand in round stone pits packed together like a giant paint palette. People climb rooftop terraces to watch — and the moment they arrive, someone hands them a sprig of mint to hold under their nose. Step through Fes's blue gate today, and you're walking straight into thirteen centuries of history, still wide awake.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Al-Qarawiyyin University was founded in 859 AD by a young woman named Fatima al-Fihri — Guinness World Records lists it as the oldest continually operating university on Earth.
  • 2The old town of Fes el Bali has more than 9,000 narrow alleys, and cars have never fit through them — so donkeys and mules still deliver everything, working as the city's furry 'trucks.'
  • 3At the Chouara Tannery, workers dye leather by hand in round stone pits packed together like a giant paint palette, almost exactly the way people did a thousand years ago.
  • 4Visitors peeking at the tannery from a rooftop are handed a sprig of fresh mint to hold under their nose instead of a face mask!
  • 5The Blue Gate, Bab Boujeloud, is tiled blue on the side facing out of the city and green on the side facing in — blue for Fes, green for Islam.
  • 6Near the Bou Inania school hangs a 700-year-old water clock with thirteen little windows and bells — and to this day, nobody is completely sure how it worked.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Bab Boujeloud (the Blue Gate)

This grand gate is the main doorway into the old walled city. Look closely and you'll see blue tiles facing out toward new Fes, and green tiles facing in — a colorful hint that you're about to step into a very different world.

📍 University of Al Qaraouiyine

Founded in 859 by Fatima al-Fihri, this is recognized as the oldest university in the world that has never stopped teaching. Students still study here today, just as they have for over 1,100 years.

📍 Chouara Tannery

Round stone pits full of colorful dye spread out below like a giant rainbow palette. Climb to a rooftop terrace, mint sprig in hand, and watch workers turn animal hides into soft leather almost exactly as it was done a thousand years ago.

📍 Bou Inania Madrasa

Carved cedar wood, colorful tiles, and lacy plaster cover every wall of this 700-year-old school. Right across the street hangs a mysterious water clock — with thirteen windows and bells — that even historians can't fully explain.
