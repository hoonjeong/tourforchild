🏝️ Fiji

330+ emerald islands scattered across the South Pacific, where everyone greets you with 'Bula!'

📖 The Story

Out in the middle of the South Pacific Ocean lies Fiji — not one island, but more than 330 of them, scattered across the sea like a handful of green jewels. Only about a third are home to anyone at all. Wherever you go, people greet you with a big smile and a warm 'Bula!' — a word that means hello, welcome, and 'may you have good life' all at once.

People have been living on these islands for a very long time. About 3,000 years ago, brave sailors called the Lapita people paddled huge ocean-going canoes across open water and found Fiji. They left behind broken pieces of pottery buried in sand dunes near a river — pottery that archaeologists still dig up today.

Fiji is also home to one of the world's most amazing legends. Long ago on the island of Beqa, a warrior caught a spirit who lived in a hole in the ground. To be set free, the spirit promised the warrior's family a magical gift: the power to walk across burning-hot stones without ever getting hurt. To this day, Fijian firewalkers still step calmly across white-hot rocks, barefoot, smiling the whole time.

Around Fiji, the sea is bursting with color — coral reefs so big and bright they can be seen from space, filled with fish in every color of the rainbow. Fiji sits so close to the International Date Line that the line actually bends around it, so the whole country can stay together on the very same day. That means, on New Year's Eve, Fiji is one of the very first places on Earth to say hello to the brand-new year!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Sigatoka Sand Dunes National Park

Wind has piled up sand here for thousands of years, and buried underneath are pieces of pottery and bones left by Fiji's very first islanders. It's one of the oldest archaeological sites in the whole South Pacific.

📍 Beqa Island, Home of the Firewalkers

This little island is where the legend of Fiji's firewalkers began, after a warrior earned a spirit's magical gift long ago. Villagers here still perform the daring ritual, stepping calmly across glowing-hot stones without a single burn.

📍 Sawa-i-Lau Caves

Deep inside a limestone island, you can swim right into a hidden cave lit by a beam of sunlight from a hole in the ceiling, then dive through a narrow tunnel into a second, secret pool. Fijian legend says a young chief once hid his sweetheart here to keep her safe.

📍 Great Astrolabe Reef

Wrapped around Kadavu Island, this is one of the biggest barrier reefs on Earth — a rainbow world of coral where manta rays glide by like giant underwater kites. Snorkelers and divers come from all over the world just to float above it.
