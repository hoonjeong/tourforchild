🔔 Philadelphia

The city where a cracked bell rang out freedom, and America was born

📖 The Story

Two hundred and fifty years ago, thirteen colonies ruled by a faraway king stretched along the coast of America. The biggest, busiest of them all was Philadelphia. Its streets buzzed with print shops and market stalls and clattering carriages, and clever people like Benjamin Franklin were printing newspapers and dreaming up new inventions. But a question was growing louder and louder in people's minds: couldn't they govern themselves instead?

In the summer of 1776, delegates gathered inside a brick building in Philadelphia. With the windows shut tight, they argued and debated for days—then finally wrote their bold decision on a single sheet of paper: the Declaration of Independence. It promised a brand-new country built on freedom and equality. That very brick building still stands today, known as Independence Hall.

When the declaration was finished, legend says a great bell was rung to spread the news across the city. But that bell hides a funny secret—after years of ringing, a long, jagged crack split right up its side! Instead of fixing it, people decided to leave it just as it was. A cracked bell, they felt, told the story of a young country that had weathered hard times to win its freedom. Today it's beloved as the Liberty Bell.

Eleven years later, people gathered in that same building again—this time to write the rulebook for running the whole country: the Constitution. That's how Philadelphia became the city where America's biggest decisions were born. Even now, walking its streets, you can feel a spark of that thrilling summer when the Liberty Bell rang out.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The Declaration of Independence was finished in Philadelphia's Independence Hall on July 4, 1776—that's why July 4th is still America's birthday today!
  • 2The Liberty Bell has a crack nearly 60 cm (2 feet) long running up its side, and people have deliberately left it unrepaired ever since.
  • 3Eleven years later, the U.S. Constitution was written in that very same building—so a nation's birth and its rulebook were both born under one roof.
  • 4Benjamin Franklin invented the lightning rod and bifocal glasses in Philadelphia, and founded America's first public library there too.
  • 5Legend says a seamstress named Betsy Ross sewed the very first American flag right there in Philadelphia.
  • 6The long stone steps at the Philadelphia Museum of Art became famous from the movie 'Rocky'—visitors still race up them and throw their arms up just like he did!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Independence Hall

The very brick building where delegates signed the Declaration of Independence in 1776. Eleven years later, the U.S. Constitution was written here too. The old meeting room still looks just as it did back then.

📍 Liberty Bell

A giant bell displayed inside a glass-walled building right across from Independence Hall. Its long crack is its trademark. Seeing it up close, you'll understand why Americans cherish it as a symbol of freedom.

📍 Betsy Ross House

The small house where Betsy Ross, said to have sewn the first American flag, once lived. Wander its narrow stairs and old rooms and imagine the moment that very first flag came to life.

📍 Philadelphia Museum of Art (the Rocky Steps)

The wide stone steps leading up to the museum became world-famous when Rocky sprinted up them in the movie. Reach the top and throw your arms up—then you'll understand why Philadelphians are so proud of these steps.
