🌉 Sydney

The shell-roofed, bridge-topped city wrapped around the world's most beautiful harbour

📖 The Story

Long, long before Sydney had a single building, the Gadigal people lived all around this sparkling harbour. They fished, sang, and passed down stories here for more than 65,000 years—making theirs one of the oldest living cultures on Earth.

Then, in 1788, tall ships arrived from far-away Britain. The newcomers built their first tiny settlement on the shore now called The Rocks, and that little settlement slowly grew into the giant city of Sydney.

The city spread out around the glittering harbour, and in 1932 a huge steel bridge was finished, joining both shores. Its curved shape reminded people of a coathanger, so that's exactly what they started calling it. Decades later, a Danish architect's sail-shaped design won a worldwide competition, and after 14 long years of building, the Sydney Opera House rose beside the water.

Today, ferries zip across the blue harbour, and the Opera House's white roof gleams in the sun. Just outside the city you can spot koalas and kangaroos, while surfers ride the waves at Bondi Beach. Not many places mix wild nature and big-city sparkle quite like this.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The Opera House roof is covered with over a million ceramic tiles, so it sparkles like fish scales in the sunlight.
  • 2That shell-shaped design was drawn by a Danish architect named Jørn Utzon, who won a worldwide design competition to build it.
  • 3The Harbour Bridge got its nickname 'The Coathanger' because of its curved shape—and you can still climb all the way to the very top of it today!
  • 4The Gadigal people and other Aboriginal groups have lived by this harbour for more than 65,000 years, making it one of the oldest continuous cultures on Earth.
  • 5Sydney Harbour is one of the largest natural harbours in the world, so ferries zip around it like water buses.
  • 6The name Bondi comes from an Aboriginal word meaning 'water breaking over rocks'—and sometimes whales swim right past the beach!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Sydney Opera House

Its roof looks like giant seashells, or a ship's white sails, rising right over the harbour. A Danish architect's design won a worldwide contest, and it took 14 years to build.

📍 Sydney Harbour Bridge

A huge steel bridge nicknamed 'The Coathanger' because of its curved shape. It was finished in 1932, and people can still climb all the way up to its very top today.

📍 Bondi Beach

A famous stretch of golden sand and blue waves, right at the edge of the city. Surfers ride the swells here, and sometimes you can spot whales swimming by in the distance.

📍 The Rocks

This harbourside neighbourhood is where Sydney's very first tiny settlement began in 1788. Old stone buildings and narrow lanes still stand here, letting you feel what Sydney was like when it was just a small town.
