🇨🇱 Chile

🏔️ Santiago

The capital city that has the snow-capped Andes standing guard right behind it

📖 The Story

Long ago, before any Spanish person arrived, the valley where Santiago sits was home to Indigenous people called the Picunche, who lived beside a river called the Mapocho, with the Andes mountains rising behind them like a giant stone wall. Then in 1541, a Spanish captain named Pedro de Valdivia crossed mountains and desert to reach this river valley, and he decided this was the perfect spot for a new city. He picked the foot of a small rocky hill the local people called Huelén, and there he planted a flag and founded Santiago.

But this new city almost didn't survive! Just months after Valdivia founded it, while he was away, warriors attacked the town and burned nearly everything down. Yet the settlers refused to give up—they rebuilt the city again and again, brick by brick, until it grew into the beating heart of the whole country.

Santiago faced another huge challenge too: the ground itself. The city sits right on top of restless earthquake fault lines, and huge earthquakes have shaken it many times over the centuries. But Chileans turned this danger into a superpower—engineers invented special earthquake-proof building tricks, like giant rubber pads and springs under buildings, so today Santiago is one of the safest cities in the world when the ground starts to shake.

Today, if you stand in the middle of Santiago and look up, you'll see snow-capped Andes peaks glittering on almost every clear day, as if the tallest mountains in the Americas were standing guard right behind the skyscrapers. Hop on a funicular train up Cerro San Cristóbal hill, and a giant white statue of the Virgin Mary will greet you at the top, with the whole city and the Andes spread out below like a giant painting.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Plaza de Armas

This grand square is exactly where Pedro de Valdivia planted his flag in 1541 to found Santiago. Today the cathedral, old palaces, and street performers surround it, and it's still the beating heart of the whole city.

📍 Cerro Santa Lucía

This little hill was once the bare rock called Huelén where the city was born. In the 1870s it was turned into a fantasy park with hidden staircases, stone towers, and fountains, and it's still one of the best spots to look out over Santiago.

📍 Cerro San Cristóbal

A rattling funicular train, one of the oldest in South America, climbs this hill straight up to a giant white statue of the Virgin Mary with her arms open wide. From the top, the whole city spreads out below with the snow-capped Andes rising right behind it.

📍 La Moneda Palace

This grand white palace is where Chile's president works, and every other day guards march past in a colorful changing-of-the-guard ceremony with a marching band. It has stood at the center of the country's story, through calm times and stormy ones, for more than two centuries.
