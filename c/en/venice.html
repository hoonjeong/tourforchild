🚤 Venice

The city that floats on water—built on a million wooden stilts

📖 The Story

Long ago, more than 1,500 years ago, people living near the coast of Italy were being attacked again and again by fierce invaders from the north. So they did something bold: they ran into a swampy, watery lagoon that nobody else wanted, and started building their homes right on top of the water. That watery hideout grew into Venice.

Building a city on a swamp sounds impossible, but the Venetians found a clever trick. They pounded millions of wooden tree trunks straight down into the mud until they hit solid ground, then built stone buildings on top of that hidden underwater forest. Because the wood never touched air, it never rotted—it slowly turned rock-hard instead, holding Venice up for over a thousand years.

Venice grew so rich from trading spices, silk, and gold with faraway lands that it became one of the most powerful city-states in the world, ruled by a leader called the Doge. A Venetian named Marco Polo sailed and walked all the way to China and came home with amazing stories of a whole new world—stories so wild that at first nobody believed him!

Today Venice still has no roads for cars—only canals, so people get around by boat or on foot across hundreds of little bridges. Gondoliers sing as they row past grand old palaces, masked dancers twirl through the streets every Carnival, and every so often the sea rises right up into St. Mark's Square, reminding everyone that this whole magical city is still floating on water.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Venice is built on more than 100 small islands, all tied together by around 400 bridges.
  • 2The whole city stands on millions of wooden piles hammered into the mud—being underwater actually kept the wood from rotting.
  • 3There's not a single road for cars in the historic center—boats and your own two feet are the only way to get around.
  • 4Marco Polo, one of history's most famous explorers, was born in Venice and traveled all the way to China along the Silk Road.
  • 5During Carnival, people wear beautifully decorated masks—a tradition that started so nobles could wander the city without anyone knowing who they were!
  • 6Venice is slowly sinking while the sea rises, so during 'acqua alta' (high water), waves sometimes splash right across St. Mark's Square.

📍 Places to visit

📍 St. Mark's Square & Basilica

Venice's grand front room, ringed by cafés and thousands of fluttering pigeons. Inside the basilica, golden mosaics shimmer overhead like a treasure chest turned inside out. During 'acqua alta,' this is the very first place the sea comes to visit!

📍 Doge's Palace

This pink-and-white palace was home to the Doge, the powerful leader who ruled Venice for over a thousand years. A famous stone bridge connects it to the old prison next door—people called it the 'Bridge of Sighs' because of how prisoners felt crossing it one last time.

📍 Rialto Bridge

The oldest bridge across the Grand Canal, so wide it has three walkways and rows of little shops right on top of it. For centuries, this was the busy beating heart of Venice's trading world—merchants haggled here over spices, silk, and gold.

📍 Murano Island

A little island where glassblowers have shaped melted glass into swirling sculptures and sparkling beads for over 700 years. Long ago, Venice was so protective of the secret that glassmakers were forbidden to ever leave the island!
