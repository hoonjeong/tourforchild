🏖️ Cancun

The beach resort a computer helped choose—built right on top of ancient Maya wonders

📖 The Story

Long before Cancún had a single hotel, this stretch of Mexico's Yucatán coast was almost empty—just a few fishing families, swaying palm trees, and iguanas dozing on white limestone rocks. The water was already a dazzling turquoise, but almost nobody in the world had heard of the place.

Then, around fifty years ago, Mexico decided to build a brand-new vacation town from scratch—and here's the wild part: they used one of the very first computers to help choose exactly where! Engineers fed in facts about sunshine, wind, and sand from up and down the coast, and the computer pointed straight at a slim, curvy island shaped like the number 7. That island became Cancún.

Bulldozers arrived, and in just a few years empty sand dunes turned into a buzzing resort city with hotels, roads, and an airport. But when the digging began, workers discovered something amazing: the ancient Maya had already been living right there for over a thousand years! Stone temples called El Rey still stand today, tucked between the hotel towers, with iguanas sunning themselves on the old walls like tiny dragons.

Just offshore, the world's second-largest coral reef stretches for miles, home to sea turtles, rainbow fish, and quiet blue sinkholes called cenotes, which the Maya believed were doorways to another world. A short trip inland leads to Chichén Itzá, a giant stone pyramid where sunlight plays a trick twice a year, making a stone snake appear to slither down the steps. So Cancún isn't only sand and swimming pools—it's a brand-new beach town built right on top of thousands of years of Maya magic.

📍 Places to visit

📍 El Rey Ruins

Right between the hotel towers sit stone temples the Maya built over a thousand years ago, long before any resort existed. Today, iguanas sunbathe on the ancient walls like tiny dragons guarding a secret.

📍 MUSA Underwater Museum

Hundreds of life-size statues rest quietly on the seafloor here, planted by artists so that baby coral could slowly grow on them like a garden. Swim or snorkel above them and you're floating over a museum that's alive and still growing.

📍 Isla Mujeres

A quick boat ride from Cancún takes you to this small island, named 'Island of Women' after explorers found statues of the Maya goddess Ixchel here long ago. Its calm, shallow bay is one of the best spots around to swim in impossibly clear turquoise water.

📍 Chichén Itzá

A giant stone pyramid built by the Maya over a thousand years ago, without any machines to help. Twice a year, at sunset on the equinox, light and shadow team up to make a stone snake appear to slither down the steps.
