🇮🇩 Indonesia

🏙️ Jakarta

The old spice-trading port that grew into one of the world's biggest, busiest cities

📖 The Story

Hundreds of years ago, on the island of Java, there was a small harbor town called Sunda Kelapa. Wooden ships loaded with pepper and spices sailed in and out every day, and traders from India, China, and Arabia all came to buy and sell. It was already one of the busiest little ports in Asia.

Then Dutch trading ships arrived and took the town for themselves. They built a brand-new city crisscrossed with canals, a bit like a tiny Amsterdam, and called it Batavia. But Batavia was never just Dutch—Indonesian, Chinese, Arab, and European neighbors all lived and worked side by side, and each left their own flavor behind.

After Indonesia won its independence, the city took a new name: Jakarta. From then on, it grew at an astonishing speed—from a few hundred thousand people into a megacity of more than ten million, one of the biggest, busiest cities anywhere on Earth.

Today Jakarta mixes old and new in one wild jumble. A golden monument towers over glass skyscrapers, colonial buildings sit right next to shiny malls, and old wooden ships still dock at the harbor just like centuries ago. Walk through Jakarta, and you're walking through 500 years of history all at once.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Kota Tua (Old Town)

This cobblestone square was the heart of old Batavia, ringed by Dutch colonial buildings that are now museums. Horse-drawn carriages called delman still clip-clop across the square, and street performers play music just like they did generations ago.

📍 Sunda Kelapa Harbor

This is the very spot where Jakarta's story began, hundreds of years before Batavia existed. Today, colorful wooden pinisi ships still line up here, loaded by hand exactly the way they were centuries ago—it's like a living history lesson floating on the water.

📍 National Monument (Monas)

Standing over 130 meters tall, Monas marks the spot where Indonesia's independence was born, its tower shaped like a giant candle and lit with a golden flame. Ride the elevator to the very top and the whole megacity of Jakarta spreads out beneath you.

📍 Istiqlal Mosque

The biggest mosque in Southeast Asia can hold more than 200,000 people at once under its giant dome. Right across the street stands Jakarta Cathedral, and the two buildings are linked by an underground passage called the 'Tunnel of Friendship.'
