🏖️ Sardinia

The island of ancient stone towers, wild horses, and people who live to 100

📖 The Story

Long, long before Rome was even a village, mysterious builders on the island of Sardinia began stacking giant stone blocks into tall, cone-shaped towers. No mortar, no glue — just rock balanced perfectly on rock. These towers, called nuraghi, are almost 4,000 years old, and nobody knows for certain why people built so many of them.

Were they forts? Homes? Places to watch for enemies from the sea? Maybe all three! Whatever the reason, the builders worked so well that over 7,000 nuraghi still stand across Sardinia today — more ancient stone towers than anywhere else on Earth. One giant nuraghe village, Su Nuraxi, became so special it's now protected as a World Heritage treasure.

Sardinia holds another secret too: an unusual number of people here live to be 100 years old or even older. Scientists call places like this 'Blue Zones.' They think it's the fresh food, the hilly walks, and the close-knit families — whatever it is, it's made this island one of the healthiest, longest-living places on the planet.

And Sardinia's coastline hides its own kind of magic. Along one cliff, 654 steps carved into the rock lead down to Neptune's Grotto, a giant sea cave with an underground lake and a whole 'forest' of stone icicles that took thousands of years to grow, drip by drip.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Su Nuraxi di Barumini

This giant nuraghe village is the biggest and most famous of them all — a whole stone complex with towers, walls, and hidden passages nearly 4,000 years old. It's so special that it's protected as a World Heritage Site.

📍 Spiaggia del Principe, Costa Smeralda

The water here is so clear and turquoise that boats seem to float in mid-air. This stretch of coast earned the nickname 'Emerald Coast' because the sea really does shimmer like a jewel.

📍 Neptune's Grotto

Climb 654 steps carved into a cliff, or arrive by boat, to reach this giant sea cave. Inside waits an underground lake and towers of stone that dripped into shape over thousands of years.

📍 Giara di Gesturi Plateau

On this wild, rocky plateau live tiny wild horses found nowhere else on Earth, galloping free just as their ancestors did thousands of years ago.
