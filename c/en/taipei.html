🏙️ Taipei

The city where centuries-old treasures and a record-breaking tower share the same skyline

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, a wide, misty basin ringed by green mountains sat at the northern tip of Taiwan. Rivers wound through marshland where the Ketagalan people fished and farmed, calling this home. Later, settlers built a walled city here and named it Taipei — simply 'North Taiwan.' Little by little, the sleepy river town grew into a busy capital.

Then, in 1949, something extraordinary happened. As war raged across the strait, crates and crates of dazzling treasures — jade carvings, ancient paintings, emperors' jewels thousands of years old — were rushed onto ships and sailed to Taipei to keep them safe. Today those treasures fill the National Palace Museum, one of the greatest collections on Earth.

Fast forward to the 2000s, and Taipei dreamed even bigger — straight up. Engineers built Taipei 101, a shimmering tower shaped like stacked bamboo, so tall it held the world record for years. But typhoons and earthquakes shake this island often, so inside the tower they hung a giant golden ball the size of a small house, swinging gently to keep the whole building steady.

Walk through Taipei today and you can time-travel in a single afternoon: bow before ancient gods at a smoky temple, sip a bubble tea invented right here, then ride an elevator to the 89th floor and watch the sun set over the mountains. Old and new, ground and sky — Taipei holds them all together.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Taipei 101

Once the tallest building on Earth, this bamboo-shaped tower has 101 floors and a giant golden damper ball swinging inside to keep it steady in storms. Ride the super-fast elevator to the observation deck and see the whole city spread out below.

📍 National Palace Museum

Home to nearly 700,000 treasures rescued and shipped across the sea in 1949, including the famous jade 'cabbage' with its hidden grasshopper. There's so much here that even seeing a new room every day, it would take years to see it all.

📍 Longshan Temple

Built in 1738, this smoky, colorful temple honors many gods under one roof. Kids and grown-ups alike come to bow, light incense, and make a wish—for love, good grades, or good health, depending on which corner they visit.

📍 Shilin Night Market

One of Taipei's biggest and most famous night markets, packed with sizzling street food stalls, games, and the smell of stinky tofu in the air. It's the best place to taste why Taiwanese night markets are famous around the world.
