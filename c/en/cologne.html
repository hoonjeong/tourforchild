⛪ Cologne

The cathedral that took 600 years to build—and still watches over the Rhine

📖 The Story

Long ago, Roman soldiers built a fort beside a wide river in what is now Germany, and named it Colonia after a princess born there. That name slowly turned into 'Cologne,' and the mighty river became the Rhine, still flowing through the city today. Even now, if you dig under the streets of Cologne, you can find real Roman walls and pipes hiding beneath the modern shops.

Hundreds of years later, in 1248, people began building an enormous cathedral to hold a golden box said to carry the bones of the Three Wise Men from the Christmas story. But the building was so hard that the workers ran out of money and stopped—leaving a giant crane sitting on an unfinished tower for over 300 years! Finally, in 1880, workers finished the towers, and for a few years Cologne Cathedral was the tallest building in the entire world.

During World War II, bombs fell on Cologne night after night, and almost the whole city crumbled into ruins. Yet somehow, the cathedral's twin towers kept standing tall above the smoke and dust—pilots even used them to find their way in the sky. When the war ended, people looked at their broken city and saw one thing still reaching for the sky: hope.

Today Cologne has been rebuilt into a bright, lively city, but the cathedral still stands exactly where it always has. Every winter, glowing Christmas markets fill the square around it, and every spring, silly costumed Carnival parades march past. And here's a sweet secret: a perfume invented in Cologne long ago became so famous that people everywhere still call nice-smelling scents 'cologne'—named after this very city!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Cologne Cathedral

This is the giant church that took over 600 years to build! Look up at its two towers—during World War II, bombs fell all around them, but they never fell down. Inside, don't miss the huge golden shrine that people say holds bones of the Three Wise Men.

📍 Hohenzollern Bridge

A busy railway bridge right next to the cathedral, crossing the Rhine River. Its railings are covered with thousands of shiny padlocks that couples lock on for good luck—some people even toss the key into the river below!

📍 Romano-Germanic Museum

This museum sits right on top of an actual Roman house from 2,000 years ago. Its floor holds a giant, colorful stone picture called a mosaic, showing the Roman god of wine—it was found by accident while digging a bomb shelter!

📍 Chocolate Museum

A whole museum shaped like a ship, dedicated to chocolate! You can watch real chocolate being made and even dip a wafer into a bubbling chocolate fountain—a sweet treat tied to Cologne's long history of trading goods along the Rhine.
