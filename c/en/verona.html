💕 Verona

The Roman arena that never stopped singing, home to Juliet's famous balcony

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, the Romans built a city in northern Italy beside a gently curving river, and they called it Verona. Right in the middle of town, they raised a huge stone arena — big enough for tens of thousands of people to sit and watch gladiators, chariot races, and wild animal shows under the open sky.

Here's the incredible part: that arena is almost as old as the famous Colosseum in Rome, and it has never stopped being used! On warm summer nights today, opera singers still perform there with no microphones at all — their voices simply bounce off the ancient stone — and thousands of people watch under the stars, just like Romans did 2,000 years ago.

Centuries later, a poet in faraway England named William Shakespeare wrote a play about two young lovers from Verona named Romeo and Juliet. He probably never even visited the city himself, but people fell so in love with the story that today you can visit a charming old stone house with a balcony, where visitors imagine Juliet once stood and looked down at Romeo.

Every single year, thousands of letters arrive in Verona addressed simply to 'Juliet.' A group of kind volunteers called Juliet's Secretaries reads every one and writes back with gentle advice about love and friendship. Down by the river stands an ancient Roman bridge that was blown apart during a war and later rebuilt stone by stone — many of the very same stones fished right back out of the water — because Verona always finds a way to bring its past back to life.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The Verona Arena was built around AD 30 — almost as old as Rome's Colosseum — and it's one of the best-preserved Roman amphitheaters in the world.
  • 2It can seat about 15,000 people, and opera singers perform there with no microphones — the 2,000-year-old stone carries their voices perfectly.
  • 3Juliet's balcony wasn't actually built until centuries after Shakespeare's play — but that hasn't stopped millions of visitors from posing beneath it every year.
  • 4Real letters addressed only to 'Juliet, Verona, Italy' arrive from all over the world; volunteers called Juliet's Secretaries answer thousands of them every year.
  • 5Touching the right shoulder of Juliet's bronze statue is said to bring luck in love — it's rubbed so shiny you can see it glowing gold.
  • 6Ponte Pietra, Verona's Roman bridge, was destroyed in World War II and rebuilt using original stones that divers pulled back out of the river.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Verona Arena

This giant Roman arena has hosted shows for almost 2,000 years without ever really stopping. Gladiators once fought here — today opera singers fill the night air instead, with no microphones needed at all.

📍 Casa di Giulietta (Juliet's House)

A charming courtyard and stone balcony inspired by Shakespeare's famous play. Visitors from around the world leave love notes on the walls and believe that touching the bronze statue's shoulder brings luck in love.

📍 Ponte Pietra (Stone Bridge)

Verona's oldest bridge, first built by the Romans over 2,000 years ago. It was blown up during World War II, but people rebuilt it using the very same stones, fished back out of the river below.

📍 Piazza delle Erbe

Once the Roman forum, this lively square has been Verona's marketplace for over 2,000 years. Look up at the old frescoed buildings and the tall Lamberti Tower, and imagine Roman traders selling their goods here long before market stalls sold fruit and souvenirs.
