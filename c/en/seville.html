💃 Seville

The Moorish city that became Spain's golden gateway to the New World

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, in the sunny south of Spain, there was a dazzling city on a river called Seville. Back then it was ruled by Moorish kings and called Isbiliya, filled with tiled courtyards, orange-tree gardens, and busy markets. To call people to prayer five times a day, the Moors built a tall, slender tower called the Giralda—no bells at all, just a voice calling out from the top.

Then, about 800 years ago, Christian armies conquered the city. But instead of knocking everything down, they kept the beautiful tower and turned it into a bell tower, and they built a stunning royal palace called the Real Alcázar right on top of an old Moorish fortress. Muslim and Christian artists worked side by side, so the palace ended up covered in swirling patterns, horseshoe arches, and gardens fit for a fairy tale.

Then came the biggest surprise of all. Sailors discovered whole new continents across the ocean, and for centuries, only Seville was allowed to trade with them! Ships piled high with gold, silver, and strange new foods like chocolate and potatoes sailed all the way up the river into the heart of the city. Seville grew so rich that it built the largest Gothic cathedral in the world—and buried the famous explorer Christopher Columbus inside it, his tomb held aloft by four giant statues.

Today you can still feel all of that history everywhere you look. Orange trees still line the streets, flamenco dancers still stamp their heels in candlelit courtyards, and the Giralda still watches over the city just as it did 800 years ago. Walking through Seville feels so magical that movie-makers have filmed fairy-tale kingdoms and dragon queens right there—because honestly, the real story was already like a legend.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The Giralda tower has no staircase inside—just wide ramps, built so guards could ride horses all the way to the top!
  • 2A bronze weathervane statue called El Giraldillo spins on top of the tower—and it 'turning' in the wind is exactly where the name 'Giralda' comes from.
  • 3Seville Cathedral is the largest Gothic cathedral in the world, and Christopher Columbus's tomb inside is carried by four giant statues.
  • 4For almost 200 years, Seville was the only port in all of Spain allowed to trade with the Americas—every ounce of New World gold passed through here first.
  • 5The Torre del Oro (Tower of Gold) once had a giant chain stretched across the river to stop enemy ships from reaching the city.
  • 6The Real Alcázar is the oldest royal palace in Europe still used by a royal family today—and it even played the kingdom of Dorne in Game of Thrones!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Seville Cathedral & the Giralda

Climb the Giralda's gentle ramps—once used by men on horseback—for a view over the whole city. Then step inside to see Christopher Columbus's tomb, held aloft by four giant bronze kings.

📍 Real Alcázar

This dazzling palace mixes Muslim and Christian art in swirling tile patterns and horseshoe arches. It's the oldest royal palace in Europe still used by a royal family—and it starred as Dorne in Game of Thrones.

📍 Torre del Oro (Tower of Gold)

This riverside watchtower once helped guard Seville's treasure ships, with a giant chain stretched across the water to block invaders. Today you can peek inside at old maps and models of the galleons that carried New World gold home.

📍 Plaza de España

Built for a huge fair in 1929, this grand half-circle plaza has a tiled alcove for every Spanish province and a canal where you can row a little boat. It's so magical that it's shown up in movies too!
