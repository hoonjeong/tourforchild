🗿 Easter Island

The loneliest island in the Pacific, watched over by giant stone faces

📖 The Story

Right in the middle of the Pacific Ocean, with nothing but blue water in every direction, floats one tiny island. It's so far from anywhere that the nearest place people live is days away by boat. Long ago, Polynesian voyagers found this faraway island using only the stars and the waves to guide their canoes. They named it Rapa Nui.

This island has something you won't find anywhere else on Earth: giant stone faces called moai. Long ago, people carved almost 900 of these huge statues out of volcanic rock, each one shaped to honor an ancestor. Some moai are as tall as a three-story building, and a few giant unfinished ones still lie on the hillside of the quarry where they were carved, never moved.

Here's the big mystery: how did people move such heavy stones all the way from the quarry to the coast? For generations, the Rapa Nui people have told a story that the moai 'walked' there on their own. Amazingly, scientists recently tried it themselves—tying ropes around a giant moai and rocking it side to side—and it really did waddle forward, step by step, just like the legend says!

Today, descendants of the Rapa Nui people still live on the island, keeping their ancestors' stories alive. When you visit, you can stand before these giant stone faces gazing quietly out to sea, waves crashing below them. It makes you wonder what those long-ago people believed in and wanted the world to remember—one of the most mysterious islands on Earth.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Rano Raraku (the Moai Quarry)

The volcanic quarry where almost every moai was born. Dozens of unfinished giant moai still lie half-buried on the hillside, frozen mid-carving—like the sculptors just stepped away for a moment.

📍 Ahu Tongariki

Fifteen moai stand shoulder to shoulder atop the island's largest stone platform. Behind them crash the ocean waves, and beyond that rises the quarry mountain—so you can see exactly where these giants were born and where they ended up.

📍 Orongo (the Birdman Village)

A cluster of stone houses perched on a clifftop. Every spring, young men used to climb down the cliff and swim to a tiny island to fetch a bird's egg in the 'Birdman' contest—and the winner became a special leader for the year.

📍 Anakena Beach

A beach of brilliant white coral sand. Legend says this is exactly where Hotu Matu'a, the island's very first king, first landed his canoe long ago. Today, moai still stand right beside the beach, gazing out at the sea.
