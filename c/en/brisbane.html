☀️ Brisbane

The river city where you can ride a ferry, swim at a fake beach, and hug a real koala

📖 The Story

About 200 years ago, in the sunny north-east corner of Australia, there was a wide river that curled and looped like a snake. In 1824, settlers built a small outpost beside it and named it Brisbane, after the river. But this wasn't meant to be a nice place at first — it was built as a punishment town, for convicts who'd caused trouble in Sydney!

Even though the trouble-making convicts left long ago, the winding river stayed, looping so wildly around Brisbane that from above the city looks wrapped in ribbon. Today, instead of prison ships, sleek catamaran ferries called CityCats zoom up and down the water, ferrying people to work and school.

Then in 1988, Brisbane threw a giant party called World Expo. After the crowds went home, the city did something brilliant: instead of leaving an empty lot, they poured in sand and built a real beach — right in the middle of the city, nowhere near the ocean! Streets Beach is still the only beach like it in Australia, and thousands of kids splash there every single day.

But the best surprise waits just a short trip from downtown, where sleepy koalas doze in the treetops at Lone Pine, the oldest koala sanctuary on Earth. Come to Brisbane, and you can ride a river ferry, swim at a fake beach in the city center, and cuddle a real koala — all in one afternoon!

📍 Places to visit

📍 South Bank Parklands (Streets Beach)

A real sandy lagoon beach, built right in the heart of the city after the 1988 World Expo. Kids splash in the shallow water with palm trees and skyscrapers all around — no ocean required!

📍 Lone Pine Koala Sanctuary

Opened in 1927, this is the world's oldest koala sanctuary — and home to more than a hundred sleepy, cuddly koalas. It's one of the very few places on Earth where kids can actually hold one!

📍 Story Bridge

One of the largest steel cantilever bridges in the world, stretching high across the Brisbane River. Brave climbers can walk right up to the very top for a bird's-eye view of the whole curling river.

📍 Mount Coot-tha Lookout

Ride up this forest-covered mountain for the best view in Brisbane — the whole looping river and glittering city skyline spread out below you. On clear nights you can even spot koalas and possums in the nearby trees.
