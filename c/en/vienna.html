🎻 Vienna

The city where emperors lived like kings and music never stopped playing

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, a small Roman army camp stood beside the Danube River, guarding the edge of an empire. Soldiers called it Vindobona. Centuries passed, and that tiny camp grew into Vienna — a city so important that one family, the Habsburgs, ruled it as their home for almost 600 years. Kings and empresses filled its streets with palaces, and Vienna became one of the grandest capitals in all of Europe.

The Habsburgs loved music almost as much as they loved power. They filled their court with the best musicians in the world, and one day a tiny six-year-old boy named Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart came to play the piano for the empress. He was so bold that after his performance, he climbed into her lap, gave her a kiss, and asked if he could marry her daughter, the little princess! Vienna soon became known as the City of Music, home to Mozart, Beethoven, and the 'Waltz King' Johann Strauss.

High above the city, the roof of St. Stephen's Cathedral tells its own story. It's covered in more than 200,000 shiny colored tiles, arranged into a giant zigzag pattern you can only truly see from far away or up in the sky. For hundreds of years, a watchman even lived way up in the tower, keeping an eye out for fires so he could ring the bells and warn the whole city.

Vienna still hums with all that old magic today. Snow-white horses dance in slow, graceful circles at the Spanish Riding School, boys barely older than Mozart still sing in the famous Vienna Boys' Choir, and a giant Ferris wheel that opened over 120 years ago still turns slowly above the park, letting everyone see the whole beautiful city from the sky.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Schönbrunn Palace

This was the summer home of Habsburg emperors, with 1,441 rooms to explore. Right next door is the oldest zoo in the world, which opened in 1752 for the whole family to enjoy.

📍 St. Stephen's Cathedral

Look up and you'll spot a giant zigzag pattern made from over 200,000 colorful roof tiles. Climb the tower, just like the old fire watchman once did, and you can see almost all of Vienna spread out below.

📍 Spanish Riding School

Inside this elegant hall, snow-white Lipizzaner horses trot and leap in perfect time to music, just as they have for centuries. Surprisingly, every one of these horses is actually born dark and turns white only as it grows up.

📍 Giant Ferris Wheel (Prater)

This giant wheel has been turning slowly above Vienna's favorite park since 1897 — more than 120 years! Climb into one of its cozy cabins and rise up for a view over rooftops, church spires, and the whole city Mozart once called home.
