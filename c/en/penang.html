🍜 Penang

A tropical island where East and West have been mixing for 200 years

📖 The Story

Long ago, a small tropical island off the coast of Malaysia was covered in tall palm trees called betel nut palms. Sailors who passed by simply called it 'Pulau Pinang'—Betel Nut Island. Ringed by warm turquoise water and green hills, it looked like a quiet, sleepy place. But it was about to become one of the most exciting crossroads on Earth.

In 1786, a British trader named Francis Light sailed in and set up a trading post at the northeastern tip of the island, in a town he named George Town. He made it a free port, meaning ships could load and unload goods without paying extra taxes. Word spread fast, and soon ships from China, India, Arabia, and Europe were all sailing in to trade spices, silk, and tea.

People followed the ships and stayed. Chinese, Malay, Indian, Arab, and European families all built their homes side by side, and their cultures mixed together in wonderful new ways. Chinese temples, Hindu shrines, mosques, and churches popped up on the very same streets—sometimes just steps apart! Some Chinese families even built whole villages on wooden stilts right over the sea, called clan jetties, so they could live close to their boats.

Today George Town's old town is so special that the whole world agreed to protect it, and colorful street art of children and cats now dances across its ancient walls. Just outside the city, a train older than your great-great-grandmother climbs one of the steepest railways on the planet up misty, cool Penang Hill. And at the golden-lit Kek Lok Si Temple, one of the largest Buddhist temples in all of Southeast Asia, thousands of lanterns glow like stars brought down to earth. One little island, holding a whole world inside it!

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Penang got its name from betel nut palm trees—'Pulau Pinang' means 'Betel Nut Island' in Malay.
  • 2George Town was named a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2008 for its one-of-a-kind mix of cultures and buildings.
  • 3A mural of two kids on a real bicycle, painted by artist Ernest Zacharevic, became so famous it started a whole city of street art—now there are hundreds of murals!
  • 4The clan jetties are entire villages built on wooden stilts over the sea. Families have lived there for more than 100 years, right above the water.
  • 5The Penang Hill funicular railway is one of the steepest in the world, and the summit can be about 5°C cooler than down by the beach.
  • 6Kek Lok Si, one of the largest Buddhist temples in Southeast Asia, lights up with thousands of lanterns for Chinese New Year—like a hillside full of stars.

📍 Places to visit

📍 George Town Street Art

Old alley walls are covered in paintings of kids, cats, and bicycles. Everyone stops to pose with the famous mural of two children riding a real bicycle.

📍 Penang Hill

Ride a funicular train over a hundred years old up one of the steepest railways on Earth, and the cool summit reveals the whole island and sea below.

📍 Kek Lok Si Temple

One of the largest Buddhist temples in Southeast Asia, with pagodas and staircases climbing up the hillside. At Chinese New Year, thousands of lanterns light up the whole slope.

📍 Clan Jetties

A whole village built on wooden stilts over the water. Families sharing the same surname have lived here, right above the sea, for more than a hundred years.
