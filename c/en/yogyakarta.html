🛕 Yogyakarta

The city with a real living king, next door to two sleeping stone giants

📖 The Story

On the island of Java in Indonesia sits a city like no other: Yogyakarta still has a real king. He's called the Sultan, and he lives in a palace called the Kraton, right in the middle of the city, just as sultans have for hundreds of years. Step through the gates and you might spot palace guards in batik sarongs and dancers practicing for a royal ceremony.

But Yogyakarta's biggest secret isn't in the palace—it's hiding in the jungle nearby. Over 1,200 years ago, thousands of workers stacked more than two million stone blocks into a giant temple shaped like a mountain, called Borobudur. It was built so you walk around and around it, climbing higher each time, like a giant stone maze leading up to the sky. Then a volcano erupted and the jungle grew wild, and Borobudur vanished under ash and vines for centuries—until people found it hiding there again.

Not far away stands Borobudur's rival: Prambanan, a cluster of soaring stone towers built for Hindu gods around the same time. Carved all over its walls is the tale of a prince, a princess, and a monkey army—the ancient story of the Ramayana. On some nights, dancers still act out that very story on a stage right beside the glowing towers, under real stars.

Watching over the whole valley is Mount Merapi, one of the most active volcanoes on Earth—its name literally means 'mountain of fire.' Its ash makes the soil incredibly rich, which is why the rice fields around Yogyakarta are so green. Between a living sultan, sleeping stone giants, and a fire-breathing mountain, Yogyakarta feels like a story that never stopped being told.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Borobudur is the largest Buddhist temple in the world, built from over 2 million stone blocks—with no cement holding them together!
  • 2Seen from the sky, Borobudur's base forms a giant mandala pattern, and monks say walking its circular paths again and again is like a journey toward enlightenment.
  • 3Prambanan's tallest tower rises about 47 meters—taller than a 15-story building—making it the tallest Hindu temple in Indonesia.
  • 4Yogyakarta is the only place in Indonesia still ruled by an actual king today—the Sultan also serves as the region's governor.
  • 5Mount Merapi erupts so often that scientists watch it almost every day; its ash has buried and uncovered temples for over a thousand years.
  • 6Malioboro Street fills up every evening with horse-drawn carriages called andong and pedicabs called becak, which people still ride to get around today.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Kraton Yogyakarta (Sultan's Palace)

The living palace of Yogyakarta's Sultan, who still rules from here today. Wander through courtyards where royal dancers and gamelan musicians once performed for kings and queens—and sometimes still do.

📍 Borobudur Temple

The largest Buddhist temple on Earth, built like a giant stone mountain over 1,200 years ago. Climb its levels and pass carvings of an ancient legend before reaching the very top, facing the open sky.

📍 Prambanan Temple

Soaring stone towers built for Hindu gods, carved from top to bottom with the story of the Ramayana. At night, dancers still perform that very legend on a stage beside the glowing towers.

📍 Taman Sari (Royal Water Castle)

A secret garden of pools once built for the sultan's family to swim, relax, and slip away unseen through hidden tunnels. Legend even says one passage stretched all the way to the sea.
