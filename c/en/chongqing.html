🌆 Chongqing

The cliffside 'Mountain City' where two rivers collide

📖 The Story

Picture a city with no flat ground at all. Chongqing sits where two mighty rivers, the Yangtze and the Jialing, crash together in southwest China. The land around them is nothing but steep hills and cliffs, so the whole city had to grow straight up instead of out. That's why everyone calls it the 'Mountain City.'

Because everything tilts and climbs, ordinary streets just weren't enough. Chongqing built giant outdoor escalators and elevators to carry people up the cliffs, and roads stack on top of each other like layers of a cake. Walk in the front door of some buildings and you're on the ground floor. Walk out the back door of the very same building, and you're eight floors up!

But the wildest trick of all happens at a train station called Liziba. There, the whole city dips so steeply that engineers didn't build the monorail around an apartment building — they built it straight through one! Real people live in the rooms above and below, and every few minutes a monorail train zooms right through a hole in the middle of the 19-story tower.

When night falls, Chongqing saves its best magic for last. Along the riverbank, a tower of wooden stilt houses called Hongya Cave climbs eleven stories up the cliff, and every single window glows gold. Down in the streets below, families gather around bubbling, spicy hot pot to warm up in the misty air. Look up at those golden lights reflected in the river, and you'll swear you've wandered straight into an animated movie.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Liziba Monorail Station

This is where the famous 'train through a building' happens. A monorail dives straight through a hole in the middle of a 19-story apartment tower, and the people who live there barely notice anymore! Crowds line up at a viewing platform just to watch a train vanish into someone's home.

📍 Hongya Cave

A cliffside village of wooden stilt houses stacked eleven stories high. Wander its wooden walkways and you'll find snack shops tucked right into the cliff. Many visitors say it looks exactly like a town from an animated fantasy film.

📍 Yangtze River Cableway

Before big bridges crossed the rivers, people rode cable cars through the sky instead. From inside a little cabin, the whole misty 'Mountain City' spreads out below you. It's still one of the best ways to feel how Chongqing rises straight up out of the water.

📍 Chaotianmen

This is the exact spot where the muddy Yangtze and the greener Jialing River crash together. Ships have gathered here for centuries, and honestly, this river meeting is the whole reason Chongqing exists. Look closely at the water and you can watch the two rivers' different colors swirl together.
