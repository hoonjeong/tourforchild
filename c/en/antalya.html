🏖️ Antalya

The Turquoise Coast where an ancient king found 'heaven on earth'

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, there was a king named Attalus II, who ruled a kingdom called Pergamon. He wanted to build a brand-new city in the most beautiful spot he could find, so he sent his men far along the coastline to search for the perfect place. When they finally found a sparkling turquoise bay, ringed with fragrant orange groves and tall mountains rising behind it, they told the king they had found 'heaven on earth.' So the king built his city right there and named it Attaleia — the very city we now call Antalya.

Centuries later the Romans took over, and in the year 130 AD the great emperor Hadrian himself came to visit. To welcome him, the people built a dazzling triple gate of gleaming white marble. That gate still stands today, and if you walk through it, your feet tread on the very same smooth marble that Roman sandals wore down almost 2,000 years ago.

But Antalya has a wonder that not even the Romans could build — waterfalls that plunge straight off cliffs into the sea! Just outside the city, the Düden Waterfalls tumble roaring over rocky cliffs and crash directly into the deep-blue Mediterranean. Boats can sail right up close so you can feel the cool spray on your face, and places where a waterfall meets the open sea like this are rare anywhere on Earth.

Not far away sits an even greater marvel: the Aspendos theatre, carved into a hillside almost 1,900 years ago. It could seat as many as 20,000 people, and it was built so cleverly that even a whisper from the stage can be heard clearly in the very top row — no microphones needed! Today, musicians still perform there under the stars, filling the same stone seats with song, just like they did two thousand years ago.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Hadrian's Gate

This gleaming triple archway was built in 130 AD to welcome Roman Emperor Hadrian on his visit. Look down as you walk through — the marble is worn smooth by 2,000 years of footsteps, including maybe an emperor's own.

📍 Düden Waterfalls

Here, a river tumbles right off a cliff and crashes straight into the Mediterranean Sea. Hop on a boat to sail up close and feel the cool spray — it's one of the rare spots on Earth where a waterfall meets the open sea.

📍 Aspendos Ancient Theatre

Carved into a hillside almost 1,900 years ago, this theatre seats up to 20,000 people and still has near-perfect acoustics. Musicians perform here today with no microphones — just like the actors did two millennia ago.

📍 Kaleiçi Old Town

A maze of narrow cobblestone lanes tucked inside ancient city walls, dotted with old wooden Ottoman houses and a harbor that ships have used since Roman times. It's the perfect place to get a little lost and discover something new.
