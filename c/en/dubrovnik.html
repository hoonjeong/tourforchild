🐉 Dubrovnik

The tiny sea republic that built a wall no army could break

📖 The Story

Long ago, on the Adriatic coast, a small city called Ragusa (today's Dubrovnik) decided it would never bow to a bigger neighbor. Though tiny, it built itself into a proud sea-trading power, sending ships loaded with silver, spices, and cloth all across the Mediterranean. Its pride was one word carved everywhere around town: Libertas — Freedom.

To protect that freedom, the people built something enormous around their entire city: a wall of stone nearly 2 kilometers around, some parts climbing 25 meters high — taller than a six-story building! Towers and fortresses guarded every gate, watching the sea for danger, and for centuries no invading army ever broke through. Ragusa stayed independent by being clever too, paying tribute to powerful neighbors instead of fighting, and becoming famous for skilled diplomats and traders.

But nature did what armies could not. In 1667, a huge earthquake shook the city and knocked down most of its beautiful buildings in minutes. Still, the great walls held strong, and the people rebuilt their home stone by stone, bringing back its wide marble streets and grand palaces.

Centuries later, in 1991, the walled city faced danger again during a war. This time, people from all over the world helped protect and repair it. Today Dubrovnik shines brighter than ever — its walls open for visitors to walk all the way around, its harbor still full of little boats, and its stone streets so polished by millions of footsteps that they gleam like mirrors.

📍 Places to visit

📍 The City Walls

Nearly 2 km of stone circling the entire Old Town, with towers to climb and sea views all around. Walk the full loop and you'll understand why no enemy ever got in.

📍 Lovrijenac Fortress

Perched on a cliff just outside the western wall, this fortress was supposedly built in just three months to stop a rival power from building there first. Locals nickname it 'Dubrovnik's Gibraltar.'

📍 Stradun

The city's grand limestone main street, lined with old stone houses and cafés. Look down at your feet — the stone has been polished so smooth by centuries of footsteps that it shines like glass.

📍 Franciscan Monastery Old Pharmacy

Tucked inside a monastery, this tiny pharmacy has been mixing remedies since 1317 — one of the oldest still working in Europe. Peer in at the old jars and tools monks used centuries ago.
