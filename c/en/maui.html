🏝️ Maui

The island named for a hero who lassoed the sun, where whales sing and roads wind past waterfalls

📖 The Story

Long ago in Hawaiian legend, there lived a clever demigod named Maui — and this island carries his name. His mother complained that the days were too short: the sun raced across the sky so fast, her cloth never had time to dry. So Maui climbed to the very top of a giant volcano, waited for the sun to rise, and lassoed it with a rope made of his sister's hair!

He made the sun promise to move slower across the sky, and ever since, days on Maui have felt long and golden. That volcano is called Haleakala, meaning 'House of the Sun.' Even today, people wake up in the dark and hike to its summit — over 3,000 meters high — just to watch the sunrise turn the sky pink and gold above a landscape so rocky and red, it looks like Mars.

Down from the volcano winds a road so twisty it has 620 curves and 59 little bridges packed into just one stretch — the famous Road to Hana. Drivers creep along slowly, and that's the whole point: around every curve hides another waterfall crashing into a pool, or a rainbow, or a grove of bamboo trees clacking in the wind.

And every winter, something even bigger arrives. Humpback whales swim thousands of miles from icy Alaska all the way to Maui's warm blue water, just to have their babies and sing haunting songs underwater. From the shore, you might spot one leaping clean out of the ocean — a splash you'll never forget.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The island is named after Maui, a legendary demigod said to have lassoed the sun to make days longer.
  • 2Haleakala means 'House of the Sun' — its summit sits over 3,000 meters high, and its crater looks so much like the Moon that NASA once tested lunar equipment there.
  • 3The Road to Hana packs 620 curves and 59 bridges into about 100 kilometers — some of the bridges fit only one car at a time!
  • 4Every winter, thousands of humpback whales migrate here from Alaska — swimming roughly 4,000 kilometers just to have their babies in Maui's warm water.
  • 5Humpback whales sing long, complex songs underwater that can travel for many kilometers through the ocean.
  • 6Some of Maui's beaches have jet-black sand, made from volcanic rock ground smooth by the crashing waves.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Haleakala Summit

Climb before dawn to this volcano's summit and watch the sunrise paint the sky pink and gold over a landscape so red and rocky it looks like another planet. Legend says this is exactly where the hero Maui lassoed the sun.

📍 Road to Hana

This famous winding road has 620 curves and 59 narrow bridges, and every turn hides something new — a crashing waterfall, a rainbow, or a quiet black-sand beach.

📍 Maalaea Bay

Warm waters here fill up every winter with humpback whales that traveled thousands of kilometers from Alaska. Watch closely from the shore and you might see one leap clean out of the water.

📍 Waianapanapa State Park

This beach glitters with jet-black volcanic sand, framed by lava-rock cliffs and turquoise waves. Legend says a hidden cave nearby was once a hiding place from a hunting hawk.
