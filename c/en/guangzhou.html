🎢 Guangzhou

The 'City of Flowers' where five rams once landed—now home to pandas and dim sum

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, five immortals rode down from the southern sky on five rams. Each ram carried a stalk of rice in its mouth, and the immortals blessed the land with rice forever, then vanished into thin air. Strangely enough, the five rams turned to stone right on the spot. That's why this city is still nicknamed the 'City of Five Rams' today.

That city is Guangzhou. It's also known as 'Canton,' and it's the birthplace of world-famous dim sum and Cantonese cooking. Choosing little steamed dumplings and buns from bamboo baskets, just by pointing at what you want, started right here.

Today, just outside the city, there's a huge safari called Chimelong Safari Park. Ride through in a car and lions and giraffes wander right up close, while snow-white tigers and adorable giant pandas wait to be met. It's also a treasured home where baby pandas are born and lovingly cared for.

Rising tall in the middle of the city is Canton Tower, nicknamed the 'Slender Waist' for its curvy, pinched-in shape. Near the very top, the world's highest and fastest outdoor Ferris wheel spins slowly around. At night, boats drift along the Pearl River, and the glowing city lights shimmer right back at you from the water.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Yuexiu Park (Five-Ram Statue)

A park with a big statue honoring the legend of the five rams that turned to stone. People here believe the rams still bring the city everlasting good fortune.

📍 Chimelong Safari Park & Paradise

Drive through and lions, giraffes, and snow-white tigers come right up close. Right next door is Chimelong Paradise, a huge amusement park full of roller coasters—so you can meet the pandas, then go have a blast.

📍 Canton Tower

About 600 meters tall, nicknamed 'Slender Waist' for its curvy shape. Ride the Ferris wheel near the top and look straight down at the Pearl River and the whole city below.

📍 Chen Clan Academy

A beautiful building over a hundred years old, covered in detailed carvings and ceramic decorations on every roof and pillar. It's fun to hunt for the dragons and flowers carved into the wood, stone, and pottery.
