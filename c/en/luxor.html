🏺 Luxor

Ancient Egypt's capital, where a boy king's gold lay hidden for 3,000 years

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, on the banks of the Nile River in Egypt, stood a magnificent city called Thebes — the place we now call Luxor. For over a thousand years it was the beating heart of ancient Egypt, home to powerful pharaohs, golden palaces, and towering temples built to honor the gods.

The Egyptians believed that after a pharaoh died, his spirit lived on forever, so they filled him a tomb with treasures for the afterlife. To keep robbers away, they hid these tombs deep in a rocky valley on the far side of the river, carving secret doors into the cliffs.

Almost every hidden tomb was found and robbed over thousands of years — except one. In 1922, an explorer named Howard Carter dug and dug until he found a sealed door leading to the tomb of a boy king named Tutankhamun, its treasures untouched for more than 3,000 years, including a dazzling solid-gold mask.

Today you can still walk among Luxor's giant stone columns and statues, exactly where pharaohs once walked. Some visitors even float over the temples at sunrise in a hot air balloon, watching the whole ancient city glow gold beneath them.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Karnak Temple Complex

The largest temple complex ever built. A 'forest' of 134 massive stone columns, covered in carvings, still stands tall. Pharaohs kept adding to it for 2,000 years, making it grow bigger and bigger.

📍 Valley of the Kings

A hidden desert valley where pharaohs were secretly buried in tombs cut deep into the rock. This is exactly where Tutankhamun's tomb was found still sealed after 3,000 years.

📍 Luxor Temple

Once connected to Karnak by the Avenue of Sphinxes, this temple glows gold under lights at night. Giant statues of Ramesses II still stand guard at its entrance.

📍 Temple of Hatshepsut

A stunning temple built into the cliffs for Egypt's most powerful female pharaoh. Its rows of columns look like a giant staircase carved right out of the mountain.
