🇵🇪 Peru

🌆 Lima

Peru's desert capital, where an ancient pyramid hides among skyscrapers and fountains dance every night

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, in 1535, a Spanish explorer named Francisco Pizarro looked at a stretch of desert coast in Peru and decided to build a brand-new city there. He named it the 'City of Kings,' because he founded it on the day Christians celebrate the visit of the Three Kings. That city grew into what we call Lima today, and it became one of the most important cities in all the Americas.

Here's something strange: Lima almost never gets real rain. It sits in one of the driest deserts on Earth, right next to the Pacific Ocean. Instead of rain, a soft grey mist called 'garúa' rolls in from the sea and blankets the whole city, keeping everything a little damp without ever really pouring.

But Lima's story doesn't start with Pizarro. It starts a thousand years earlier. Long before the Incas or the Spanish arrived, a people now called the Lima culture built a giant mud-brick pyramid right where the city stands, stacking tiny adobe bricks like books on a shelf so it could bend and survive Peru's earthquakes. Amazingly, that ancient pyramid, called Huaca Pucllana, still stands today, surrounded by modern skyscrapers!

And under one of Lima's oldest churches lies an even spookier secret: tunnels full of bones, neatly arranged by monks centuries ago. Above ground, though, the city dances every night with the world's biggest fountain show, glowing in every color you can imagine. From ancient pyramids to sparkling water, Lima is a city where every layer tells a different story.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Plaza Mayor de Lima

This grand square is where Pizarro founded Lima back in 1535, and it's still the heart of the city today. Around it stand the golden-balconied Government Palace and Lima's cathedral, both built where the story began.

📍 Huaca Pucllana

A giant mud-brick pyramid built by the Lima culture some 1,500 years ago, long before the Incas ever arrived. It still stands tall today, surrounded by the busy modern neighborhood of Miraflores, like a secret hiding in plain sight.

📍 Catacombs of San Francisco Monastery

Underneath this beautiful old monastery, tunnels wind through the dark, filled with the bones of about 25,000 people. Monks arranged some of the bones in neat circles and patterns, spooky, but fascinating too!

📍 Circuito Mágico del Agua (Magic Water Circuit)

At night, this park bursts into color with the world's largest fountain complex, holding a Guinness World Record. Water dances, lights swirl, and kids run laughing right through the spray.
