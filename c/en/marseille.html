⛵ Marseille

France's oldest city, started by one wine cup handed to a stranger

📖 The Story

Long, long ago—about 2,600 years back—Greek sailors from a city called Phocaea crossed the whole Mediterranean Sea in wooden boats and dropped anchor in a beautiful natural harbor in the south of France. They named their new home Massalia. It grew into the oldest city in all of France—older than Paris by hundreds of years!

People still tell a wonderful legend about that very first day. A local princess named Gyptis was choosing a husband at a feast, and she was supposed to pick one man by handing him a cup of wine. Instead, she walked right past all the local princes and handed the cup to Protis, the captain of the Greek ships! Her father was so pleased, he gave the sailors land to build their new city.

Massalia grew into busy Marseille, a port city where ships from all over the world came to trade. Just offshore, sailors built a stone fortress called Château d'If to guard the harbor. Later, a writer turned it into the most famous prison in books—Château d'If is where the hero of The Count of Monte Cristo made his daring escape!

Today Marseille is full of wonders. Its Old Port still bustles with fishing boats bringing in fish for bouillabaisse, a golden statue of the Virgin Mary watches over the city from a hilltop church nicknamed 'La Bonne Mère,' and just outside town hide the Calanques—towering white cliffs with water so blue and clear it looks like a swimming pool made by giants.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Marseille was founded around 600 BC by Greek sailors from Phocaea, making it the oldest city in France.
  • 2Its founding legend says a princess named Gyptis picked Greek captain Protis as her husband by handing him a wine cup at a feast—and Marseille was born as a wedding gift of land.
  • 3The island fortress Château d'If became famous for the dramatic escape scene in the novel The Count of Monte Cristo.
  • 4The Calanques near Marseille are steep white cliffs with turquoise water squeezed into narrow inlets—found almost nowhere else in Europe.
  • 5Traditional Savon de Marseille soap has been made the same way, from olive oil, since at least the 1600s.
  • 6Bouillabaisse, Marseille's famous fish stew, was invented by fishermen who tossed their unsellable leftover fish into one big, delicious pot.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Old Port (Vieux-Port)

The harbor where it all began 2,600 years ago. Fishing boats still dock here every morning, and a real fish market still fills the air with the smell of the sea.

📍 Notre-Dame de la Garde

A golden statue of Mary tops this hilltop church, watching over every ship that comes and goes. Climb up and you can see the whole city, the sea, and even the Calanques in the distance.

📍 Château d'If

This small stone fort on an island once guarded the harbor and later became a prison. It's most famous for one story: the hero of The Count of Monte Cristo breaks out of it!

📍 Calanques National Park

Hike between cliffs of white rock until you reach hidden coves of blue-green water. Boats, kayaks, and swimmers all come here for what feels like a secret paradise.
