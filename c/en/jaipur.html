🏰 Jaipur

The Pink City built like a giant puzzle—painted one color just to say welcome

📖 The Story

Long ago, in the desert lands of northwestern India, there lived a young king named Sawai Jai Singh II. He loved learning more than almost anything—especially mathematics and the stars. One day he decided to do something almost nobody had done before: build a brand-new capital city from scratch. That's how Jaipur was born, in the year 1727.

Jaipur wasn't built any old way. The king and his clever architect laid it out like a giant puzzle board, with neat square blocks and wide streets crossing each other in perfect lines. It became one of India's first fully planned cities—and somehow, the whole thing was finished in just about four years!

Here's a twist nobody expects: Jaipur wasn't always pink! In 1876, the city painted every building in the old town a warm, welcoming pink to greet a visiting British prince. Everyone loved how it looked so much that a law was made—buildings in the old city must stay pink forever. That's exactly why people now call it 'the Pink City.'

And the wow keeps going. At Hawa Mahal, hundreds of tiny honeycomb windows let royal princesses peek at busy street parades without ever being seen. Up on a hilltop, Amber Fort hides a room covered in thousands of tiny mirrors that twinkle like a night sky when you light just one candle. Today, this whole pink puzzle-city is even a UNESCO World Heritage Site!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Hawa Mahal (Palace of Winds)

A pink, honeycomb-shaped palace wall with 953 tiny windows. Royal women once sat behind these windows to watch street parades below—seeing everything while staying completely unseen.

📍 Amber Fort

A grand hilltop fort with a dazzling secret inside: the Sheesh Mahal, a room covered in thousands of tiny mirror pieces. Light just one candle in there and the whole ceiling sparkles like a sky full of stars.

📍 Jantar Mantar Observatory

A garden full of giant stone instruments built to study the sky. One of them is the world's largest stone sundial—so accurate it can tell time down to about two seconds.

📍 Jal Mahal (Water Palace)

A palace that seems to float right in the middle of a lake. Only its top floor peeks above the water—the other floors stay hidden and flooded on purpose, like a magic trick made of stone.
