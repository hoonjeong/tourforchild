🚲 Amsterdam

The canal city built on wooden poles, where houses lean on purpose and bikes outnumber people

📖 The Story

Long ago, a small group of fishermen lived where the Amstel River flows into a big bay. To keep the water from flooding their homes, they built a dam right across the river. People started calling the village 'Amstelredamme'—dam on the Amstel—and over time that name became Amsterdam.

By the 1600s, Amsterdam had grown into one of the richest cities on Earth. Ships sailed out to faraway lands and came back loaded with spices, silk, and treasures, and merchants grew so wealthy that they started the world's very first stock exchange. With all that gold pouring in, the city dug three grand curved canals that wrap around its center like a rainbow.

But there was a problem: the ground under Amsterdam is soft, squishy swamp, not solid rock. So builders drove thousands of long wooden poles deep into the mud before laying a single brick. Houses were even taxed by how wide they were, so people built them tall and narrow with steep, twisty stairs—and put a hook at the top of the roof to hoist furniture up through the window instead.

Today, those same canals still sparkle in the sunshine, houseboats bob gently along the water, and bicycles outnumber people on every street. Amsterdam is still the watery, wonky, wonderful city that grew out of one small dam.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Amsterdam's name literally comes from 'Amstel + dam'—a dam built across the Amstel River.
  • 2The Royal Palace on Dam Square stands on 13,659 wooden poles hammered deep into the swampy ground.
  • 3Houses were once taxed by width, so people built them tall and narrow—some lean forward on purpose so furniture wouldn't scrape the front!
  • 4Many houses have a hook at the top of the roof, used to hoist furniture up through the window because the staircases are too steep and narrow.
  • 5Amsterdam has roughly as many bicycles as people—on some streets you'll see more bikes zoom by than cars or pedestrians.
  • 6The city's canal ring has over 100 kilometers of canals and more than 1,500 bridges—that's more bridges than Venice!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Dam Square & the Royal Palace

This is the very spot where the first dam was built on the Amstel River—the birthplace of the whole city. The grand palace standing here today floats on 13,659 wooden poles buried in the mud beneath it.

📍 Anne Frank House

Behind a hidden bookcase in this canal house, a girl named Anne Frank and her family hid from danger during World War II and kept a secret diary. Her words later touched readers all around the world.

📍 Herengracht — the "Golden Bend"

This curving stretch of Amsterdam's grandest canal is lined with the tallest, fanciest houses in the whole city, built by wealthy merchants during the Golden Age. Look up at the rooftops and you can still spot the old furniture-hoisting hooks!

📍 Houseboat Museum

Step inside a real houseboat floating on the canal and see how a whole family can live, sleep, and cook right on the water. Amsterdam has thousands of houseboats bobbing along its canals today!
