🦁 Singapore

The tiny 'Lion City' island that grew into one of the world's most dazzling cities

📖 The Story

Long ago, a prince named Sang Nila Utama sailed across the sea and landed on a small island covered in jungle. As his boat touched the shore, he spotted a strange animal with an orange body and a black head bounding through the trees. He'd never seen anything like it, so he decided it must be a lion — and named the island "Singapura," which means "Lion City."

Centuries passed, and that jungle island grew into something new. In 1819, a British trader named Sir Stamford Raffles arrived and realized this tiny spot sat exactly where trade ships between India and China had to pass. Almost overnight, a sleepy fishing village turned into one of the busiest ports on Earth, filled with sailors, spices, and silk from every direction.

When Singapore became its own independent country in 1965, it had almost nothing — no oil, no farmland, barely any space at all. But its people had a bold idea: build UP instead of OUT, and turn the whole city into a giant garden. They planted millions of trees, grew forests on rooftops, and even dreamed up trees made of steel that could light up the night sky.

Today that tiny island is one of the most dazzling cities on the planet. A stone lion with a fish's tail — the Merlion — still spouts water over the harbor where Sang Nila Utama once landed. And even the airport doesn't feel like an airport: it has its own indoor waterfall thundering down from the roof, tall enough to make you gasp before you've even left the terminal!

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The Merlion statue's lion head honors the founding legend, and its fish body honors the island's start as a humble fishing village.
  • 2The name "Singapore" comes from Sanskrit words for "lion" (simha) and "city" (pura) — even though real lions never actually lived on the island!
  • 3Jewel Changi Airport has the world's tallest indoor waterfall, called the Rain Vortex, crashing 40 meters down right inside the airport building.
  • 4The Supertrees at Gardens by the Bay are as tall as 16-story buildings — some even collect rainwater and make solar power, just like real trees.
  • 5The rooftop pool at Marina Bay Sands stretches across three towers, 57 floors up — one of the largest infinity pools on Earth!
  • 6Singapore's hawker centers, its open-air food courts, were added to UNESCO's list of humanity's cultural treasures — official proof that street food can be a national treasure.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Merlion Park

This is where the legendary lion statue stands, spouting water into the very harbor where Sang Nila Utama's boat is said to have landed. Half lion, half fish, the Merlion tells Singapore's whole story in one statue.

📍 Gardens by the Bay (Supertree Grove)

Giant tree-like towers called Supertrees rise here, some as tall as 16-story buildings. At night they light up in rainbow colors, and every evening they put on a dazzling light-and-music show.

📍 Marina Bay Sands SkyPark

Three towers hold up a boat-shaped platform way up on the 57th floor, home to one of the world's largest rooftop pools. From up there you can see the whole glittering bay laid out below you.

📍 Jewel Changi Airport (Rain Vortex)

Right inside the airport, water thunders down a 40-meter indoor waterfall called the Rain Vortex, surrounded by an indoor rainforest. Even before you leave the terminal, Singapore already feels like magic.
