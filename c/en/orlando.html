🎢 Orlando

The swampy land that grew into the theme-park capital of the world

📖 The Story

Long ago, in the middle of Florida, there was a quiet town built beside sparkling lakes and swampy woods. People say the town got its name from a soldier named Orlando Reeves, who once stood watch here to protect settlers—though nobody's completely sure that's how it happened, it became a story everyone loved to tell. For nearly a hundred years, orange groves and lakes were about all this town was known for.

Then, in the 1960s, a filmmaker named Walt Disney had a giant secret. He wanted to build a brand-new kind of magic park, bigger than anything before. So he quietly sent people to buy up mile after mile of swampy land around Orlando, using made-up company names so nobody would guess who was buying and raise the prices!

There was just one problem: this land was mostly wet, buggy swamp, not exactly perfect for building castles. So Disney's engineers built a hidden system of canals and pumps under the ground to drain the water away, keeping the whole park dry even in Florida's heaviest rains. They even built Cinderella Castle so its bricks get smaller near the top—a trick called forced perspective that makes the castle look far taller than it really is!

Since the park opened in 1971, Orlando has grown into the place with more theme parks than anywhere else on Earth—roller coasters, talking robots, real alligators sunning by lakes, and secret tunnels running beneath it all. And it all started with lakes, swamps, and one person's very big, very secret dream.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Cinderella Castle (Magic Kingdom)

The castle rising at the very center of Magic Kingdom. Its bricks shrink as they go up—a trick called forced perspective that makes the real 189-foot castle look even taller. Hidden underneath run secret tunnels called the utilidors.

📍 Spaceship Earth (EPCOT)

A giant silver ball that's the symbol of EPCOT. Ride inside and travel through the story of how people went from discovering fire all the way to reaching outer space.

📍 Lake Eola Park

A sparkling lake right in downtown Orlando. You can pedal around in giant swan-shaped boats, and at night the fountain lights up in glowing colors.

📍 Gatorland

Nicknamed the 'Alligator Capital of the World,' where you can see real wild alligators up close. Long before Disney ever arrived, alligators were the true owners of this swampy land.
