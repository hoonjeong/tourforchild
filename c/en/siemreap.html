📖 The Story

Long ago, in the heart of the Cambodian jungle, a mighty empire called the Khmer built a city so grand it seemed to touch the sky. Their king, Suryavarman II, wanted to build the greatest temple the world had ever seen — a home for the Hindu god Vishnu. Thousands of workers hauled giant sandstone blocks, some as heavy as a truck, for decades. What they built was Angkor Wat — still, to this day, the largest religious monument on Earth.

Angkor Wat was just one part of an enormous city called Angkor. Around a thousand years ago, up to a million people lived there — more than in London at the time! Wide roads, giant reservoirs, and hospitals connected the city, and armies rode into battle on elephants, just like in the carvings still on the temple walls today.

But empires don't last forever. Wars and changing rivers made people leave, and slowly the jungle crept back in. Enormous trees dropped their roots right onto the temples, wrapping around towers like giant fingers. For centuries, the temples slept quietly under leaves and vines, almost forgotten by the outside world.

Today you can wake up before dawn and watch the sun rise behind Angkor Wat's five towers, painting the sky pink and gold. You can find giant stone faces smiling down at Angkor Thom, and squeeze between tree roots at Ta Prohm, just like an explorer discovering a lost city for the first time.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Angkor Wat is the largest religious monument in the world — its moat alone is wider than five soccer fields end to end.
  • 2Hindu temples usually face east, but Angkor Wat was unusually built facing west — some say it's because it was later used as a tomb too.
  • 3Some sandstone blocks used to build Angkor Wat weigh as much as a small elephant, and were floated down rivers on rafts.
  • 4Angkor Thom's Bayon temple has more than 200 giant stone faces, all smiling the exact same mysterious smile.
  • 5At Ta Prohm, silk-cotton and strangler fig trees have grown so big their roots swallow entire temple walls.
  • 6Angkor Wat appears on Cambodia's national flag — the only national flag in the world with a building on it.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Angkor Wat

The heart of the Angkor ruins and the largest religious monument in the world. At dawn, the sun rises behind its five towers and turns the sky and reflecting pool gold. Its walls are covered in carvings telling stories from Hindu mythology.

📍 Bayon Temple (Angkor Thom)

The central temple of Angkor Thom, where more than 200 giant stone faces smile out in every direction. Wherever you stand, it feels like one of those mysterious smiles is looking right at you.

📍 Ta Prohm

A temple where enormous tree roots wrap right around the walls and rooftops. It looks like the jungle is swallowing the temple whole, making you feel like you've stepped into a lost temple from an adventure movie.

📍 Tonle Sap Floating Village

A whole village floating on Southeast Asia's biggest lake. The lake grows and shrinks by many times its size with the seasons, and the houses float right along with the rising and falling water.
