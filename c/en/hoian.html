🏮 Hoi An

The old trading port where time stopped — now lit by lanterns, not lights

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, a small river town in Vietnam called Hoi An became one of the busiest harbors in all of Asia. Ships from Japan, China, India, and even faraway Holland sailed up the Thu Bon River, their decks piled with silk, spices, and blue-and-white porcelain. For hundreds of years, sailors from many countries walked the same narrow streets, trading goods and swapping stories in a dozen different languages.

The merchants who came to stay built their own little neighborhoods side by side. The Japanese traders built a beautiful covered bridge with a tiny temple tucked inside it, guarded by stone animals at each end. Chinese merchants built grand meeting halls with dragon-carved roofs. Somehow, all these different styles blended together into one town that felt like nowhere else on Earth.

Then, slowly, the river began to fill with sand, and the big trading ships could no longer squeeze through. One by one, the merchants sailed away, and Hoi An grew quiet. But because nothing much happened there for a couple hundred years, nobody knocked down the old wooden houses to build something new — the whole town simply dozed off, perfectly preserved, like a town caught mid-yawn.

Today, Hoi An has woken back up — but its ancient houses, bridge, and halls are all still standing right where they were. Once a month, on the night of the full moon, the town switches off its electric lights, and thousands of paper lanterns glow instead — red, yellow, and gold, reflecting in the river. Walk through Hoi An at night and you can almost hear the old sailors' footsteps again.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Japanese Covered Bridge

Built about 400 years ago by Hoi An's Japanese merchants, this graceful covered bridge has a tiny temple hidden inside and stone dogs and monkeys standing guard at either end. It's the symbol of the whole town — it even appears on Vietnam's 20,000-dong banknote!

📍 Tan Ky Old House

This 200-plus-year-old merchant house blends Vietnamese, Chinese, and Japanese design all in one building. Look closely at the walls near the courtyard — faint lines mark exactly how high floodwater has risen in past storms!

📍 Fujian Assembly Hall

Chinese merchants from Fujian built this grand hall to honor Thien Hau, the goddess said to protect sailors at sea. Its gate and roof are covered in swirling dragons, and giant coiled incense spirals hang from the ceiling, burning slowly for days.

📍 Thu Bon River Lantern Boats

At night, little wooden boats glide along the river, each one loaded with paper lanterns for sale. Buy one, light its candle, and set it afloat — locals believe watching your lantern drift into the dark carries your wish along with it.
