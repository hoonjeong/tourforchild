🛕 Bangkok

The city with the world's longest name, built on golden temples and shimmering canals

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, there was a great Thai kingdom called Ayutthaya, but after it was destroyed in a war, the Thai people needed a brand-new capital. In 1782, King Rama I chose a spot on the east bank of the Chao Phraya River and began building a shining new city. He named it Krung Thep — and gave it a much longer name too, to bless it with good fortune.

That full name is so long, it holds a world record! It has 168 letters and is the longest place name on Earth, according to the Guinness World Records. Most Thai people just use a short nickname for it — and the rest of the world calls it Bangkok.

Bangkok grew up along water. Boats zipped down canals called khlongs, carrying rice, fruit, and flowers to floating markets, so people started calling it the 'Venice of the East.' In the middle of it all, the king built a dazzling palace with golden roofs and a tiny green Buddha statue so precious it gets its clothes changed by the king himself, three times a year!

Right across the river, a temple called Wat Arun sparkles at sunrise, covered in millions of tiny broken pieces of colorful porcelain. Not far away lies a giant gold Buddha, so long he has to lie down just to fit inside his own temple! Even today, Bangkok mixes these 200-year-old wonders with sky trains and skyscrapers, so old and new sit side by side.

📍 Places to visit

📍 The Grand Palace & Wat Phra Kaew

This dazzling palace of golden spires was home to Thai kings for 150 years. Inside its walls sits the tiny, precious Emerald Buddha, dressed by the king himself in different robes for each season.

📍 Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn)

Rising beside the river, this temple glitters at sunrise thanks to millions of colorful porcelain pieces pressed into its spires. Climb its steep steps and you'll feel like you're standing inside a giant work of art.

📍 Wat Pho (Temple of the Reclining Buddha)

Home to a giant reclining Buddha, so big he barely fits inside his own hall. This temple is also known as the birthplace of traditional Thai massage, taught here for centuries.

📍 Chao Phraya River

This wide, busy river is Bangkok's original highway. Long-tail boats zoom past floating markets and canal-side houses, showing you why old Bangkok was called the 'Venice of the East.'
