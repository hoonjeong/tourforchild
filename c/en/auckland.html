⛵ Auckland

The city built on 53 sleeping volcanoes, between two sparkling harbours

📖 The Story

Picture a city built on top of not one, not two, but around fifty sleeping volcanoes! That's Auckland, the biggest city in New Zealand, sitting right between two sparkling harbours. Māori called this place Tāmaki Makaurau, which means 'the place desired by many,' because the land was so rich and the fishing so good that everyone wanted to live there.

One of those volcanoes is so young that people actually watched it being born! About 600 years ago, the sea near Auckland suddenly started to boil and hiss, and a brand-new island called Rangitoto rose right out of the water, spitting fire and rock. Māori families who lived nearby ran for their lives—and we know this because their footprints were found perfectly preserved in the ash on the island right next door!

Today Auckland is famous as the 'City of Sails,' because more people here own a boat than almost anywhere else on Earth. On a sunny weekend, the harbour fills up with hundreds of white sails gliding along, while downtown, the giant Sky Tower pokes up into the clouds—built right on the same restless ground as all those ancient volcanoes.

The best part? Auckland's volcanoes aren't scary at all anymore—they're grassy green hills you can climb in minutes! Stand on top of one, and you can see the whole city, the harbour bridge, and little Rangitoto floating on the sea, and imagine the fiery night it was born.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Sky Tower

This 328-meter tower shoots up from downtown Auckland like a giant needle, and it was the tallest tower in the whole Southern Hemisphere when it opened. Brave visitors can even walk on a glass floor near the top—or jump off on a wire attached to a cable!

📍 Rangitoto Island

This perfectly round island rose straight out of the sea about 600 years ago, and it's still the youngest and largest volcano in the whole Auckland field. You can hike up to its crater in under an hour and look right down into the very spot that once spat fire.

📍 Mount Eden (Maungawhau)

The tallest of Auckland's volcanic hills, with a huge grassy crater you can walk right down into. Long ago, Māori built a fortified village called a pā here, and you can still see the ring-shaped terraces carved into its slopes.

📍 One Tree Hill (Maungakiekie)

One of the largest and most important pā sites Māori ever built, with terraces still rippling down its sides. Climb to the top for a view over the whole city and both of Auckland's harbours at once.
