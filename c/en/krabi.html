🏝️ Krabi

Krabi: where the ocean floor turned into giant stone towers

📖 The Story

Long, long ago—many millions of years before people existed—the land we now call Krabi was hiding under the sea. Coral reefs grew thick and busy, and countless tiny sea creatures lived and died there, leaving their shells behind on the seafloor.

Over millions and millions of years, those shells and coral piled up and turned into solid limestone rock. Then the earth's crust slowly pushed and squeezed, and that ancient seabed rose right out of the ocean—twisting into the dramatic cliffs and towers that stick straight up from Krabi's turquoise water today.

For hundreds of years, sea nomads called the Chao Leh sailed these waters in wooden boats, reading the tides and stars better than anyone. Fishermen found secret lagoons hidden inside ring-shaped cliffs, and monks climbed a jungle mountain to build a temple where a giant golden Buddha now watches over the land.

Today, you can only reach Krabi's famous Railay Beach by boat, because sheer cliffs wall off any road. Kayak through a narrow gap in the rock and a hidden turquoise lagoon opens up like a secret room. Krabi isn't just a beach—it's proof that the sea and the earth can team up to build something wonderfully strange.

  • 1Krabi's pointy stone towers are limestone made from coral and seashells piled up tens of millions of years ago, then pushed up from the seafloor.
  • 2Railay Beach has cliffs on every side and no road at all—the only way in is by boat.
  • 3Hong Island hides a lagoon inside a ring of cliffs. You can only reach it by kayaking through a narrow rock gap—'Hong' means 'room' in Thai.
  • 4The Emerald Pool is a blue-green pond hidden in the middle of the jungle, kept sparkling by warm mineral spring water bubbling up from below.

  • 6Sea nomads called the Chao Leh have lived on boats and in stilt villages for generations, and they're amazing at holding their breath and swimming.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Railay Beach

A beach ringed by sheer limestone cliffs with no road in or out. Rock climbers travel from all over the world to scale these walls. The only way in is by boat.

📍 Hong Island

A hidden emerald lagoon sits inside a ring of cliffs. Paddle a kayak through a narrow rock gap to reach it, and it feels like slipping into a secret room.

📍 Emerald Pool (Sa Morakot)

A glowing blue-green pool hidden deep in the jungle. Warm mineral spring water bubbles up from below, keeping the water sparkling all year round.

📍 Tiger Cave Temple (Wat Tham Suea)

Climb 1,237 steps to reach a giant golden Buddha at the top. Inside the cave below, there's a rock said to bear the print of a tiger's paw.
