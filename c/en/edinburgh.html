🏰 Edinburgh

The fairytale city built on top of two sleeping volcanoes

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, a fiery volcano stood right where Edinburgh now sits. Millions of years passed, and wind, rain and ice wore the mountain down—but its hard volcanic core stayed standing tall as a steep, rocky crag. About a thousand years ago, people looked at that crag and thought: what a perfect spot for a castle no enemy could ever climb! So Edinburgh Castle was built right on top of a volcano that had long gone quiet.

As the city grew up below the castle, people packed into narrow stone alleys called 'closes,' stacking houses up to ten storeys high—skyscrapers of their day! When the city needed more room, workers simply built new streets right over the old ones, sealing a whole little neighborhood of tiny shops and homes into the ground beneath. You can still walk down those hidden underground streets today, frozen exactly as they were hundreds of years ago.

Not far from the castle lived a little dog named Bobby. When his owner died, Bobby refused to leave—he sat by the grave in Greyfriars churchyard every single day, through rain, wind and snow, for fourteen long years. The whole city fell in love with the loyal little dog, and his statue still stands watch nearby today.

Climb the hill called Arthur's Seat—yes, another old volcano!—and the whole city spreads out below like a fairytale map: the castle on its rock, the winding Royal Mile, and rooftops stretching all the way to the sea. Once a year every August, Edinburgh turns into a giant stage for the world's biggest arts festival, with jugglers, musicians and storytellers filling every street corner. No wonder people call Edinburgh a city built on legends.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Edinburgh Castle stands on 'Castle Rock,' the plug of a volcano that erupted about 350 million years ago—the rock is way, way older than the castle!
  • 2The big green hill beside the city, Arthur's Seat, is actually another long-dead volcano. You can hike right up to the top in about an hour.
  • 3Hidden beneath the Royal Mile is a whole underground street called Mary King's Close, sealed up when the city built new streets right over it more than 250 years ago.
  • 4A loyal little dog named Bobby guarded his owner's grave in Greyfriars Kirkyard for fourteen years. Visitors still rub his statue's nose for good luck today.
  • 5Every day except Sunday, a cannon called the One O'Clock Gun booms from the castle at exactly 1pm—it once helped ships in the harbor set their clocks.
  • 6Every August, Edinburgh hosts the Fringe, the largest arts festival on Earth, when the whole city fills with street performers, comedians and shows.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Edinburgh Castle

A castle standing tall on a long-dead volcanic rock. Kings and queens lived here for centuries, and it still fires a cannon every day at 1pm.

📍 Arthur's Seat

Another ancient volcano rising right in the middle of the city. Hike to the top and the whole of Edinburgh spreads out below you.

📍 Mary King's Close

An old alleyway buried beneath a newer street. Head down the narrow stairs and you'll find life just as it was hundreds of years ago.

📍 Greyfriars Kirkyard

The graveyard where the loyal dog Bobby watched over his owner's grave for fourteen years. A small statue honoring Bobby stands near the entrance.
