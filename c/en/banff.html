🏔️ Banff

The mountain valley where a hot-spring cave became Canada's very first national park

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, giant rivers of ice called glaciers carved through the Rocky Mountains, shaping jagged peaks and brilliant blue lakes. For thousands of years, Stoney Nakoda and other Indigenous peoples traveled and hunted through these valleys, calling the land home. Then in 1883, railway workers blasting a path through the mountains stumbled on something strange — a small hole in the rock, puffing out warm steam.

The workers climbed down a wobbly ladder and found a hidden cave with warm water bubbling straight out of the earth! Word spread fast, and soon a few people were arguing over who should own this amazing spot. So the government made a wonderful decision instead: the hot spring and the mountains around it would belong to everyone. In 1885, Canada's very first national park was born.

But Banff had more magic tucked away nearby. A little way up the valley sat two lakes so clear and blue-green they looked almost unreal — Lake Louise and Moraine Lake. Their dazzling color comes from 'rock flour,' powder ground from stone by glaciers, floating in the water and bouncing back the sunlight. Anyone who saw them for the first time could hardly believe their eyes.

Today Banff is still a small, busy town with wild magic all around it. Elk with huge antlers sometimes stroll right down Main Street, and at night the sky can shimmer green with the northern lights. From the top of the Sulphur Mountain gondola, or beside the grand, castle-like Banff Springs Hotel, you can look out over the very same peaks that greeted glaciers, explorers, and hot-spring hunters long ago.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Banff became Canada's first national park in 1885 — after just three railway workers found a hot-spring cave, it became one of the very first national parks in the whole world.
  • 2The turquoise color of Lake Louise and Moraine Lake comes from 'rock flour,' superfine rock powder ground up by glaciers that floats in the water.
  • 3Moraine Lake and its row of 'Ten Peaks' were once so famous they appeared on the back of Canada's $20 bill.
  • 4Banff sits inside a national park bigger than some entire countries, so it's still common to spot elk, bighorn sheep, or even a black bear near town.
  • 5The Banff Springs Hotel looks like a castle because its builders wanted travelers to feel like royalty — one even said, 'If we can't export the scenery, we'll import the tourists.'
  • 6On clear winter nights, the aurora borealis sometimes dances green and pink above Banff's mountains.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Lake Louise

A brilliant blue lake fed by melting glacier water, with a shining white glacier behind it. Its color comes from tiny rock powder floating in the water. Paddle a canoe across it and it feels like floating inside a painting.

📍 Moraine Lake

A lake ringed by ten jagged peaks like a stone curtain. It was once so beautiful it appeared on Canada's $20 bill, earning it the nickname 'the most photographed spot in Canada.'

📍 Cave and Basin National Historic Site

The very cave where railway workers first discovered the hot spring in 1883. The story that began here is the reason Banff became Canada's first national park.

📍 Banff Springs Hotel

A giant stone hotel that looks just like a castle. Built more than 100 years ago by the railway company to draw in travelers, it still stands tall, surrounded by forest and mountains.
