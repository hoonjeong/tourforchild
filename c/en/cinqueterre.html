🌈 Cinque Terre

Five rainbow villages clinging to the cliffs of Italy's coast

📖 The Story

On the wild green coast of Liguria in northwestern Italy, five tiny villages perch on cliffs above a sparkling blue sea. Together they're called Cinque Terre — Italian for 'Five Lands.' Their houses are painted every color you can imagine: pink, yellow, orange, blue — stacked on top of each other like a box of crayons tipped over a cliff.

Long, long ago, there was barely any flat ground here at all — just steep, rocky mountains tumbling straight into the sea. So people carved thousands of tiny stone-walled terraces up the slopes, like giant staircases, to plant grapevines where nothing should really grow. It took hundreds of years of patient stacking, one stone at a time.

For centuries, the five villages had no road connecting them to each other or the outside world. The only ways in or out were by boat, on foot along cliffside trails, or — much later — through train tunnels blasted straight through the mountains. One trail hugging the cliffs between two villages became so famous for couples strolling hand in hand that people started calling it the 'Path of Love.'

Today, Cinque Terre is protected as a national park and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, precisely because no road or concrete ever flattened this place. Hop a hiking trail, a tiny train, or a boat, and in one single day you can visit all five candy-colored villages — each one perched on its own cliff, guarding its own little harbor, exactly as it has for a thousand years.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Cinque Terre means 'Five Lands' — for its five villages: Monterosso, Vernazza, Corniglia, Manarola, and Riomaggiore.
  • 2One village, Corniglia, isn't even next to the sea — it sits on a hilltop, and to reach it you must climb 382 zigzag steps called the Lardarina.
  • 3There are almost no cars in the villages — many streets are just staircases, too narrow and steep for anything with wheels.
  • 4The steep terraced vineyards produce a sweet golden wine called Sciacchetrà, made from grapes dried in the sun before pressing.
  • 5A famous cliffside trail between Manarola and Riomaggiore is nicknamed the 'Via dell'Amore' — the 'Path of Love.'
  • 6Cinque Terre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and Italy's smallest national park, because its villages and terraces have stayed almost unchanged for centuries.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Vernazza

A village with a tiny harbor and an old castle. Long ago, boats hid in this cozy inlet to escape pirate ships. Today it's called one of the prettiest harbors in the world.

📍 Manarola

The most photographed of the five villages. Colorful tower-like houses stack up on the rocks, and there's a natural rock pool where you can jump right into the sea.

📍 Riomaggiore

The southernmost of the five villages. Colorful houses stack along a narrow valley, and it's where the famous 'Path of Love' trail begins.

📍 Corniglia

The only one of the five villages set back from the sea, perched on a clifftop and ringed by vineyards. Reaching it means climbing 382 steps — which makes it feel extra special.
