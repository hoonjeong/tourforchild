💦 Niagara Falls

The thundering falls where swimming-pool-loads of water crash down every single second

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, the land where Canada and the United States now sit was buried under thick sheets of ice—giant glaciers. About 12,000 years ago, that ice slowly melted, and a huge rush of water carved a new path called the Niagara River. When the river reached a spot where the ground suddenly dropped away, the water had nowhere to go but down, and it began crashing over the edge with a roar.

Here's a secret: Niagara Falls isn't just one waterfall—it's three! The biggest, curved like a horseshoe, is Horseshoe Falls on the Canadian side. The American Falls and the thin, veil-like Bridal Veil Falls sit on the U.S. side. Together, these three falls dump enough water every single second to fill several Olympic swimming pools.

Long ago, Indigenous peoples told a legend that a girl was sent over the falls in a canoe to calm the spirit of thunder. That's why, since 1846, the boats that carry visitors right up close to the roaring water have been called the 'Maid of the Mist.' Ride it today, and the spray will still soak you before you even reach the falls!

All that crashing water slowly wears away the soft rock below, so the falls are actually inching backward, bit by bit, year after year. About a hundred years ago, an inventor named Nikola Tesla figured out how to turn that mighty rush of water into electricity. Niagara's power began lighting up whole cities—nature's own strength switching on the future overnight!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Horseshoe Falls

Curved like a horseshoe, this is the biggest and mightiest of the three falls. Nearly 90 percent of all the water rushes over it here, and watching that emerald-green wall of water thunder down will leave you speechless.

📍 Maid of the Mist Boat Tour

Since 1846, this boat has been sailing visitors right up to the base of the falls. Everyone gets a blue rain poncho, because you'll feel the spray on your whole body. Its name comes from an Indigenous legend about a canoe girl.

📍 Journey Behind the Falls

An elevator takes you down into tunnels carved into the cliff, right behind the crashing water. Standing there with a wall of white water pounding just in front of you, you'll finally understand why this place sounds like thunder.

📍 Table Rock

A viewing platform right beside Horseshoe Falls, close enough that the falling water feels almost within reach. Your heart will race watching it crash so near—and on sunny days, a rainbow often appears in the mist to double the magic.
