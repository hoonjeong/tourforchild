🇩🇪 Germany

🥨 Munich

Founded by monks, crowned by kings, and home to a wave you can surf in the city center

📖 The Story

Long ago, before Munich became a big city, a group of monks built a small settlement on a hill by a river crossing, where an old trading road carrying salt crossed the water. People started calling the place 'bei den Mönchen'—'by the monks'—and over time that became the city's name, München. As the settlement grew, a market square opened up in the middle, and that spot is still the heart of the city today: Marienplatz.

Centuries later, a powerful family called the Wittelsbachs made Munich their royal capital, and they wanted it to look magnificent. They built grand palaces and started a giant twin-towered church called Frauenkirche. Legend says the devil once snuck inside to see it, and where he stood stamping his foot in anger after realizing he'd been tricked, a strange footprint-shaped mark can still be found on the floor.

In 1810, the crown prince married a princess, and the whole city was invited outside the gates to celebrate with horse races and a huge party. Everyone had so much fun that they decided to do it again every single year—and that party grew and grew until it became Oktoberfest, the biggest folk festival on Earth, packed with giant pretzels and oceans of beer.

Munich still has surprises hiding around every corner. At the New Town Hall, a giant clock called the Glockenspiel comes alive every day, its little figures reenacting a knights' tournament from hundreds of years ago. And in the middle of a huge park called the English Garden, there's something you'd never expect in a city center—a river with a wave that never stops, where surfers ride it all year round, rain, shine, or snow.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Munich's name comes from an old German phrase meaning 'by the monks'—monks really did help found the city.
  • 2Oktoberfest began in 1810 as a royal wedding party and is now the world's largest folk festival, with about 6 million visitors every year.
  • 3The Glockenspiel clock in Marienplatz has 43 bells and 32 life-size figures that act out a 500-year-old story every day.
  • 4Legend says the devil left an angry footprint on the floor of Frauenkirche church after realizing he'd been tricked.
  • 5In the middle of the city, surfers ride a never-stopping wave on the Eisbach river in the English Garden—even in winter!
  • 6The English Garden is one of the world's biggest city parks—even bigger than Central Park in New York.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Marienplatz & the Glockenspiel (New Town Hall)

The beating heart of Munich and its oldest square. Every day at set times, figures on the New Town Hall tower spin around, acting out a knights' tournament and a coopers' dance from hundreds of years ago.

📍 Frauenkirche (Cathedral of Our Lady)

Its two onion-domed towers can be seen from almost anywhere in Munich, making it the city's symbol. Step inside and you can hunt for the strange footprint mark said to be left by the devil.

📍 Nymphenburg Palace

The grand summer palace of the Wittelsbach royal family. 'Mad' King Ludwig II was born here, and the canals in its huge gardens turn into an ice-skating rink in winter.

📍 English Garden (Eisbach Wave)

A giant park bigger than New York's Central Park. The Eisbach river flowing through it has a wave that breaks in the exact same spot forever, so surfers ride it right in the middle of the city.
