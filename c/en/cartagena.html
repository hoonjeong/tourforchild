🌺 Cartagena

The walled Caribbean city that pirates could never crack

📖 The Story

Long ago, on the Caribbean coast of what is now Colombia, Spanish sailors built a harbor town called Cartagena. Ships loaded with gold and silver from all over South America gathered here before crossing the ocean to Spain. That made Cartagena one of the richest, most tempting ports in the whole New World.

Word of all that treasure spread fast—and so did pirates. Fierce sea captains sailed in again and again, hoping to snatch the gold for themselves. One famous raider, Francis Drake, even managed to capture the city for a while and only left after collecting a giant ransom!

So the people of Cartagena decided: never again. They built a colossal stone wall that wrapped around the whole city, and up on a hill they raised an enormous fortress called San Felipe de Barajas, laced with tunnels that zigzagged like a maze. The tunnels were built so cleverly that guards inside could hear enemy footsteps echoing from far away, while staying safely hidden themselves.

The plan worked. Years later, a British admiral arrived with one of the biggest fleets ever assembled—around 180 ships and tens of thousands of soldiers—determined to take Cartagena. But the walls and the fortress held strong, and the invaders were sent home defeated. Today you can still walk along those same walls at sunset, wander streets bursting with flowers and color, and imagine the pirates, treasure, and brave defenders who made Cartagena a legend.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Castillo San Felipe de Barajas

This massive fortress was built to protect Cartagena from pirates and enemy fleets. Wander through its maze-like tunnels and you'll find spots engineered so defenders could hear voices and footsteps from far outside—without ever being seen.

📍 The City Walls (Las Murallas)

These thick stone walls circle the old town and took generations to finish. Walk along the top at sunset, just as Cartagena's guards once did, watching for ships appearing on the horizon.

📍 Getsemaní

Once a humble neighborhood outside the walls, Getsemaní is now covered in rainbow murals, fluttering flags, and lively street music. It's proof Cartagena's colorful story is still being written today.

📍 Islas del Rosario (Rosario Islands)

A sprinkle of small coral islands scattered in the turquoise water off Cartagena. Treasure ships once sailed these very seas—today you can snorkel above coral reefs full of colorful fish.
