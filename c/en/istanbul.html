🕌 Istanbul

The only city in the world standing on two continents at once

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, on a strip of land where two seas meet, Greek sailors built a small city and named it Byzantium. It sat right where a narrow strait called the Bosphorus divides two continents—Europe on one side, Asia on the other. Ships from every direction had to pass through this strait, so the city grew rich and important almost overnight.

About 1,700 years ago, a Roman emperor named Constantine fell in love with this spot and made it his new capital, renaming it Constantinople. For centuries it was the crown jewel of a vast empire, and its rulers built an enormous domed church called Hagia Sophia—so huge that for nearly a thousand years, no building on Earth was bigger.

Then, in 1453, a young Ottoman sultan sailed his ships over land on greased logs to sneak up on the city's harbor, and after a long siege, Constantinople fell. The Ottomans renamed the city Istanbul, turned Hagia Sophia into a mosque by adding four tall minarets, and built a dazzling new home for their sultans called Topkapi Palace.

Today, Istanbul is still the only city in the world sitting on two continents at once—you can have breakfast in Europe and, after a short ferry ride across the Bosphorus, eat dinner in Asia! Thousands of friendly cats wander its streets and markets, and in the maze-like Grand Bazaar, merchants have been selling treasures the same way for over 500 years.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Hagia Sophia

This enormous domed building was first a church, then a mosque, and now welcomes visitors of every kind. Step inside and look up—the dome seems to float, almost like it has no support at all!

📍 Topkapi Palace

Home to Ottoman sultans for about 400 years, this palace has courtyards, kitchens that once fed thousands, and a treasury sparkling with jewels. Kids especially love spotting the huge emerald dagger and the famous 86-carat diamond.

📍 Grand Bazaar

One of the world's oldest shopping malls, with over 4,000 shops packed under covered stone arches. Merchants have been selling lamps, spices, and rugs here in the very same spot for more than 500 years.

📍 Galata Tower

Climb this tall stone tower and you can see both continents at once—Europe on one side of the Bosphorus, Asia shimmering on the other. Legend even says a man once strapped on wings and glided from its top all the way across the strait!
