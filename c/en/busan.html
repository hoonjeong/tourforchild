🌊 Busan

Korea's rainbow port city, built with hope by people who'd lost everything

📖 The Story

Busan is Korea's biggest port city, where tall mountains tumble right down into a sparkling blue sea. Ships from all over the world sail in and out of its harbor every day, and the smell of the sea drifts through every street. It's a city that has always looked outward, welcoming people from faraway places.

Long ago—well, not so very long ago—in the 1950s, a terrible war broke out across Korea, and almost the whole country was in danger. But Busan, tucked safely at the very southeastern tip of the peninsula, stayed out of harm's way. So for a while, Busan became Korea's temporary capital, and millions of people fled here to be safe, packing the city to bursting.

Many of those families had nowhere to live, so they built tiny houses up a steep hillside, painting them in every color they could find. Over the years that hillside turned into Gamcheon Culture Village—a rainbow-colored maze of houses, alleyways, and murals that looks like a village stacked out of Lego blocks.

Today Busan is bursting with life again—fishmongers shout out fresh catch at Jagalchi Market, families build sandcastles on Haeundae Beach, and movie stars walk the red carpet at one of Asia's biggest film festivals. A city built by people who never gave up now sparkles brighter than ever.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Jagalchi Market is Korea's biggest fish market—you can pick a wriggling live fish and eat it minutes later, freshly cooked right at the stall.
  • 2Gamcheon Culture Village is nicknamed 'Korea's Machu Picchu' because its rainbow houses climb the hillside in neat, stacked rows, just like the ancient ruins.
  • 3During the Korean War, Busan became the country's temporary capital for nearly three years, sheltering millions of people who had nowhere else to go.
  • 4Busan is home to the only United Nations cemetery in the world, where soldiers from 21 different countries who helped Korea are remembered together.
  • 5Every summer, Haeundae Beach hosts a sand festival where artists build giant sculptures taller than a house—all out of sand!
  • 6In Nampo-dong, movie stars press their hands into cement plaques on the sidewalk—it's Busan's own version of Hollywood's Walk of Fame.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Gamcheon Culture Village

A village war refugees built shoulder-to-shoulder on a steep hillside. Today every house is painted a different color, and the stairways are filled with art and sculptures.

📍 Jagalchi Market

Busan's biggest, oldest fish market. Weaving through the shouting merchants and wriggling fish, you get a taste of the real, living Busan.

📍 Haeundae Beach

Busan's wide, beautiful signature beach. In summer it fills with people and giant sand sculptures—proof the city is bursting with life once again.

📍 UN Memorial Cemetery

A quieter side of Busan's wartime story. Soldiers who came from faraway countries to help Korea rest here, in the only UN cemetery of its kind in the world.
