🏔️ Zermatt

A car-free village beneath the world's most famous pointy mountain

📖 The Story

High up in the Swiss Alps sits a tiny village called Zermatt, tucked beneath a mountain so perfectly pointy it looks like a giant stone pyramid. This mountain is the Matterhorn, and it's shaped so sharply that from every direction it looks like a different peak. In Zermatt, you won't hear a single car horn — no gas-powered cars are allowed in the village at all, just electric taxis, bikes, and horse-drawn sleighs gliding through snowy streets.

For hundreds of years, the Matterhorn scared away every climber who dared try it — its rock walls were too steep, too sharp, too dangerous. While climbers had already reached the top of nearly every other big peak in the Alps, the Matterhorn stood alone, unclimbed, like a puzzle nobody could solve. Then in 1865, a young Englishman named Edward Whymper and his team finally scrambled to the very top — becoming the first people ever to stand on its icy summit.

Today Zermatt has found gentler ways to reach the mountains. A cheerful red cogwheel train called the Gornergrat Railway has been climbing a nearby ridge since 1898, without a single tunnel, so riders can watch the Matterhorn grow bigger the whole way up. Cable cars carry visitors even higher, all the way to Matterhorn Glacier Paradise — at 3,883 meters, the highest cable car station in all of Europe, where you can find snow for skiing even in the middle of summer.

And here's something sweet: many people say the triangular shape of Toblerone chocolate was inspired by this very mountain. So the next time you unwrap a Toblerone bar, you might be holding a tiny, tasty copy of one of the most famous mountains on Earth.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Matterhorn

The sharp pyramid peak you can see from almost anywhere in Zermatt. Ever since its first ascent in 1865, it's been a dream mountain for climbers worldwide, and its almost-too-perfect triangle shape still amazes everyone who sees it.

📍 Gornergrat

A viewpoint you reach on the red cogwheel train that's been climbing since 1898. From the top, you can see the Matterhorn and even the second-largest glacier in the Alps spread out before you.

📍 Zermatt Village (Bahnhofstrasse)

In this car-free heart of the village, only electric taxis, bicycles, and (in winter) horse-drawn sleighs go by. Walking past the old wooden storehouses on stilts, you'll feel like time has slowed way down.

📍 Matterhorn Glacier Paradise

A cable car ride takes you up to 3,883 meters here — the highest cable car station in Europe. It's a magical spot where you can ski on bright white snow even in the middle of summer.
