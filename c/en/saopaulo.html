🏙️ Sao Paulo

The little hilltop school that grew into South America's biggest city

📖 The Story

Long ago, in 1554, a small group of priests climbed a hill in Brazil and built a simple school made of mud and straw. They named it after Saint Paul, because they finished it on his special day, January 25th. Back then it was just a tiny clearing in the forest, ringed by two rivers—nobody could have guessed what it would become.

Two hundred years later, coffee farmers discovered that the red soil nearby grew the most delicious coffee beans in the world. Ships carried sacks of these beans—people called them 'green gold'—across the ocean, and the money came pouring back. Trains were built, factories opened, and boats full of hopeful families arrived from Italy, Japan, Portugal, Lebanon and dozens of other countries, all looking for a new start.

The little hilltop town exploded into a giant city almost overnight. Skyscrapers shot up so fast and so close together that today so many people zip between them by helicopter that São Paulo has more helicopters buzzing over it than almost any other city on Earth! One museum downtown even looks like it's floating—its whole glass box hangs in the air, held up by just four giant red pillars.

Walk through São Paulo today and you can taste it all: crispy Italian-style pizza, delicate Japanese sushi in a neighborhood with its own red temple gate, and colorful street art splashed across whole alleys. And if you climb back up that very first hill, the tiny mission school is still standing—a quiet little reminder of how one small dream in the forest grew into the biggest city in South America.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Pátio do Colégio (Founding Site)

This is the very spot where priests built that first little school in 1554—the whole city grew up around it. A small chapel and museum stand here now, so you can stand exactly where São Paulo began.

📍 MASP – São Paulo Museum of Art

A giant red-and-glass box that seems to float in mid-air, held up by just four huge pillars with nothing underneath. Kids love running through the wide open space beneath it before going in to see the art.

📍 Liberdade (Japantown)

Walk under a huge red torii gate into streets lined with lanterns, ramen shops, and cherry blossom festivals. It's home to the largest Japanese community anywhere outside Japan.

📍 Beco do Batman (Batman's Alley)

A whole alley covered top to bottom in colorful street art, started decades ago after someone painted a batman. Artists still paint over the walls constantly, so it looks a little different every time you visit!
