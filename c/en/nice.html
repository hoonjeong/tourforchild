🇫🇷 France

🏖️ Nice

Named for Victory, with a beach made of pebbles — the sunniest city on the French Riviera

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, Greek sailors sailed along the sunny coast of southern France and founded a small port town. They had just won a battle, so they named it after Nike, the goddess of victory — and that's how the city got its name: Nice. Tucked between blue mountains and an even bluer sea, people still call it 'Nice the Beautiful.'

Here's something surprising: Nice's beach isn't covered in soft sand at all. It's covered in smooth, round pebbles called galets, polished by thousands of years of rolling waves. When the sea pulls back, the stones clatter and clink together — the sound of Nice's very own beach music.

About 200 years ago, wealthy visitors from England began spending their winters here, escaping the cold, gray skies back home. They loved strolling by the sparkling Baie des Anges, the Bay of Angels, so much that they helped pay for a long walkway right along the water. Today that walkway, the Promenade des Anglais — the 'Walkway of the English' — stretches for miles, and people still stroll it exactly the way those visitors once did.

Nice has one more surprise: it hasn't always been part of France! For a long time it belonged to a kingdom across the border, until 1860, when the people of Nice voted to join France instead. Every winter since, the city has thrown one of the world's biggest carnivals, with giant flower-covered floats and a joyful 'Battle of Flowers,' where dancers toss thousands of real blossoms into the cheering crowd.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Promenade des Anglais

This sunny seaside walkway stretches for miles along the Bay of Angels. Wealthy English visitors helped build it 200 years ago so they could stroll by the sea all winter long — and people still do exactly that today.

📍 Colline du Château (Castle Hill)

A tall green hill where a real castle once stood, until it was torn down hundreds of years ago. Climb up today and you'll find leafy gardens, a splashing waterfall, and the best view over all of Nice's rooftops and the sea.

📍 Cours Saleya Market

The heart of Nice's old town, piled every morning with mountains of colorful flowers, fruit, and spices. Grab a warm slice of socca here — a crispy chickpea pancake that's been a Nice specialty for generations.

📍 Musée Matisse

A museum inside a beautiful old villa, filled with paintings and cut-paper artworks by Henri Matisse, who fell in love with Nice's sunlight and lived here for years. It sits in a peaceful olive grove above the city.
