🛕 Ayutthaya

The golden city of a hundred temples that rivers, elephants, and a tree once swallowed whole

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, in the heart of Thailand, three rivers wrapped around a magnificent island city called Ayutthaya. Ships from Portugal, Japan, Persia, and China sailed right up to its docks to trade silk, spices, and gems. The city was so grand and so watery that visitors called it the 'Venice of the East.'

For 417 years, Ayutthaya was the glittering capital of the Kingdom of Siam. Golden temple spires poked into the sky, kings rode royal elephants through the streets, and at its richest, more people lived here than in London or Paris. It was one of the biggest, busiest cities on the whole planet.

But in 1767, an enemy army swept in and set the whole city on fire. Palaces crumbled, golden roofs melted, and many stone Buddha statues even lost their heads. Ayutthaya, once so proud and shining, was left empty and quiet, slowly disappearing under jungle vines.

Yet something magical happened while the city slept. At one old temple, a giant Bodhi tree grew right around a fallen stone Buddha head, wrapping it snugly in its roots like a gift. Today you can walk through Ayutthaya's red-brick ruins and towers, and meet that tree-hugged face yourself — proof that even a 'sleeping' city can still whisper its story.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Ayutthaya was the capital of Siam for 417 years (1350–1767) — longer than many countries have even existed!
  • 2At its peak, over a million people lived here, making it one of the largest cities on Earth — bigger than London or Paris at the time.
  • 3Traders from Japan, Persia, Portugal, the Netherlands, and China all had their own neighborhoods in the city, making it wonderfully multicultural.
  • 4Built on an island hugged by three rivers, Ayutthaya was nicknamed the 'Venice of the East' — people traveled around by boat.
  • 5At Wat Mahathat, a stone Buddha head is completely wrapped in the roots of a Bodhi tree that grew around it over hundreds of years.
  • 6Many Buddha statues in Ayutthaya lost their heads long ago — some knocked off by invaders hunting for hidden gold, some later taken by treasure hunters — so rows of calm, headless Buddhas still sit in the ruins today.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Wat Mahathat

Home to Ayutthaya's most famous sight: a stone Buddha head completely hugged by the roots of an old Bodhi tree. Nobody planted it there on purpose — the tree just grew around the fallen head over centuries, wrapping it like a natural picture frame.

📍 Wat Phra Si Sanphet

The grandest royal temple in the old palace grounds, with three towering bell-shaped chedis in a row. Each one holds the ashes of an ancient Siamese king, and it was once so lavish it had a Buddha statue covered in real gold.

📍 Wat Chaiwatthanaram

A stunning riverside temple built to look like the famous Angkor temples in Cambodia. At sunset, its tall central tower and surrounding spires glow gold and orange over the water — one of the most photographed views in all of Thailand.

📍 Wat Yai Chai Mongkol

A giant chedi you can climb for a view over the fields, plus a huge, peaceful Reclining Buddha statue draped in orange robes. It was built to celebrate an ancient Siamese king's famous victory in a duel fought riding elephants.
