🌲 Vancouver

Where snow-capped mountains meet the ocean, and thousand-year-old trees stand beside skyscrapers

📖 The Story

Long before this place was called Vancouver, Coast Salish peoples — the Musqueam, Squamish, and Tsleil-Waututh — had lived along this coastline for thousands of years. They paddled cedar canoes between forested islands and carved tall totem poles that told their families' stories. The forest here was enormous — trees so wide that three kids holding hands couldn't wrap around them, and so tall they seemed to touch the clouds.

In 1792, a British explorer named Captain George Vancouver sailed into this harbor and mapped the coastline. Decades later, a small town grew up nearby and took his name: Vancouver. But this brand-new city almost didn't survive its first year — in 1886, a fire swept through and burned nearly the whole town down in under an hour! Yet people rebuilt so fast that within a year, shops, streets, and houses stood again.

As Vancouver grew into a big city, something wonderful happened. Instead of cutting down all the old forest, people decided to keep a giant chunk of it right in the middle of downtown. That's Stanley Park — bigger than New York's Central Park. Thousand-year-old trees still stand tall there, totem poles rise up from the grass, and a path curls all the way around the park along the sea.

Today, Vancouver is famous for an almost magical view: snow-capped mountains rise right behind the skyscrapers, so on the very same day you could ski down a slope in the morning and walk along a beach in the afternoon. Orcas swim through the nearby waters, eagles soar overhead, and a wobbly bridge sways high above a forest canyon just outside downtown. In Vancouver, wild nature and the city have never really said goodbye to each other.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Stanley Park

A giant park almost completely surrounded by the sea, right beside downtown's skyscrapers. Walk beneath thousand-year-old trees and you'll come across a cluster of colorful totem poles carved by First Nations artists.

📍 Capilano Suspension Bridge

A wobbly bridge stretching 137 meters across a deep forest canyon, swaying 70 meters above a rushing river. Cross it if you dare, then walk a treetop bridge among towering evergreens.

📍 Granville Island Public Market

Once a noisy factory island, now a lively public market packed with fresh fish, sweet treats, and street performers. Colorful fishing boats bob in the water just outside, and seaplanes buzz overhead.

📍 Grouse Mountain

Ride a gondola almost straight up the mountain right behind the city, and in minutes you're above the clouds, looking down at Vancouver and the ocean. In winter you can ski here in the morning and be on a beach by afternoon.
