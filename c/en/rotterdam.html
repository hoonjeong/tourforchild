🚢 Rotterdam

The city bombs destroyed - and dreamers rebuilt like nowhere else on Earth

📖 The Story

Long ago, Rotterdam began as a tiny fishing village where villagers built a dam across a little river called the Rotte to keep the water out. That's actually where the name comes from - "Rotter-dam"! Ships started sailing in and out along the wide river beside it, and slowly the sleepy village grew into one of the busiest harbor cities in the whole world.

But one terrible afternoon in May 1940, everything changed. During World War II, bombs fell on Rotterdam, and in just a few hours, almost the entire old town center - its centuries-old streets and buildings - was gone. It was one of the saddest days the city ever knew.

Yet Rotterdammers didn't just rebuild the old city the way it used to look. They rolled up their sleeves and asked a bold question: what if we build something the world has never seen before? Architects dreamed up wobbly yellow houses tilted like they're about to tip over, and a giant rainbow-shaped market hall painted inside with a ceiling mural bursting with giant fruit and flowers.

Today Rotterdam looks like no other Dutch city - so full of daring, futuristic buildings that people nickname it "Manhattan on the Maas." Ships from every corner of the planet still glide through its harbor, and a swan-shaped bridge glows over the river at night. Ride to the top of the Euromast tower, and you can see this brave, rebuilt city spreading out beneath you like a giant Lego town brought to life.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Rotterdam's name literally means "dam on the Rotte river" - the whole city is named after a tiny stream!
  • 2The tilted yellow Cube Houses were designed to look like a forest of trees, each one twisted 45 degrees like it's about to fall over (but it never does).
  • 3Inside the Markthal, a giant ceiling painting called "Horn of Plenty" covers 11,000 square meters with fruits, flowers, and bugs - it's one of the largest artworks in the Netherlands.
  • 4The swooping white Erasmus Bridge is nicknamed "The Swan," and one of its tilted towers stands taller than a 40-story building.
  • 5Long before the Mayflower, in 1620, a group called the Pilgrims set sail from Rotterdam's old harbor of Delfshaven on their very first ship before heading on to America.
  • 6At the top of the needle-shaped Euromast tower, a spinning glass capsule called the Euroscoop slowly corkscrews up the outside for an even higher view.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Cube Houses (Kubuswoningen)

A whole neighborhood of bright yellow houses tilted at a wild angle, each one balanced on a single narrow pillar. They were designed to look like an abstract forest - and yes, real families live inside them today!

📍 Markthal (Market Hall)

Step inside this giant rainbow-shaped hall and look up - a ceiling mural bursting with 10-meter-tall fruits and flowers stretches above rows of cheese, fish, and flower stalls. It's like shopping inside a painting!

📍 Erasmus Bridge (The Swan)

This graceful white bridge swoops across the river Maas, held up by cables from one tall, curved tower - which is exactly why everyone calls it "The Swan." At night it lights up and glows over the water.

📍 Delfshaven (Historic Harbor)

One of the only corners of Rotterdam that survived the bombing, with cozy old brick houses and a windmill lining a quiet canal. It's also the very spot where the Pilgrims boarded a ship bound for America back in 1620.
