🏦 Zurich

A sparkling lake city where a kneeling horse and Roman roots became Switzerland's chocolate capital

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, Roman soldiers marched north and found a hill beside a deep blue lake, with a river rushing past. They built a small customs post there and called it Turicum. Over hundreds of years, that tiny outpost grew into a city we now call Zürich.

One old legend says the emperor Charlemagne once went hunting on that same hill. Suddenly his horse stopped and knelt down, right on top of two hidden graves—the graves of two saints, Felix and Regula. Charlemagne was so amazed that he ordered a great church built on the spot, and today its two tall towers still rise above the rooftops.

Wander through Zürich's Old Town today and you'll find narrow cobblestone lanes, colorful guild houses, and church bells that echo across the water. The lake and river are so clean that on hot summer days, people jump right in for a swim—swans glide by, and more than a thousand public fountains bubble with fresh drinking water, free for anyone to sip.

Zürich mixes old magic with new marvels: chocolate makers here dreamed up some of the world's favorite sweets, and a young scientist named Albert Einstein once studied in its university halls. Every spring, the whole city gathers to burn a giant snowman stuffed with firecrackers—and legend says the faster his head bangs, the sunnier summer will be!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Grossmünster

This church's twin towers mark the spot where, legend says, Charlemagne's horse knelt on two saints' graves. Climb one tower's 187 steps for a sweeping view over the whole city and lake.

📍 Fraumünster

Step inside to see five glowing stained-glass windows designed by the artist Marc Chagall. Sunlight through the glass paints the floor in rainbow colors.

📍 Lindenhof Hill

This grassy hilltop was once a Roman fort watching over the river below. Today it's a quiet park with a giant chessboard and the best view of Zürich's Old Town rooftops.

📍 Lake Zürich

Swans glide across water so clean that people swim right downtown in summer. Boats chug along the shore, with snow-capped Alps peeking out in the distance on clear days.
