🗼 Pisa

The world's most famous 'mistake'—leaning strong for over 800 years

📖 The Story

Long ago, the people of Pisa, a city in Italy, decided to build a grand bell tower right beside their beautiful cathedral. In 1173, they began stacking white marble, ring by ring, story by story. But there was one secret nobody knew: the ground beneath the tower was soft and squishy, made of sand and clay.

By the time they'd built just three stories, the tower started to slowly tip to one side! The soft ground on that side had sunk lower than the rest. Everyone was alarmed—but then a war broke out, and builders had to stop for almost 100 years. That long pause actually saved the tower. While work was paused, the soft ground slowly settled, so the tower stopped sinking so fast.

When the war ended, builders came back with a clever trick: they made the upper floors on the leaning side a little taller, so the tower curved back the other way—like a banana! By 1372, almost 200 years after the very first stone was laid, the tower was finally finished.

Instead of tearing down their crooked tower, the people of Pisa kept it standing just as it was—and it became one of the most beloved buildings on Earth. Legend says Galileo, a scientist born right there in Pisa, dropped balls from the top to study gravity. In the 1990s, engineers gently straightened the tower just a bit, so it can keep leaning safely for hundreds of years to come.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The tower took almost 200 years to build (1173–1372) because a war paused construction for nearly 100 years.
  • 2Built on soft sandy, clay-like ground, the tower started leaning after just three stories were finished.
  • 3Look closely and the tower actually curves like a banana—builders made the upper floors taller on the leaning side to compensate.
  • 4There are 7 bells at the top, one for each note of the musical scale, do-re-mi-fa-sol-la-ti.
  • 5Legend says Galileo, born right in Pisa, dropped balls from the tower to study gravity.
  • 6In the 1990s, engineers carefully removed some soil under the tower to reduce the lean—so it should stay safe for hundreds more years.

📍 Places to visit

📍 The Leaning Tower of Pisa

The star of this story! An 8-story white marble bell tower that still leans about 4 degrees today. Climb the spiral staircase inside all the way to the top.

📍 Pisa Cathedral (Duomo)

The grand cathedral the leaning tower was built to serve as a bell tower for. Its white-and-gray marble stripes are gorgeous—and if you look closely, even the cathedral leans just a little!

📍 Baptistery of St. John

A huge round-domed building, one of the largest baptisteries in Italy. Make a sound inside and the echo rings on and on—it's an amazing acoustic trick!

📍 Camposanto Monumentale

A long, quiet cloister at the edge of the Field of Miracles, its walls covered in centuries-old frescoes. Legend says its soil was shipped all the way from the Holy Land in Jerusalem.
