🐠 Okinawa

The island kingdom of red castles, turquoise seas, and the longest-living people on Earth

📖 The Story

Long ago, before Okinawa was part of Japan, it was its own kingdom called Ryukyu, with its own king, its own language, and a beautiful castle painted the color of a dragon-king's palace. That castle, Shuri, still stands today, glowing bright vermillion against the sky.

The Ryukyu Kingdom was a tiny nation with a giant job: it was the ocean's great go-between. Ships loaded with silk, spices, and treasures sailed back and forth between China, Korea, Japan, and lands across Southeast Asia, and Ryukyu traded with them all, growing rich and wise from four hundred years of friendship on the waves.

Step onto any beach here and the sea turns an impossible turquoise blue, hiding one of the largest coral reefs on Earth. Gentle giant whale sharks—the biggest fish in the world—glide through these waters, and some even swim inside a tank so huge at the Churaumi Aquarium that it feels like diving into the ocean itself.

Maybe that's why Okinawa is famous for something else amazing: its people live longer, healthier lives than almost anywhere else on the planet. Grandparents dance the Eisa drum dance at summer festivals, lion-dog Shisa statues grin from every rooftop, and the whole island seems to hum with sunshine, ocean, and old, old stories.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Shisa are lion-shaped guardian statues. They usually stand in pairs — one with its mouth open to welcome good luck in, and one with its mouth shut to keep bad luck out.
  • 2The world's biggest fish, whale sharks, swim in the Churaumi Aquarium's 'Kuroshio Sea' tank — one of the largest aquarium tanks anywhere on Earth.
  • 3The Ryukyu Kingdom lasted about 450 years, with its own flag and language, trading all the way from China to Southeast Asia — before it later became part of Japan.
  • 4Okinawa is one of the world's famous 'Blue Zones,' places where people live unusually long and healthy lives.
  • 5A cliff called Cape Manzamo was carved by waves into the shape of an elephant's trunk. Its name even means 'a meadow big enough for ten thousand people to sit.'
  • 6Shuri Castle is painted bright vermillion red and blends Chinese and Japanese building styles. It has burned down and been rebuilt many times — even after a fire in 2019, Okinawans are lovingly rebuilding it again.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Shuri Castle

The vivid red palace of the Ryukyu kings, blending Chinese and Japanese style into something all its own. Fire has burned it down more than once — even in 2019 — but each time, the people of Okinawa lovingly rebuild it, brick by painted brick.

📍 Churaumi Aquarium

Home to the giant Kuroshio Sea tank, so huge that whale sharks and manta rays glide right past the glass. Stand in front of it and you'll feel like you've stepped straight into the deep blue ocean.

📍 Cape Manzamo

A dramatic cliff where waves have carved the rock into the shape of an elephant's trunk dipping into the sea. Its name means 'a meadow big enough for ten thousand people to sit' — and the view is easily that grand.

📍 Kabira Bay

One of the most beautiful bays in Japan, where the water shifts between a dozen shades of blue and green over the coral below. Glass-bottom boats let you peer down at the reef without even getting wet.
