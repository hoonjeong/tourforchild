🎄 Strasbourg

The fairy-tale border city where storks, a giant clock, and Christmas magic all call home

📖 The Story

On the border between France and Germany, where a wide river called the Rhine flows, sits a colorful city called Strasbourg. Its name means something like 'city of the roads,' because for hundreds of years, travelers and traders pulled their carts through here on their way to somewhere else. Walk down its cobblestone streets today and you'll see rows of houses painted pink, yellow and blue, with wooden beams crisscrossing their fronts like a giant tic-tac-toe board. And if you look up, you might spot a huge nest on a chimney — with a long-legged stork standing guard.

Rising above all those rooftops is an enormous pink-stone cathedral with one single spire pointing straight into the sky. For more than two hundred years, it was the tallest building anywhere in the world! Inside, a magical clock as tall as a house comes alive every day at half past noon: painted apostles parade past a wooden rooster, and the rooster flaps its wings and crows, just as it has for centuries.

Storks have nested on Strasbourg's roofs for so long that people believe they bring good luck — and, in old stories, even bring new babies to families! But storks once nearly disappeared from the city forever. Hunters and hard winters wiped most of them out, until kind Strasbourgeois built safe nests and raised young storks by hand, coaxing the birds to come home again.

Every winter since the year 1570, Strasbourg has thrown one of the world's oldest Christmas markets, filling the whole city with twinkling lights and a giant Christmas tree that people call the 'Capital of Christmas.' And down by the canals, in a pretty old neighborhood called La Petite France, boats glide past crooked houses that used to belong to tanners and millers hundreds of years ago. Cross one more bridge, and you'll find the glass towers of the European Parliament — a hopeful sign that France and Germany, who once fought over this very city, are now the best of neighbors.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Strasbourg Cathedral

This pink sandstone cathedral had just one spire built, yet it soared higher than any other building on Earth for over two centuries. Step inside at 12:30pm to watch the astronomical clock's parade of apostles and its famous crowing rooster.

📍 La Petite France

Wander this fairy-tale district where colorful half-timbered houses lean over sparkling canals. Tanners and fishermen once lived and worked here — today, boats and swans glide by instead.

📍 Place Kléber (Christmas Market Square)

Every December, this main square glows with one of the world's oldest Christmas markets and a giant, glittering tree. It's the beating heart of the city Strasbourg proudly calls the 'Capital of Christmas.'

📍 Barrage Vauban

This old stone dam once protected the city from attackers by flooding the fields around it. Climb to its rooftop terrace for one of the best views of Strasbourg's spires and canals.
