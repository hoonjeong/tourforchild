🏖️ Punta Cana

Miles of powder-white beach where ancient caves, hidden lagoons, and dolphins share the coast

📖 The Story

Long before any resort stood here, the Taino people lived along this coast, fishing these waters and calling their island 'Quisqueya' — mother of all lands. Deep in hidden caves nearby, they carved pictures into the stone walls: suns, faces, and creatures that still look down on visitors today, thousands of years later.

For a long time, this stretch of coastline stayed wild and quiet — just coconut trees, white sand, and turquoise water rolling in for miles and miles, with hardly anyone around to see it. It wasn't until about 50 years ago that people rediscovered how beautiful this coast truly was, and little by little, it grew into one of the most famous beach destinations in the world.

But the real magic hides inland, in the jungle. There, twelve freshwater lagoons sit tucked among the trees, so clear and round that people named them 'Ojos Indigenas' — the Indigenous Eyes — because from above, they really do look like a pair of eyes blinking up through the green.

And just offshore, the ocean holds its own wonders: pods of wild dolphins leaping through the waves, and a short boat ride away, an island called Saona with a sandbar so shallow you can stand in the sea and watch starfish resting on the sand beneath your feet.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Ojos Indigenas Ecological Park

Hike through the jungle to find these twelve round freshwater lagoons — so clear you can see straight to the bottom, and shaped like eyes when viewed from above.

📍 Los Haitises National Park

Deep in mangrove-lined caves here, Taino people carved suns, faces, and creatures into the stone thousands of years ago — drawings you can still see today by boat.

📍 Saona Island

A short boat ride away, this island has a sandbar so shallow and calm you can wade out among wild starfish resting on the seabed.

📍 Bavaro Beach

One of the most beautiful stretches of Punta Cana's coast, protected by a coral reef just offshore that keeps the water calm and clear — perfect for spotting colorful fish and, sometimes, wild dolphins passing by.
