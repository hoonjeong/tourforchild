🏖️ Miami

The pastel sun-city a single box of orange blossoms helped build

📖 The Story

Long before skyscrapers, the Tequesta people fished along rivers and mangrove swamps where Miami now stands. Warm sea breezes swept the coast, and green mangrove forests hugged the shoreline. Back then, nobody could have dreamed a sparkling city of towers would ever rise here.

In the winter of 1895, a brutal freeze swept across Florida and killed off orange groves everywhere—except in one spot: the land around a woman named Julia Tuttle. Julia packed a box of fresh, unfrozen orange blossoms and sent it to railroad tycoon Henry Flagler with a simple message: nothing here froze at all! Amazed by the blossoms, Flagler extended his railway all the way down to Miami, and in 1896, Miami officially became a city.

Once the train arrived, people poured in. Along the beach, pastel pink, mint, and sky-blue Art Deco buildings rose in colorful rows, while Cuban immigrants filled a neighborhood called Little Havana with lively music and the smell of strong coffee. A tiny swampy outpost had transformed almost overnight into a dazzling, international city.

But Miami's real magic hides right next door. The Everglades, a one-of-a-kind river of grass, flows right beside the city—and it's the only place on Earth where alligators and crocodiles live side by side. Gentle sea cows called manatees glide through the city's canals and bay, making Miami a truly special place where skyscrapers and wild creatures live as neighbors.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Miami is one of the few major U.S. cities credited to a woman founder—Julia Tuttle herself!
  • 2A single box of unfrozen orange blossoms convinced a railroad to come to town—flowers basically built the city.
  • 3The Everglades right next door is the only place on Earth where alligators and crocodiles live side by side.
  • 4Gentle sea creatures called manatees swim through Miami's canals and bay—big, but famously calm and slow.
  • 5Miami Beach has the world's largest collection of Art Deco buildings, painted in rows of pink, mint, and sky blue.
  • 6In Little Havana, you can watch grandpas gather to play dominoes on the street—Cuban culture is alive all around.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Vizcaya Museum and Gardens

A grand, century-old mansion with Italian-style gardens by the bay. Stand on its stone terrace facing the sea and feel just how beautiful this coast has always been.

📍 Ocean Drive Art Deco District

A street lined with pastel pink, mint, and sky-blue buildings. It holds the world's largest gathering of Art Deco buildings, a style that was all the rage in the 1930s.

📍 Everglades National Park

A vast wetland known as the 'river of grass.' It's the only place on Earth where alligators and crocodiles share the same waters, plus a home for manatees and pink flamingos.

📍 Freedom Tower

The building that welcomed Cuban immigrants arriving in the 1960s. Along with Little Havana, it tells the story of how Miami came to embrace so many cultures.
