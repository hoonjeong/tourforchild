🎓 Boston

The city where America's fight for freedom began with tea in the harbor

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, before the United States even existed, Boston was a busy port town ruled by faraway Britain. Ships came and went loaded with tea, cloth, and sugar. But British kings kept adding taxes on things people bought—even tea—and the people of Boston grew angrier every year. They had a saying: no taxation without representation, meaning they wouldn't pay taxes if they had no voice in the rules.

One cold December night in 1773, a group of Bostonians dressed up like Mohawk warriors and quietly rowed out to three tea ships docked in the harbor. By lantern light, they hauled up chest after chest of tea, smashed them open with hatchets, and dumped the leaves straight into the water. By morning, the harbor was practically a giant, floating cup of tea—and Britain was furious.

That bold 'tea party' helped spark a war. A couple of years later, a silversmith named Paul Revere galloped through the dark on his horse, warning towns that British soldiers were coming. He and his friends hung two lanterns in a church tower to signal whether the troops were arriving by sea or by land. Those events led straight into the American Revolution.

Boston never forgot that daring night. Today you can walk the Freedom Trail, a bright red-brick line through the city, and follow the very steps of the rebels, the lantern-lit church, and the harbor where the tea went splash. You can climb aboard old ships, ring a ship's bell, and toss a pretend tea chest into the water yourself!

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1On the night of the Tea Party, Bostonians dumped 342 chests of tea — about 92,000 pounds — into the harbor.
  • 2They dressed up as Mohawk warriors to hide who they were, but honestly, most neighbors could still guess.
  • 3Paul Revere's 'midnight ride' used a lantern signal — 'one if by land, two if by sea' — hung high in Old North Church's steeple.
  • 4USS Constitution, nicknamed 'Old Ironsides,' is the world's oldest commissioned warship still afloat — and it still sails every year!
  • 5Boston Common, laid out in 1634, is the oldest public park in the United States. Long ago, people even grazed their cows there!
  • 6Fenway Park opened in 1912 and is the oldest major-league ballpark still in use, famous for its towering green wall, the 'Green Monster.'

📍 Places to visit

📍 Boston Common

America's oldest public park, laid out way back in 1634. Long ago, townsfolk grazed cattle and gathered here; today, families stroll and paddle swan boats right where colonists once did.

📍 Old North Church

The very steeple where two lanterns once flashed Paul Revere's signal. Look up at the tower and imagine those tiny lights glowing in the dark that night.

📍 Boston Tea Party Ships & Museum

Replica ships float right where the real tea went overboard. Climb aboard and toss a pretend tea chest into the harbor yourself!

📍 USS Constitution

The world's oldest floating commissioned warship, nicknamed 'Old Ironsides.' Its wooden hull was so tough that cannonballs were said to just bounce off!
