🇯🇵 Japan

⛩️ Kyoto

Japan's capital for over 1,000 years—where old Japan is still alive

📖 The Story

Long ago, more than 1,200 years back, Japan's emperor decided to build a brand-new capital city. He called it Heian-kyo—'the capital of peace and tranquility'—and today we know it as Kyoto. Streets were laid out in a neat grid, copied from a great Chinese city, like a giant checkerboard. Walk through Kyoto today and you can still feel those crisp straight streets running north-south and east-west.

Kyoto stayed Japan's capital for over 1,000 years! Emperors and nobles lived there, and elegant arts bloomed—poetry, painting, tea ceremonies, and gorgeous gardens. Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion, was built in this era too, covered in real gold leaf. Its reflection shimmers on the pond below like a palace straight out of a fairy tale.

Then, in the middle of the 1900s, a huge war broke out, and Kyoto nearly got bombed. But a foreign official who treasured Kyoto's beauty and history helped get it taken off the bombing list. Thanks to him, Kyoto's old temples and streets are still standing exactly as they were, almost untouched.

Visit Kyoto today, and you'll find Fushimi Inari Shrine, where thousands of bright red gates form tunnels winding up a mountain. There's Arashiyama's bamboo grove too, so thick and tall it blocks out the sky. In the streets, geiko (geisha) and maiko still glide by in beautiful kimono. Kyoto is a city where old Japan is still alive and breathing.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Fushimi Inari Shrine

Thousands of bright red torii gates form a tunnel winding all the way up the mountain. Each one was donated by someone whose wish came true, so walking through feels like passing by countless happy stories.

📍 Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)

A dazzling temple whose whole building shines with real gold leaf. On a clear day, its reflection in the pond makes it look like there are two golden pavilions.

📍 Arashiyama Bamboo Grove

Tall bamboo grows so thick it turns the sky green overhead. When the wind blows, the leaves rustle like a song—a sound so special it was chosen as one of Japan's soundscapes to protect.

📍 Gion District

A lane lined with old wooden houses. In the evening, you might spot geiko and maiko gliding by in gorgeous kimono—like stepping back in time to old Kyoto.
