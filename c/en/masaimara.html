🇰🇪 Kenya

🦁 Masai Mara

Where a million wild animals cross a river—the greatest wildlife show on Earth

📖 The Story

In southwestern Kenya, when the morning sun rises over an endless green grassland, lions stretch awake and a family of elephants strolls by. This is the Masai Mara. On this land, where the Maasai people have herded cattle for generations, all of Africa's 'Big Five'—lion, elephant, giraffe, leopard, and rhino—live side by side.

But the most amazing thing in the Masai Mara happens every summer. When the grass in Tanzania's Serengeti dries up, more than a million zebras and wildebeest set off on a massive migration, searching for water and fresh grass in the Mara. When this enormous herd walks, the ground actually rumbles, and you can hear the thunder of hooves from far away.

The most heart-pounding moment of the whole journey comes when the animals must cross the Mara River. Huge Nile crocodiles, some nearly 100 years old, wait quietly beneath the surface. Terrified animals shove and leap into the water together, and when the herd finally scrambles up the far bank, it looks almost like the whole crowd is roaring with relief.

The name 'Mara' means 'spotted' in the Maasai language—because clouds and acacia-tree shadows dapple the vast grassland like spots on a giant hide. Early in the morning, if you float up into the sky in a hot air balloon, you can look down and see that spotted plain and the countless animals walking across it all at once. It feels like gazing at a living map.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Every year, more than a million wildebeest and zebras travel between the Serengeti and the Masai Mara—it's called the 'Great Migration.'
  • 2'Mara' means 'spotted' in the Maasai language, named for the way clouds and tree shadows dapple the grassland with patches of light and dark.
  • 3The Masai Mara has one of the highest lion populations in Africa, earning it the nickname 'kingdom of lions.'
  • 4Some Nile crocodiles in the Mara River are nearly 100 years old, and they wait quietly each year for the migrating herds to cross.
  • 5There's no fence between the Masai Mara and Tanzania's Serengeti—animals can freely cross the invisible border between the two countries!
  • 6Float above the plains in a hot air balloon at sunrise, and you can spot herds of zebras, giraffes, and elephants all from the sky at once.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Mara River

The dramatic highlight of the Great Migration—animals risk everything to cross this river, while giant Nile crocodiles nearly 100 years old wait silently below.

📍 Mara Triangle

A vast grassland stretching below the Oloololo Escarpment. It's kept remarkably wild, famous for close-up sightings of lions, cheetahs, and elephants.

📍 Maasai Village (Manyatta)

A village where Maasai people wrapped in bright red shuka cloth have herded cattle for generations. Visitors can watch traditional dances and see how their mud houses are built.

📍 Hot Air Balloon Safari

Just as the sun rises, hot air balloons drift up over the plains. It's a magical way to look down on the spotted grassland and the herds of animals crossing it from high in the sky.
