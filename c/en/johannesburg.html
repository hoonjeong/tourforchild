🇿🇦 South Africa

🏙️ Johannesburg

The city built almost overnight on gold, next door to where humans took their first steps

📖 The Story

In the year 1886, a few people digging on a rocky ridge in South Africa found something shiny buried in the rock. It was gold — and not just a little. It turned out to be one of the biggest gold deposits ever discovered on Earth. Word spread fast, and soon thousands of fortune-seekers rushed in from all over the world, hoping to strike it rich.

Almost overnight, a tent camp turned into streets, then buildings, then a whole city: Johannesburg. People call it 'eGoli' — 'the Place of Gold' — and even today, hundreds of kilometers of old gold-mine tunnels snake through the ground beneath the city, some reaching almost 4 kilometers down, among the deepest holes humans have ever dug.

But gold isn't the oldest treasure around here. Just outside the city, hidden in a network of caves, scientists have found some of the very oldest fossils of human ancestors ever discovered — bones over 2 million years old, including a nearly complete skeleton nicknamed 'Little Foot.' Scientists now call this area the Cradle of Humankind, because it may be one of the places where our story as humans truly began.

So this one city holds both endings of an amazing story — the deepest of gold rushes, and some of humanity's very first footsteps. And today, it's also a doorway to adventure: just a short drive away, lions, elephants, giraffes, and rhinos roam free, waiting for the next family brave enough to go looking for them.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Gold Reef City

This theme park sits right on top of a real old gold mine. Brave visitors can ride an elevator 220 meters down the original mine shaft and even watch molten gold get poured into a bar.

📍 Sterkfontein Caves

Inside this cave system, scientists found some of the oldest human-ancestor fossils on the planet, including a nearly complete 2-million-year-old skeleton nicknamed 'Little Foot.' Walking through feels like stepping into humanity's very own storybook.

📍 Maropeng Visitor Centre

This museum sits at the entrance to the Cradle of Humankind and lets kids walk through an underground boat ride showing Earth's history, then come face to face with models of our earliest ancestors.

📍 Rhino and Lion Nature Reserve

Just outside the city, this reserve lets families spot lions, rhinos, and other wild animals roaming in the open — a perfect first taste of African safari, no long flight required.
