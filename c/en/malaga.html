🇪🇸 Spain

🏖️ Malaga

The 2,800-year-old port city that gave the world Picasso

📖 The Story

Almost 2,800 years ago, sailors from a faraway land called Phoenicia landed on the sunny coast of southern Spain and built a little trading town. They named it Malaka, and it grew into a busy port where ships came and went loaded with fish, oil, and salt. Today that same town is called Málaga — and it's one of the oldest cities still standing in all of Western Europe.

Over the centuries, many different people fell in love with this sunny harbor. Romans built a stone theatre where crowds watched plays under the open sky. Later, Moorish rulers arrived from North Africa and stayed for almost 800 years, building a hilltop fortress called the Alcazaba and, even higher up, a castle named Gibralfaro to watch for ships out at sea.

Then, in 1881, something wonderful happened in a sunny square in the middle of the city: a baby boy named Pablo Picasso was born. He grew up watching pigeons in the plaza and doodling on café napkins, and one day he'd become one of the most famous artists who ever lived. Málaga never forgot him — its Picasso Museum is filled with paintings his own family gave the city.

Walk through Málaga today and you can feel all of this history stacked up like layers of a cake — Roman stones under Moorish towers, Picasso's plaza a few steps from the beach. Fishermen still grill sardines on the sand the old way, and the castle on the hill still looks out over the very same blue sea the Phoenicians sailed in on, thousands of years ago.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Malaga is nearly 2,800 years old, founded by Phoenician sailors around 770 BC — one of the oldest cities in Western Europe.
  • 2Its old name 'Malaka' may come from a word for salt, because the town was famous for salting fish to make a tangy sauce called garum.
  • 3The Alcazaba fortress and Gibralfaro castle on the hill above are connected by a zigzagging wall, so soldiers could dash between them without ever stepping outside.
  • 4A Roman theatre lay buried and forgotten for centuries — workers only rediscovered it by accident in 1951 while building a garden.
  • 5Painter Pablo Picasso was born in Málaga in 1881; his family later donated hundreds of his artworks to the city's Picasso Museum.
  • 6On Málaga's beaches, cooks still skewer fresh sardines on bamboo sticks and grill them over open wood fires — a treat called 'espetos.'

📍 Places to visit

📍 The Alcazaba

This Moorish palace-fortress was built on top of old Roman ruins, so as you climb through its gardens and towers you're literally walking over layers of history. Peacocks roam its courtyards today, but centuries ago it was the seat of Muslim rulers watching over the port below.

📍 Gibralfaro Castle

Perched on the highest hill in town, this castle once kept watch for enemy ships out on the horizon. Climb its walls today and you'll get the best view in Málaga — rooftops, the harbor, and the same blue sea sailors have crossed for thousands of years.

📍 Roman Theatre

Tucked right at the foot of the Alcazaba, this stone theatre was built around 2,000 years ago for Romans to watch plays and music under the stars. It was buried and forgotten for ages until workers accidentally dug it up in 1951 — a true hidden treasure.

📍 Picasso Museum Málaga

Just steps from the plaza where Picasso was born, this museum holds paintings, sculptures, and sketches his own family gave to the city he never forgot. Kids can spot his playful, colorful style up close — proof that doodling can turn into something amazing.
