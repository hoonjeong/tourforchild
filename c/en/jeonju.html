🏮 Jeonju

Hometown of the king who founded a dynasty—a whole city of hanok and delicious food

📖 The Story

About 600 years ago, in a city in southwestern Korea, a special family lived quietly among the townsfolk. Their descendant, General Yi Seong-gye, would one day become a king. People started calling this town 'the hometown of kings'—and that town is Jeonju.

In 1392, General Yi founded a brand-new kingdom called Joseon and became its very first king. He never forgot the hometown of his ancestors. He built a special hall in Jeonju called Gyeonggijeon and placed his royal portrait inside it, so his hometown would always remember him.

About a hundred years ago, the people of Jeonju decided to protect their traditions by building hundreds of traditional wooden houses called hanok, all clustered together in one neighborhood. Today, more than 800 hanok roofs huddle side by side in Jeonju Hanok Village—the biggest hanok village in all of Korea.

Jeonju is also famous for its delicious food. Bibimbap—rice topped with colorful vegetables arranged like a rainbow—is said to have been invented right here. That's why UNESCO named Jeonju a 'Creative City of Gastronomy.' Walk through Jeonju today, and you can taste a thousand years of history in every bite!

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Jeonju is called the roots of the Joseon dynasty, because King Taejo's family lived here before he became king.
  • 2Inside Gyeonggijeon Shrine hangs a 600-year-old royal portrait of King Taejo—it's one of South Korea's National Treasures.
  • 3Jeonju Hanok Village has more than 800 tile-roofed hanok houses packed together—the biggest hanok village in Korea.
  • 4In 2012, UNESCO named Jeonju a 'Creative City of Gastronomy'—one of the very first cities in Asia to earn that title.
  • 5Jeonju bibimbap is famous for piling on more than 10 colorful toppings, arranged like a rainbow over the rice.
  • 6Jeonju hanji, handmade mulberry paper, has been crafted for over 1,000 years and was once used for the kingdom's royal documents.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Gyeonggijeon Shrine

This is where King Taejo's royal portrait is kept. A 600-year-old ginkgo tree stands nearby, as if it has watched over all that history too.

📍 Jeonju Hanok Village

A neighborhood where tiled rooftops ripple like waves. Its alleys are full of visitors dressed in colorful hanbok taking photos—it feels like stepping back in time.

📍 Pungnammun Gate

This was the southern gate of the old wall that once ringed Jeonju. Most of the wall is gone now, but this gate still stands tall, guarding the memory of the old city.

📍 Jeondong Catholic Church

A beautiful red-brick church built more than a hundred years ago. Standing right next to the hanok village, it creates a special scene where old Korean tradition and Western-style architecture meet side by side.
