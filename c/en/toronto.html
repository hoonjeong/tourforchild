🍁 Toronto

The lakeside city whose name means 'trees standing in water'—now home to a sky-high tower and a real castle

📖 The Story

Long before Toronto was a big city, Indigenous peoples—the Huron-Wendat, the Haudenosaunee, and the Mississaugas of the Credit—paddled a trail of rivers and lakes that cut straight through what is now Ontario. At one spot, wooden stakes stuck up out of shallow water, built to trap fish swimming by. In the Mohawk language, that spot was called 'tkaronto'—'where there are trees standing in the water.' Centuries later, settlers borrowed that beautiful name for the busy new town growing beside Lake Ontario.

That little lakeside town grew and grew until it became Toronto, now Canada's biggest city. Ships and trains brought people from every corner of the globe, and today more than half of everyone living there was born in another country. Walk down one street and you might hear over a hundred different languages—Toronto is one of the most mixed, most colorful cities on Earth.

Toronto also decided to touch the sky. In 1976, builders finished a needle-thin tower called the CN Tower, and for 32 years it was the tallest free-standing structure anywhere on land in the whole world! Near the top, brave visitors can stand on a floor made of solid glass and look straight down—more than 300 meters, with nothing but see-through glass between their sneakers and the tiny cars below.

But Toronto keeps its best surprises hidden. Underneath the busy downtown streets winds PATH, a secret underground city of tunnels where thousands of people shop and walk without ever feeling a raindrop. And on a hill above the city sits Casa Loma, a real castle with secret passages and a hidden staircase, built by a man who simply wanted to live like a knight. A whole city, born from one river word—still full of surprises today!

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1"Toronto" comes from the Mohawk/Huron word "tkaronto," meaning "where there are trees standing in the water"—describing wooden fish traps in a narrow channel far north of the city.
  • 2For 32 years (1975–2007) Toronto's CN Tower was the tallest free-standing structure on land anywhere in the world.
  • 3Near the top of the CN Tower you can walk out onto a glass floor and look straight down more than 300 meters.
  • 4Casa Loma is a real, honest-to-goodness castle right in the city, complete with secret tunnels, hidden passages, and stables for prize horses.
  • 5Under downtown Toronto runs PATH, the largest underground walking network in the world—about 30 kilometers of tunnels linking shops, subway stations, and buildings.
  • 6More than half of Toronto's people were born outside Canada, and over 160 languages are spoken there—making it one of the most multicultural cities on the planet.

📍 Places to visit

📍 CN Tower

For 32 years this needle-thin tower was the tallest free-standing structure on Earth. Near the top, you can stand on a real glass floor and look straight down more than 300 meters—if you dare!

📍 Casa Loma

A real castle right inside the city! It was built by a man who dreamed of living like a knight, complete with secret tunnels, hidden staircases, and stables fit for prize horses.

📍 Toronto Islands

A cluster of car-free islands just a quick ferry ride from downtown, with beaches, bike paths, and views of the skyline across the water. It's the perfect place to look back and see the whole city—including that giant tower—at once.

📍 Royal Ontario Museum

This giant museum has real dinosaur skeletons, ancient treasures, and a dazzling crystal-shaped addition that looks like giant glass rocks crashed into the old stone building. It's a perfect place to explore the many cultures that make Toronto so colorful.
