🏛️ Crete

The Greek island where gods were born and a maze hid a monster

📖 The Story

Long, long ago—more than 4,000 years ago—the island of Crete was home to one of Europe's very first great civilizations: the Minoans. Their king, Minos, ruled from a dazzling palace at Knossos with more than 1,300 rooms and hallways that twisted every which way. It was so enormous and confusing that visitors swore you could get lost in it forever.

Legend says something scary lived deep beneath that palace—a monster called the Minotaur, half man and half bull, locked away in a maze called the Labyrinth. The brilliant inventor Daedalus built that maze so cleverly that nobody who entered could ever find the way out—except the hero Theseus, who followed a ball of string given to him by princess Ariadne and made it home safely.

Here's the real, true part of the story: around 3,600 years ago, a gigantic volcano erupted on a nearby island, shaking the ground and sending huge waves crashing onto Crete's shores. Scientists believe this disaster helped weaken the mighty Minoans, and a few centuries later a great earthquake finally toppled their glittering palace at Knossos.

The palace lay buried and forgotten for thousands of years until an archaeologist dug it up about a hundred years ago and uncovered walls painted with leaping dolphins and athletes vaulting over the horns of charging bulls. Today you can walk those same ancient hallways and imagine the palace exactly as it was when the story of the Minotaur first began.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Knossos Palace

The legendary home of King Minos and his maze. Wander its rebuilt red columns and colorful frescoes, and you're standing exactly where the Minotaur story was born.

📍 Dikteon Cave

High in the Lasithi mountains, this dripping cave with glittering stalactites is said to be where baby Zeus was hidden from his father. Climb down into its cool, glowing chambers and see why people believed a god could be born here.

📍 Samaria Gorge

One of the longest gorges in Europe, this deep canyon cuts 16 kilometers through the White Mountains down to the sea. Hikers pass through the famous 'Iron Gates,' where the cliff walls squeeze to just a few meters apart.

📍 Elafonisi Beach

A dreamy lagoon where the sand looks pink! Millions of tiny crushed shells mix into the white sand, and the shallow turquoise water is so warm and calm that kids can wade far out from shore.
