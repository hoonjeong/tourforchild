⛰️ Rio de Janeiro

The 'Marvelous City' where a mountaintop Christ watches over jungle, beaches, and the world's biggest party

📖 The Story

Long ago, in the year 1502, Portuguese sailors sailed into a huge, sparkling bay in Brazil. They thought it was the mouth of a giant river, so they named it 'Rio de Janeiro'—'River of January.' It wasn't a river at all, just a beautiful bay ringed by green mountains that shoot straight up from the sea. But the name stuck, and a great city grew up around that bay, tucked between jungle-covered peaks and golden beaches.

Rio grew into one of the liveliest cities on Earth, and in the 1920s, people there decided to build something the whole world would recognize. High on a mountain called Corcovado, workers spent almost ten years hauling stone up by train and by hand to build a giant statue of Christ with his arms stretched wide, as if he were hugging the entire city. When it was finished in 1931, it became one of the New Seven Wonders of the World.

Every year, Rio throws the biggest party on the planet: Carnival. For five wild, sparkly days, samba schools with thousands of dancers in dazzling feathered costumes parade down a special runway called the Sambódromo, dancing and drumming until the whole city seems to shake with music.

But Rio isn't just concrete and confetti—it's wrapped in real jungle. Long ago, people cut down the forest on its hills to plant coffee, but the ground dried up so much that the city started running out of water. So people planted the whole forest back again, tree by tree, by hand. Today that forest, called Tijuca, has grown so thick and wild that it's the largest forest inside any city in the world, with waterfalls, monkeys, and toucans, all just minutes from the beach.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Christ the Redeemer (Corcovado)

Standing atop Corcovado Mountain with arms open wide, this giant statue has watched over Rio since 1931. You can ride a cozy little train straight up through the rainforest to reach it, and on a clear day, you can see the whole city, the bay, and the beaches spread out below.

📍 Sugarloaf Mountain

This smooth granite peak rises straight out of the sea like a giant loaf of sugar—which is exactly how it got its name. Since 1912, a glass cable car has carried visitors up in two stages, and from the top you can spot Christ the Redeemer waving back at you from across the bay.

📍 Copacabana Beach

This famous curved beach stretches for about 4 kilometers, its sidewalk paved in a swirling black-and-white wave pattern that ripples on for as far as you can see. Locals play footvolley (soccer played over a volleyball net!) right in the sand, and on New Year's Eve, millions gather here wearing white for fireworks over the ocean.

📍 Tijuca National Forest

Once stripped bare for coffee farms, this hillside jungle was replanted tree by tree in the 1800s—and today it's the largest forest inside any city on the planet. Wander its trails and you might spot a waterfall, a howler monkey, or a flash of a rainbow-colored toucan overhead.
