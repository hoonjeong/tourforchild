⛰️ Guilin

Hundreds of fairy-tale green mountains, and a river so pretty it's on the money

📖 The Story

Picture a place where hundreds of green, pointy mountains poke up out of flat land, like a fairy-tale painting come to life. That magical place is real, and it's called Guilin, in southern China. Its name means 'Forest of Sweet Osmanthus'—because every autumn, tiny golden flowers bloom all over the city and fill the air with a sweet, sugary smell.

But how did Guilin get those strange, pointy mountains? Long, long ago—over 300 million years ago—this whole area was hidden under a warm, shallow sea. Billions of tiny sea creatures died and left their shells behind, and over millions of years those shells piled up and turned into soft limestone rock. When the sea disappeared and the land rose up, rain and rivers slowly dissolved the limestone, carving it into thousands of tall, pointy peaks—like nature's own sandcastles.

Right through the middle of all those peaks winds a river called the Li River, and the view is so beautiful that people say, 'Guilin's mountains and waters are the finest under heaven.' It's so famous that its picture is printed on the back of China's 20-yuan money! One hill by the river even has its own legend: it's shaped exactly like an elephant dipping its trunk into the water, and people say it's a magical elephant who was turned to stone, with an old pagoda sitting on its back like the hilt of a sword.

Today, families still float down the Li River on bamboo rafts, past fishermen who use trained birds called cormorants to help them catch fish, just like people did hundreds of years ago. You can duck into glowing caves full of sparkling rock formations, or climb a hill with a hole shaped like the moon. In Guilin, every mountain seems to be telling its own wondrous story.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Li River

Float down the Li River on a bamboo raft and drift past endless green peaks lined up like a folding screen. The view is so famous it's painted onto China's 20-yuan bill.

📍 Elephant Trunk Hill

This hill looks exactly like an elephant dipping its trunk into the river. Legend says it's a magical elephant turned to stone, and the old pagoda on its back looks just like the handle of a sword.

📍 Reed Flute Cave

A cave glowing with colorful lights on sparkling stalactites and stalagmites. It's so dazzling people call it the 'Palace of Natural Art,' and travelers have been leaving poems and drawings on its walls for over 1,000 years.

📍 Moon Hill

A hill with a giant hole near the top, shaped just like a full moon. Climb the steps and you can look right through the hole at a picture-perfect view of fields and peaks below.
