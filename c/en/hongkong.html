🌆 Hong Kong

The little fishing village that grew into a skyline of a thousand lights

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, before Hong Kong had a single skyscraper, it was just a quiet fishing village tucked between green mountains and the sea. Fisherfolk lived on wooden boats bobbing in a deep, calm harbor, and traders shipped fragrant incense wood from here—which is why the name "Hong Kong" means "Fragrant Harbour."

That deep, sheltered harbor turned out to be magic for business. Ships from all over the world began stopping by, and the little fishing village grew and grew into a busy port city where East and West mixed together—Chinese temples stood near double-decker trams, and dim sum steamers sat beside British-style clock towers.

Today Hong Kong has one of the most famous skylines on Earth—towers of glass and steel packed onto a thin strip of land between the sea and the mountains. Every night, colorful lights dance across dozens of buildings in a show called the Symphony of Lights, and a little green-and-white boat called the Star Ferry still chugs across the harbor exactly the way it has for over 120 years.

But just a short ride away, the modern city fades and something totally different appears: a giant bronze Buddha sitting calmly on a mountaintop, and a village where houses stand on stilts over the water. In Hong Kong, ancient fishing boats and shiny skyscrapers, quiet temples and roaring city streets—all somehow live together in the very same place.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1"Hong Kong" means "Fragrant Harbour"—named for the sweet-smelling incense wood once shipped out from its port.
  • 2The Star Ferry has been chugging across Victoria Harbour for more than 120 years, and it's still one of the cheapest, most scenic boat rides in the world.
  • 3The Peak Tram climbing up to Victoria Peak is one of the oldest funicular railways in Asia—it's been hauling people up the steep mountain since 1888.
  • 4Hong Kong has more skyscrapers than any other city on the planet—over 500 buildings taller than 150 meters!
  • 5The Tian Tan Buddha on Lantau Island is one of the world's largest seated bronze Buddha statues, and you have to climb 268 steps just to reach it.
  • 6Every night, the "Symphony of Lights" show lights up dozens of skyscrapers around Victoria Harbour—it once held the Guinness World Record for the largest permanent light show on Earth.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Victoria Peak

The highest hill overlooking the city, reachable by a super-steep old tram. From the top you can see the whole forest of skyscrapers and the harbor spread out like a glittering toy city.

📍 Star Ferry

A little green-and-white ferry that's been crossing Victoria Harbour since 1888. It costs almost nothing, but the view of the skyline from the water is one of the most famous in the world.

📍 Tian Tan Buddha (Lantau Island)

A giant bronze Buddha sitting on top of a mountain, watching calmly over the island. Climb its 268 steps, or float up close in a glass-bottomed cable car.

📍 Tai O Fishing Village

An old fishing village where wooden houses are built on stilts right over the water. Boats still glide between the houses like a watery neighborhood street.
