🏝️ Bali

The Island of the Gods, where the whole island goes dark and silent once a year

📖 The Story

Bali is a small island in Indonesia, a country made up of more than 17,000 islands where most people follow Islam. But Bali is different: almost everyone here follows a Hindu faith that first came from India many centuries ago, filled with gods, spirits, and daily rituals. Walk down any street and you'll step around tiny woven baskets of flowers, rice, and incense—offerings called 'canang sari'—left on doorsteps, sidewalks, and even car dashboards, refreshed several times every single day.

Once a year, though, Bali goes completely quiet. It's called Nyepi, the Day of Silence, and it's like nothing else on Earth: no lights, no fires, no work, no travel—even the airport shuts down for 24 whole hours. The night before, giant scary monster statues called 'ogoh-ogoh' are paraded through the streets with drums and torches, then burned, to scare away evil spirits before the island goes dark and silent.

Bali's green hills are carved into giant staircases of rice paddies that shimmer like mirrors in the sun. Farmers have shared the mountain water fairly for over a thousand years using a clever system called 'subak,' guided by water temples that decide whose field gets watered first. It's so old and so wise that the whole system is now protected as a World Heritage treasure.

Bali's temples seem to grow right out of the land and sea. One sits on a rock offshore, cut off by waves at high tide; another perches on a cliff seventy meters above crashing surf, watched over by cheeky monkeys. Behind it all looms Mount Agung, a sacred volcano the Balinese believe is a home of the gods—so almost everything on the island, even the direction houses face, points respectfully toward it.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Tanah Lot Temple

A small Hindu temple perched on a rock just offshore, surrounded by crashing waves. At high tide it becomes its own tiny island, and legends say a sea snake guards it from evil spirits and intruders.

📍 Uluwatu Temple

This temple balances on a cliff 70 meters above crashing waves, guarded by cheeky monkeys who love to snatch sunglasses. Every evening, dancers perform the hypnotic 'Kecak' fire dance here as the sun sinks into the ocean.

📍 Tegalalang Rice Terraces

Emerald-green rice paddies cascade down the hillside like giant staircases, watered by the ancient 'subak' system. Farmers still tend the fields by hand here, just as their ancestors did over a thousand years ago.

📍 Mount Batur

An active volcano rising beside a big crater lake, where hikers climb in the dark to watch the sunrise from its smoking summit. Balinese believe mountains like this are sacred homes of the gods, which is why temples always face toward them.
