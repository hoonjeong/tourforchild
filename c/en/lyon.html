🦁 Lyon

The city of two rivers, secret passageways, and the night movies were born

📖 The Story

More than two thousand years ago, Roman settlers climbed a hill overlooking two rivers in what is now France and built a city called Lugdunum. Below them, the fast blue Rhône River rushed into the slow green Saône, swirling together like paint mixing in water. Lugdunum grew so important that it became the capital of all Roman Gaul, and today we call it Lyon.

Centuries later, Lyon became famous for something delicate: silk. Weavers called canuts carried bolts of precious silk across the hilly old town every day, but rain could ruin the fabric in seconds. So they built hundreds of secret shortcuts called traboules—hidden passages that cut straight through courtyards and buildings. Step through an ordinary doorway in Vieux Lyon today, and you might pop out on a completely different street!

Then, in 1895, two brothers named Auguste and Louis Lumière tried something nobody had ever done before. In a factory in Lyon, they pointed a strange box at moving workers and captured them on film. When they showed the film to a room full of people, the audience gasped and some say they even ducked, sure that a train rolling toward the camera was about to burst into the room. It was the world's very first movie, and fittingly, 'Lumière' means 'light' in French.

Lyon never forgot that magic. Every December 8th, families set tiny candles along every windowsill in the city, and the whole town glows for the Fête des Lumières, the Festival of Lights. It began as a thank-you after Lyon was spared from a terrible plague long ago, and now millions of visitors come each year to watch Lyon shine.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Lyon began as a Roman city called Lugdunum in 43 BC, and it grew so powerful it became the capital of all Roman Gaul—you can still climb up to see its ancient Roman theatre today.
  • 2Lyon sits exactly where two rivers meet: the fast blue Rhône and the slow green Saône. Stand at the tip of the peninsula and you can actually see their two colors swirling together before they blend!
  • 3Vieux Lyon hides more than 500 secret passageways called traboules. Silk workers once used them to keep fabric dry, and decades later, secret fighters used them to hide from danger during World War II.
  • 4In 1895, brothers Auguste and Louis Lumière invented the movie camera in Lyon and showed the world's very first film. Their last name, Lumière, means 'light' in French!
  • 5Every December 8th, Lyon glows for the Fête des Lumières. It began in 1852 when people spontaneously lit candles in their windows, and Lyon has lit up every year since.
  • 6The famous puppet Guignol was born in Lyon around 1808. He was invented by a tooth-puller who used funny puppet shows to help nervous patients forget their fear before treatment!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Fourvière Hill

Climb this hill and you'll find a real Roman theatre from Lugdunum's earliest days, plus a sparkling white basilica watching over the whole city. From the top, you can see both rivers glittering below—exactly why the Romans chose this spot 2,000 years ago.

📍 The Traboules of Vieux Lyon

Duck through an unmarked doorway in the old town and follow a traboule—one of the secret passages silk weavers once used to protect their fabric from rain. Some lead through quiet courtyards, others pop out on a totally different street.

📍 Institut Lumière

This is the very house and factory where Auguste and Louis Lumière invented the movie camera and filmed the world's first film in 1895. Step inside to see their original equipment and imagine that very first flickering picture.

📍 La Confluence

Right at the tip of Lyon's peninsula, the fast blue Rhône crashes into the slow green Saône. Look closely at the water and you might just catch the moment the two colors swirl into one.
