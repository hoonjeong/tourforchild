🏙️ Ho Chi Minh City

A city of a million scooters — with a secret underground world beneath it

📖 The Story

Arrive in Ho Chi Minh City and the very first thing you'll notice is the traffic — but not cars. It's scooters, thousands and thousands of them, flowing down the streets like a river made of motorbikes! This huge city sits beside the Saigon River in southern Vietnam, and its markets smell of sizzling noodles, fresh herbs, and strong sweet coffee dripped drop by drop.

Long before it was this big, the city was a small fishing village. Over hundreds of years it grew and changed names again and again, finally becoming known as Saigon. When French builders arrived, they shipped bricks and iron all the way from France by boat to build a grand pink cathedral and a fairy-tale post office — everything built strong and to last, so nothing here would rot in the rain or fall apart.

Then, during a long and difficult war, something incredible happened underground. Villagers dug secret tunnels so long that if you laid them out straight, they'd stretch farther than 200 kilometers! Down there were hidden kitchens, tiny hospitals, and even classrooms, all connected by tunnels no wider than a grown-up's shoulders. Smoke from cooking fires was cleverly piped far away and let out to look like morning mist, so nobody outside would ever guess a hidden world was breathing right beneath their feet.

In 1975, the city took a brand-new name — Ho Chi Minh City — to honor the leader who helped unite the country. Today it's a dazzling mix of glass skyscrapers and that same old cathedral, sizzling street stalls beside giant glowing screens, with kids riding to school tucked safely on the back of a parent's scooter. Day or night, this city never really sleeps — it just keeps humming, buzzing, and flowing, like that river of scooters, always on the move.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Ben Thanh Market

This bustling market's clock tower has been a symbol of the city for over 100 years. Inside, stalls packed tightly together sell everything from steaming bowls of pho to silk scarves and juicy tropical fruit — it's a feast for your nose and eyes at once.

📍 Notre-Dame Cathedral

Every brick of this rosy-pink cathedral sailed here by boat all the way from France over a hundred years ago. Twin bell towers rise above the square, and the pink never fades because the color is baked right into the brick itself.

📍 Central Post Office

Right next to the cathedral stands a grand old post office with high arched ceilings and a giant antique map on the wall. Locals still line up here to mail letters and postcards, just like they did over a hundred years ago.

📍 Cu Chi Tunnels

About an hour outside the city, you can crawl through part of the real Cu Chi tunnel network. Some passages have been made a little wider for visitors, but you'll still feel just how small, dark, and clever this hidden underground world truly was.
