🏰 Cesky Krumlov

The fairy-tale town a river tied in a knot

📖 The Story

Long ago, a river called the Vltava came curling through the hills of Bohemia, looping so sharply that it circled almost all the way around itself, like a snake tying a knot. Right in that curl, people built a castle on a rocky cliff, and a little town grew snug against the water below. They named it Krumlov, meaning "crooked meadow," after the river's wild bend.

A noble family called the Rosenbergs moved in and turned the castle into something enormous — one palace after another, courtyard after courtyard, until it became the second-biggest castle in the whole country, beaten only by the castle in Prague. To make themselves sound extra fancy, the Rosenbergs even claimed to be related to an Italian family whose name meant "bear." So what did they do? They kept real, live bears in the moat right by the castle gate — and some still live there today!

Painters covered the castle's tallest tower in swirling patterns so clever that people still get fooled by them. From the ground it looks like carved stone blocks and fancy windows, but it's all just flat paint — a giant trick for the eyes! High above the town, the family also built a strange, stacked bridge with a hidden hallway inside, so they could stroll from the castle to their garden without a single ordinary person spotting them.

For hundreds of years afterward, the little town went quiet — too poor and too out-of-the-way for anyone to knock down the old buildings and put up new ones. That's exactly why it survived, untouched, like a fairy tale nobody woke up. Today you can float down the same curling river in a rubber raft, paddle right past the castle walls, and wave up at the bears — in a town that time somehow forgot to change.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Cesky Krumlov Castle

Perched on a rocky cliff above the river bend, this is the second-largest castle in the Czech Republic. Climb the tall Round Tower and look closely — those 'carved stone' patterns are just clever painting!

📍 Bear Moat

Right by the castle's stone bridge, real bears have padded around this moat since the 1700s. The noble family wanted everyone to think they were related to a family whose name meant 'bear' — so they got themselves actual bears!

📍 Cloak Bridge

This odd, stacked bridge has three levels of stone arches and a hidden hallway tucked inside. Nobles used it to slip from the castle straight to the theatre and gardens without a single person down in the town spotting them.

📍 The Vltava River Bend

Stand on any bridge in town and you'll see the river curl almost all the way around itself — the very bend that gave the town its name. In summer, hop in a rubber raft here and float right past the castle walls.
