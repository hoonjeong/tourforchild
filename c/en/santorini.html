🌅 Santorini

The island a volcano split in two — and maybe inspired Atlantis

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, in the middle of the Aegean Sea there was one round island called Strongyli. A huge volcano slept quietly at its center. Back then, people lived in a lively seaside town called Akrotiri. They painted colorful pictures on their walls and sailed ships to trade goods with faraway lands.

Then, about 3,600 years ago, the ground began to shake, and the volcano erupted with tremendous force — one of the biggest eruptions the world has ever seen. Luckily, the earlier tremors had warned everyone, so people packed up and hurried away from the town in time. That's why, unlike Pompeii, not a single trace of a person was ever found at Akrotiri.

The eruption was so enormous that the whole center of the island collapsed and sank, and seawater rushed in to fill the giant hollow — a caldera. The once-round island became the crescent shape we know today. Some people believe this dramatic event is the true story behind the ancient legend of Atlantis, the island that sank beneath the waves.

Today, Santorini's white houses and blue-domed roofs cling together along the cliff's edge. Every evening, people gather in the village of Oia to watch the sun sink into the sea and clap when it disappears. On a small island in the middle of the sea, called Nea Kameni, the volcano still breathes warmly — proof that the mountain hasn't vanished, only fallen asleep for a while.

  • 1Santorini used to be one round island called Strongyli, until a volcano blew its whole center away about 3,600 years ago — one of the biggest eruptions in human history.
  • 2The blasted-out center filled with seawater, forming the huge sunken bay we call a caldera today.
  • 3The ancient town of Akrotiri was buried in ash just like Pompeii, but not a single body was ever found — early earthquakes warned everyone to escape in time.
  • 4Some scientists think this eruption inspired the ancient legend of Atlantis, the island that vanished beneath the sea.
  • 5Santorini has beaches in different colors — black, red, and white — each formed from a different kind of volcanic rock.
  • 6It almost never rains here, so grapevines are woven into low, round basket shapes called 'kouloura' to shield the grapes from wind and trap the morning dew.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Akrotiri Archaeological Site

This buried Minoan town is often called the 'Greek Pompeii,' but it's even older and no bodies were ever found here. Walk its ash-covered streets and see three-story houses, drainage pipes, and colorful frescoes painted 3,600 years ago.

📍 Oia Village

This clifftop village sits right on the rim of the volcano's crater. Every evening, travelers gather among its blue-domed churches to watch one of the world's most famous sunsets sink into the sea.

📍 Fira Town

The island's capital clings to the caldera's edge, 300 meters above the sea. You can ride a cable car — or a friendly donkey — up the zigzag cliff path to reach it.

📍 Nea Kameni Volcanic Islet

A small dark island rising right in the middle of the caldera — the very volcano that changed everything. You can hike to its smoking crater and even swim in warm, mineral-rich waters nearby.
