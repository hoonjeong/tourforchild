☕ Melbourne

The gold-rush city where trams rattle, laneways glow with art, and penguins waddle home every night

📖 The Story

Long, long before any city stood here, Aboriginal peoples known as the Kulin Nations—especially the Wurundjeri—lived beside a winding river called the Yarra for tens of thousands of years, fishing and gathering along its banks. In 1835, settlers from Europe arrived and built a small trading post beside that same river, naming it Melbourne. Back then it was just a muddy little village with a handful of wooden huts.

Then in 1851, gold was discovered in the hills outside the city—and everything changed overnight! Ships packed with dream-chasers sailed in from every corner of the world, and Melbourne's population exploded almost instantly. So much gold poured through the city that Melbourne briefly became one of the richest places on Earth, and people started calling it 'Marvellous Melbourne.'

All that gold money built grand stone buildings, wide boulevards, and a brand-new way to get around: trams. Melbourne's tram network grew so big that today it's still the largest in the entire world, with hundreds of kilometers of track still rattling through the city.

These days, Melbourne's story keeps growing in surprising new ways. Its narrow laneways, once just quiet alleys, are now covered top to bottom in wild, colorful street art. And every evening at St Kilda beach, tiny penguins waddle out of the sea and tuck themselves into the rocks for the night—a whole different kind of city magic.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Melbourne has the largest tram network in the world—over 250 km of track, with some trams more than 100 years old still trundling along.
  • 2During the 1850s gold rush, Melbourne got so rich so fast that people called it 'Marvellous Melbourne'—it was briefly one of the wealthiest cities on the planet.
  • 3Laneways like Hosier Lane are covered in constantly changing street art, since artists are legally allowed to paint over old works—so it looks different every visit.
  • 4Every night, tiny 'little penguins'—the smallest penguin species in the world—waddle ashore at St Kilda Pier and hide in the rocks until morning.
  • 5The Melbourne Cricket Ground (the 'MCG') can hold about 100,000 people and was where Australia played its very first cricket Test match.
  • 6Locals joke that Melbourne has 'four seasons in one day,' because the weather can swing from sunshine to rain and wind before lunchtime.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Flinders Street Station

Melbourne's grand yellow train station, over 100 years old. Rows of clocks above the entrance are why people say 'meet me under the clocks'—still the city's favorite meeting spot today.

📍 Royal Exhibition Building & Carlton Gardens

A grand building built with gold-rush money. Australia's first federal Parliament was opened here in 1901, and it's now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

📍 Hosier Lane

A laneway with every wall covered in colorful street art. Artists are free to paint new works over old ones, so the picture you see today might be completely different tomorrow.

📍 St Kilda Pier

Tiny penguins nest in the rocks beside this long wooden pier. At dusk, you can watch them waddle out of the sea and head home for the night.
