🎡 Macau

The tiny city where Portugal and China blended together for 400 years

📖 The Story

Long ago, on the southern coast of China, there was a small fishing village. The villagers built an old temple to honor A-Ma, the goddess of the sea, and prayed she would bring their fishing boats safely home. Then one day, sailors from far-away Portugal arrived on that very shore.

The Portuguese sailors asked the villagers, 'What is this place called?' The villagers answered with the temple's name: 'A-Ma-Gau,' meaning 'Bay of A-Ma.' To Portuguese ears, it sounded like 'Macau'—and that is how this little harbor got its name.

From that day on, Portugal settled here and grew it into a busy trading port for more than 400 years. Over that long time, Portuguese-style plazas and churches stood side by side with Chinese temples and alleyways, and street signs began appearing in both Portuguese and Chinese. Bit by bit, two very different worlds blended into one single city.

In 1999, Portugal peacefully handed Macau back to China. But the mark the two nations had made together never disappeared. Today, when you walk through Macau, you can meet Portugal and China holding hands at every single step.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Ruins of St. Paul's

This 17th-century church burned down in 1835, leaving only its carved stone front wall standing. It rises like a grand stone stage backdrop and is Macau's most famous landmark.

📍 A-Ma Temple

An old temple honoring A-Ma, goddess of the sea—the very place Macau's name began. Wisps of incense smoke still curl through this quiet temple where the story of Macau first started.

📍 Senado Square

A square in the heart of Macau's old town, paved with black and white stones in a wave pattern—so walking across it feels like strolling over ocean ripples. Colorful Portuguese-style buildings line the edges.

📍 Taipa Old Village (Egg Tart Lane)

A cozy village where narrow lanes smell of freshly baked egg tarts. A little pastry that traveled from Portugal was reborn here as Macau's own sweet, beloved treat.
