🌋 Pompeii

The 2,000-year-old Roman city a volcano froze in time

📖 The Story

Long, long ago—2,000 years back—there was a lively city called Pompeii in southern Italy. Its markets buzzed with people, and bakeries smelled of fresh bread. Kids played on the stone-paved streets, and behind the town stood a huge mountain called Vesuvius. But nobody knew that mountain was actually a dangerous volcano.

Then, on a summer day in the year 79 AD, the mountain suddenly went 'BOOM!' A column of ash shot tens of kilometers into the sky, and hot ash and stones rained down on the city for two whole days. In no time, Pompeii was buried under more than 6 meters of ash and vanished from people's memory.

But something amazing happened. Because the ash covered the whole city so fast, the bread in the bakeries, the paintings on the walls, even the cart-wheel grooves in the road all hardened exactly as they were that day. Pompeii wasn't gone forever—it had become a giant time capsule, fast asleep for 2,000 years.

Then, about 250 years ago, people digging in the ground stumbled upon this sleeping city! Today, when you walk through Pompeii, you can see how Romans lived, what they ate, and how they played 2,000 years ago—like taking a time-travel trip back to that very day.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The ash covered everything so fast that bread, dishes, and paintings hardened in place—the whole city became a time capsule.
  • 2Deep cart-wheel grooves are still in the stone road, plus stepping-stones people hopped across so their shoes stayed dry—an ancient crosswalk!
  • 3The walls are covered in 2,000-year-old graffiti—Romans wrote 'so-and-so was here' too!
  • 4Where bodies vanished inside the hardened ash, empty spaces were left; plaster poured in brought back the shapes of people and even a dog.
  • 5One house has a floor mosaic reading 'CAVE CANEM'—'Beware of the dog.' They had 'beware of dog' signs 2,000 years ago!
  • 6Pompeians bought warm food at fast-food counters called 'thermopolia'—just like a snack bar today!

📍 Places to visit

📍 The Forum

The wide square that was Pompeii's beating heart. People shopped, held trials, and threw festivals here. Turn around and you'll see Vesuvius—the volcano that started it all.

📍 Amphitheatre

One of the oldest surviving Roman amphitheatres. Up to 20,000 fans packed in to cheer on gladiator fights—a lot like a soccer stadium today!

📍 Thermopolium (ancient snack bar)

Pompeii's 2,000-year-old 'fast-food' shop. Big jars were set into a stone counter to keep food warm, and there were even painted menu pictures on the wall!

📍 Garden of the Fugitives

Plaster casts here bring back the final moments of people fleeing the eruption. It's sad, but it's the most vivid, moving way to feel that day 2,000 years ago.
