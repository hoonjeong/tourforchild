🎨 Madrid

Spain's capital, chosen because it sits at the very heart of the map

📖 The Story

Long ago, on a dry hilltop above a small river in the middle of Spain, Muslim rulers built a fortress to guard the road to the great city of Toledo. They called it 'Mayrit,' probably from an old word for the many underground springs bubbling beneath the ground. For centuries it stayed a modest little town — nobody dreamed it would ever become a capital.

Then, in 1561, King Philip II looked at his map of Spain and noticed something: this small town called Madrid sat almost exactly in the middle of the whole country. So he picked it as his new capital, and today a brass plaque in the main square marks 'Kilometre Zero' — the very point every road in Spain is measured from.

Once it became the king's city, Madrid grew fast and grand. Kings built a giant Royal Palace with thousands of rooms, and later a lush green park called El Retiro, with a glass palace and a lake for rowing boats. Painters like Velázquez and Goya filled the new Prado Museum with some of the most famous paintings on Earth.

Madrid still keeps its wildest old secret, though: its coat of arms shows a bear standing on its hind legs, reaching up into a strawberry tree — because bears really once roamed the forests around the city, snacking on its sweet red berries. Every New Year's Eve, crowds still gather right at Kilometre Zero to eat twelve lucky grapes as the clock strikes midnight — one grape per chime, one wish per month of the year ahead.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Puerta del Sol

This bustling square is home to the 'Kilometre Zero' plaque, the exact point every Spanish road is measured from. Look for the statue of a bear reaching into a strawberry tree — Madrid's official symbol. On New Year's Eve, thousands gather here to eat their lucky midnight grapes.

📍 Royal Palace of Madrid

This is one of the biggest palaces in Europe, with about 3,000 rooms — more than Buckingham Palace or Versailles! Kings and queens once lived here, and the Spanish royal family still uses it for grand ceremonies today.

📍 Prado Museum

One of the greatest art museums on Earth, packed with masterpieces by Velázquez and Goya. Don't miss Goya's eerie 'Black Paintings,' once painted right onto the walls of his own house.

📍 Retiro Park

Madrid's grandest park has a lake for rowing boats and a shimmering Crystal Palace made almost entirely of glass. It's the perfect place to imagine the wild forests — and maybe even bears — that once grew where the city now stands.
