🐻 Berlin

The city a wall once split in two — and the world watched come back together

📖 The Story

Berlin, the capital of Germany, is an old city built along the Spree River. Right in the heart of Europe, it's full of grand palaces and museums—but its most amazing story happened less than a hundred years ago.

After World War II ended, Berlin was split in two: West Berlin and East Berlin. Then one night in 1961, East German soldiers suddenly began building a wall straight through the middle of the city. When people woke up the next morning, a neighbor who'd been right next door the day before was now, overnight, a citizen of a different country.

For the next 28 years, the Berlin Wall cut the city in half, and people could cross at only a few spots, like the famous 'Checkpoint Charlie.' But people never gave up. They dug secret tunnels underground, hid in car trunks, and paddled homemade boats across the river just to reach their families. On the west side, people painted the wall in bright colors—while the east side stayed grim and heavily guarded.

Then, on November 9, 1989, the wall finally opened! People from both East and West climbed on top together, hugging and dancing, and swung hammers to chip off pieces as souvenirs. Today, the longest surviving stretch is covered edge to edge with paintings by artists from all over the world, making it the biggest open-air art gallery on Earth. Berlin shows us that even a wall can fall—and people can become one again.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Brandenburg Gate

For decades, this grand gate stood locked inside the 'death strip' between East and West — so close, yet no one could touch it. When the wall fell, crowds gathered right here to celebrate, and today it stands as the proud symbol of a reunited Berlin.

📍 East Side Gallery

This is the longest piece of the Berlin Wall still standing — over a kilometer long. Right after the wall fell, artists from around the world painted it edge to edge, turning a symbol of division into the world's largest open-air art gallery.

📍 Checkpoint Charlie

This was the most famous crossing point between East and West Berlin, guarded by soldiers on both sides. Tanks once faced off here nose-to-nose, but today you can pose for photos where the tension used to be so thick you could almost touch it.

📍 Fernsehturm (TV Tower)

At over 200 meters tall, this is the tallest structure in Germany, and you can see it from almost anywhere in Berlin. On sunny days, light bounces off its silver ball into a giant cross — a twist the atheist East German government never wanted, nicknamed 'the Pope's Revenge.'
