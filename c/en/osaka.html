🐙 Osaka

The gold-roofed castle city that became Japan's laughter-loving kitchen

📖 The Story

Long ago, Osaka grew rich as a merchant city built along wide rivers. So many boats crowded in from all over Japan, carrying rice and goods, that people started calling Osaka "the nation's kitchen" — if Japan needed rice or fish or fabric, chances were it passed through Osaka first.

In the middle of this bustling city, a powerful lord named Toyotomi Hideyoshi built an enormous castle more than 400 years ago. He wanted the biggest, boldest castle in Japan, so he covered parts of the roof in gold and filled it with treasure. For a while, Osaka Castle was the most dazzling fortress the country had ever seen.

But castles have tough lives. Osaka Castle burned down in a great battle, got struck by lightning, and burned again — not just once, but several times over the centuries! Each time, though, people rebuilt it, because Osaka refused to let its proud symbol disappear.

Today's castle was rebuilt in 1931, and it hides a very modern secret: an elevator zips visitors straight up to the top floor! All around it, the city still buzzes like it always has — sizzling street food, giant glowing signs, and people who love nothing more than making each other laugh.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Osaka Castle's roof once glittered with golden tiger-fish ornaments called shachihoko, meant to protect the castle from fire — even though it burned down anyway!
  • 2The castle you see today was rebuilt in 1931, and it has a secret modern elevator that carries visitors all the way to the top floor.
  • 3During the Edo period, prices set at Osaka's rice market were followed by the whole country, which is why people called it "the nation's kitchen."
  • 4The giant glowing Glico Running Man sign has lit up the Dotonbori canal since 1935 and has been redesigned many times, always bigger and brighter.
  • 5Osaka is considered Japan's comedy capital — a joking style called manzai started here, and locals are famous for making strangers laugh.
  • 6Takoyaki, the round grilled octopus balls loved all over Japan, were invented right here in Osaka.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Osaka Castle

The very castle from the story, rebuilt with gold-topped roofs and a hidden elevator inside. Climb to the top floor and the whole city spreads out below, just like the view the lords who ruled here once saw.

📍 Dotonbori

A canal-side street packed with giant glowing signs, including the famous Glico Running Man. It's the best place to sniff out sizzling takoyaki and feel Osaka's food-loving, laughter-loving spirit in action.

📍 Shitennoji Temple

One of Japan's very first official Buddhist temples, built way back in 593 by Prince Shotoku. Walking through its ancient gates feels like stepping even further back in time than the castle does.

📍 Kuromon Ichiba Market

A lively covered market nicknamed "Osaka's Kitchen," packed with stalls selling fresh seafood, fruit, and street snacks. It's the perfect spot to taste why Osaka earned its foodie reputation.
