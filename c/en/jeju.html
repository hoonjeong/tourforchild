🍊 Jeju

The volcano-built island where sea-diving grandmas hold their breath and dive for treasure

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, the spot where Jeju Island now sits was just open ocean. Deep underground, bubbling hot magma pushed up through the sea floor and burst into fire. Volcanoes erupted again and again for millions of years, until finally a great island rose above the waves. Right in its middle stands a big, round volcano called Hallasan.

Every time the volcano erupted, small hills popped up all around it too. Jeju people call these little volcanic bumps "oreum," and more than 360 of them are scattered across the island! Hot lava that flowed all the way to the sea left behind fields of black rock — and long tunnels underground. One of those tunnels, Manjanggul, is a giant lava cave so big you can walk right through it.

Jeju is also home to some very special people: the "haenyeo," or sea women, who hold their breath and dive deep into the ocean without any oxygen tanks to gather abalone and seaweed. Some of them can hold their breath for minutes at a time — can you believe that? Passed down from mother to daughter for generations, this amazing skill is now protected as a treasured UNESCO tradition.

Visit Jeju today, and you can hike all the way up Hallasan to Baengnokdam, a blue crater lake at the top, or watch the sun rise red over the sea at Seongsan Ilchulbong. You can walk through a pitch-dark lava cave, and meet stone grandfather statues called "dol hareubang" standing along black rock walls. Shaped by fire and sea together, this island keeps surprising you with a new story at every step — a living volcano museum.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Hallasan (Mt. Halla)

South Korea's tallest mountain — and the very volcano that built Jeju Island. Climb all the way up, and a blue crater lake called Baengnokdam appears at the top! Legend says immortals once rode white deer here to drink from it.

📍 Seongsan Ilchulbong (Sunrise Peak)

A giant, castle-shaped peak born when a volcano erupted right out of the sea about 5,000 years ago. Climb up early and watch the red sun rise over the ocean — you'll gasp "wow!" without even trying. It's also a UNESCO World Natural Heritage site.

📍 Manjanggul Cave

A long underground tunnel carved out by rivers of flowing hot lava. It's one of the longest lava tube caves in the world, and walking inside feels like strolling through the belly of a volcano. Deep inside stands a giant stone pillar formed when lava hardened in place.

📍 Jeju Haenyeo Museum (Sea Women's Culture)

Here you can meet the story of Jeju's haenyeo — sea-diving grandmas who plunge deep underwater without oxygen tanks to gather abalone and conch. You might even hear their famous "hoo-i!" whistle-breath as they surface for air. This one-of-a-kind skill and culture is now a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage.
