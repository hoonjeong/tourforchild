🦌 Nara

Japan's first capital, where deer bow and a giant Buddha has stood watch for 1,300 years

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, in the year 710, Japan built its very first true capital city right here in Nara. It was called Heijo-kyo, and it was designed to look just like Chang'an, the grand capital of China, with wide streets laid out in a neat grid. Emperors lived in a shining palace, and merchants, monks, and travelers filled the streets below.

Around that time, sickness and troubles were spreading across the land, so the emperor made a bold decision: build the biggest bronze Buddha statue Japan had ever seen, and a temple grand enough to hold it. Almost the entire country's bronze went into casting the statue, and it's said that more than two million people helped build it. When it was finished, the Great Buddha Hall at Todai-ji became one of the largest wooden buildings on Earth.

But Nara has another story too — about deer. Long ago, people believed a god arrived in Nara riding on a pure white deer, so from that day on, deer were treated as sacred messengers instead of wild animals. For more than a thousand years, no one has been allowed to harm them, so today hundreds of deer wander freely through the park, bowing their heads for a cracker.

Even after Japan's capital moved away to Kyoto, Nara's temples stayed right where they were. Walk through Nara today, past bowing deer, beneath the towering Great Buddha, and inside Horyu-ji's ancient halls — the oldest wooden buildings in the world — and it feels like stepping straight into a storybook that's 1,300 years old.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Nara was Japan's very first true capital, founded in 710 and named Heijo-kyo.
  • 2The Great Buddha inside Todai-ji stands about 15 meters tall — as high as a 4-to-5-story building — with a face as long as a grown-up is tall.
  • 3One pillar in Todai-ji has a hole cut exactly the size of the Buddha's nostril. Legend says squeezing through it brings good luck, so kids always give it a try.
  • 4Nara's deer are considered sacred messengers of the gods and have been protected for over a thousand years — and they've even learned to bow for a deer cracker.
  • 5Horyu-ji's buildings are the oldest wooden structures still standing anywhere in the world, built about 1,300 years ago.
  • 6Todai-ji's treasure house, the Shosoin Repository, is packed with goods that traveled the Silk Road — Persian glassware, Chinese instruments — like a time capsule of ancient trade.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Todai-ji Temple (Great Buddha Hall)

One of the largest wooden buildings on Earth, home to a bronze Great Buddha standing about 15 meters tall. Don't miss the pillar with a hole exactly the size of the Buddha's nostril — squeeze through for good luck!

📍 Nara Park (Deer Park)

Home to hundreds of deer that have been protected as sacred messengers for over a thousand years. Hold out a deer cracker, and some will actually bow their heads before taking it!

📍 Kasuga Taisha Shrine

The very shrine linked to the legend of the god who arrived riding a white deer. A forest path is lined with over 3,000 stone lanterns, and when they're all lit at night, the sight feels almost magical.

📍 Horyu-ji Temple

The oldest wooden buildings still standing anywhere in the world, built roughly 1,300 years ago. It's amazing that they're still standing strong after all this time.
