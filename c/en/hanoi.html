🏮 Hanoi

The 1,000-year-old 'dragon' capital where a magic sword sleeps in a lake

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, in the year 1010, a king named Ly Thai To sailed up a wide river looking for the perfect spot to build a new capital. As his boat rounded a bend, he saw something amazing rising out of the water: a golden dragon, soaring straight up into the clouds! The king took it as a sign from the heavens and named his new city Thang Long, which means 'Ascending Dragon.' That city is the one we now call Hanoi.

Hanoi kept growing for a thousand years, tucked inside a loop of the Red River — which is actually where its name comes from: 'Ha Noi' means 'inside the rivers.' Right in the heart of the city is the Old Quarter, a maze of narrow streets where merchants once sold just one thing per street: silk on one, silver on another, paper lanterns on a third. Walk there today, and many streets still sell exactly what they did hundreds of years ago!

Hanoi's most magical legend belongs to a lake called Hoan Kiem. The story goes that heaven's Dragon King once lent a magic sword to a Vietnamese hero named Le Loi, so he could drive invaders out of the land. Le Loi won, and years later, while boating on this very lake, a giant golden turtle rose out of the water. It grabbed the sword right off his belt and dove back down to return it to the Dragon King — which is why the lake is called Hoan Kiem, 'Lake of the Returned Sword.'

Step into Hanoi today and old and new still dance together. At night in the Old Quarter, you can watch performers stand chest-deep behind a bamboo screen, making wooden puppets glide and splash across the water like something alive — an art called water puppetry, born centuries ago in flooded rice paddies. A thousand years after that golden dragon rose from the river, Hanoi is still full of wonders like these.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Hoan Kiem Lake

The legendary lake where a giant turtle is said to have taken back a magic sword. A bright red bridge called The Huc leads to a tiny temple island in the middle, and locals still say a real giant turtle once lived in its waters.

📍 Temple of Literature

Vietnam's first university, built in 1070. Rows of stone turtles carry tablets carved with the names of the country's best scholars — touching a turtle's head is even said to bring good luck on exams!

📍 Old Quarter (36 Streets)

A thousand-year-old maze of streets, each once named for the single craft sold there. Wander past Silver Street and Paper Street, and you can still find shops selling those very things today.

📍 One Pillar Pagoda

A tiny wooden temple balanced on a single stone pillar, built to look like a lotus flower blooming from a pond. Legend says a king built it after dreaming of a goddess who handed him a baby son while sitting on a lotus.
