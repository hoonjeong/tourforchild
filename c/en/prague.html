🏰 Prague

The fairy-tale 'City of a Hundred Spires,' guarded by a castle, a magic clock, and a clay giant's legend

📖 The Story

High above a curling river in the heart of Europe sits a city so full of towers and spires that people call it 'the City of a Hundred Spires.' Its name is Prague, and it has been a busy, magical town for over a thousand years. Long ago, Czech kings ruled from a hill above the water, and the whole city grew up around their castle like a fairy tale come true.

About 700 years ago, a king named Charles IV loved Prague so much he decided to make it one of the greatest cities in Europe. He built a beautiful stone bridge across the river, filled it with statues, and even opened one of the very first universities in this part of the world. People still cross his bridge and study at his university today!

In the middle of Prague's Old Town, a giant clock has been ticking since the year 1410 — and it still works! Every single hour, tiny wooden windows pop open, and twelve apostles parade past while a skeleton rings a bell. It's one of the oldest astronomical clocks in the whole world, and it still tells the time of the sun, the moon, and the stars, just like it did 600 years ago.

But Prague hides an even stranger story. Long ago in its old Jewish Quarter, people whispered about a rabbi who shaped a giant out of river clay and brought it to life with a magic word to protect the city. They called him the Golem. Nobody knows if the tale is completely true — but in Prague, castles, clocks, and clay giants all somehow feel like they could be real.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Prague Castle

This hilltop castle has been home to kings, emperors, and presidents for over a thousand years. It's so huge that Guinness World Records calls it the largest ancient castle complex on Earth. Look up and you'll spot St. Vitus Cathedral's spires poking into the clouds.

📍 Charles Bridge

Built by King Charles IV almost 700 years ago, this stone bridge is lined with 30 statues of saints. Musicians and artists still fill it every day, just like they filled the marketplaces of old Prague.

📍 Astronomical Clock (Old Town Square)

This clock has told the time since 1410 — that's over 600 years! Stand below it on the hour to watch tiny wooden apostles parade past the windows while a skeleton rings its bell.

📍 Golden Lane (Prague Castle)

Tucked inside Prague Castle's walls, this tiny street of colorful little houses once housed castle guards, goldsmiths, and — legend says — alchemists trying to turn metal into gold.
