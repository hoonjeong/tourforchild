🏔️ Interlaken

The little town squeezed between two lakes of different colors, launchpad into the high Alps

📖 The Story

Picture a town squeezed like a sandwich between two lakes — one lake glowing turquoise, the other a deep, milky blue-green. That town is Interlaken, and its very name means 'between the lakes' in Latin. Both lakes are fed by melting glaciers high in the Alps, and tiny bits of ground-up rock called 'glacier flour' float in the water, painting each lake its own magical color.

Almost 900 years ago, monks built a quiet abbey right here, and for a long time Interlaken was a peaceful little village most people had never heard of. Then, about 200 years ago, poets and travelers — including the famous poet Lord Byron — visited, fell in love with the towering mountain views, and wrote about them. Word spread, and Interlaken suddenly became the place everyone wanted to see the Alps from.

Right in the middle of town there's a big green meadow called the Höhematte — and it's not just for picnics. Every day, colorful paragliders drift down from the mountains like giant butterflies and land softly on the grass, dozens of times a day! From Interlaken you can also hop on a train that climbs and tunnels straight through a mountain to Jungfraujoch, the highest railway station in all of Europe, nicknamed the 'Top of Europe.'

Hidden inside another mountain nearby are the Trümmelbach Falls — ten thundering waterfalls made of glacier meltwater, roaring through tunnels and caves you can actually walk inside. Between glowing lakes, floating paragliders, and waterfalls hiding inside mountains, Interlaken feels like nature's own playground, tucked snugly between two lakes and surrounded by giant peaks.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1'Interlaken' is Latin for 'between the lakes' — the town really does sit right between Lake Thun and Lake Brienz.
  • 2The two lakes are different colors even though they're right next to each other, because glaciers grind rock into a fine powder called 'glacier flour' that floats in the meltwater.
  • 3Right in the center of town is a big meadow called the Höhematte, where colorful paragliders land almost every day of the year — sometimes over 100 times in a single day!
  • 4The train to Jungfraujoch climbs to 3,454 meters, making it the highest railway station in Europe — people nicknamed it the 'Top of Europe.'
  • 5Workers spent 16 years digging a tunnel through solid rock to build that railway, finally finishing in 1912.
  • 6Trümmelbach Falls, hidden inside a nearby mountain, is the only glacier waterfall in the Alps you can walk right inside to see — and another peak, the Schilthorn, has a revolving restaurant that once played a villain's hideout in a James Bond movie.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Höhematte Meadow

A wide green meadow right in the middle of town. Look up and you might see colorful paragliders drifting down from the mountains to land softly on the grass. You could sit on a bench and watch the sky all day.

📍 Jungfraujoch – Top of Europe

Reached by a special train that climbs and tunnels through the mountain, this is the highest railway station in all of Europe. Icy, snow-covered Alpine peaks stretch out forever outside the window, and there's even a glittering ice cave inside the station.

📍 Trümmelbach Falls

Ten roaring waterfalls hidden inside a mountain. An elevator and stairways take you right up close to glacier meltwater crashing through narrow tunnels — the roar is so loud it rumbles in your ears!

📍 Schilthorn (Piz Gloria)

A jagged peak you reach by cable car, home to the revolving restaurant Piz Gloria — once used as a villain's secret base in a James Bond movie. On a clear day you can see three famous peaks, the Eiger, Mönch, and Jungfrau, all at once.
