♨️ Budapest

Three towns that joined hands to become the Pearl of the Danube

📖 The Story

Long ago, there wasn't just one city called Budapest — there were three! On one side of the wide Danube River sat Buda, a hilltop town with a royal castle. On the other side sat Pest, a busy flat town full of markets. A third little town, Óbuda, sat just next to Buda. Back then there wasn't a single bridge, so people crossed the river by boat.

Then, almost 200 years ago, a Hungarian count named István Széchenyi decided the cities needed a real bridge. He built the Chain Bridge — the very first permanent bridge over the Danube — with two huge stone lions guarding each end. Legend says the sculptor forgot to carve tongues in the lions' mouths, and was so embarrassed he jumped into the river! Nobody actually knows if that part is true.

But the real secret of this city bubbles up from underground. Beneath Budapest lie more than a hundred natural hot springs, warm water rising up from deep inside the earth. Ancient Romans built baths here two thousand years ago, and later the Ottomans built beautiful domed bathhouses too. Today you can still swim outdoors in steaming warm water — even while snow falls around you!

In 1873, Buda, Óbuda, and Pest finally joined hands and became one city: Budapest. Now, fairy-tale white towers called the Fisherman's Bastion look out over the river, and a giant Parliament building holds Hungary's thousand-year-old crown. At night the whole riverside glows with golden light, which is why people call Budapest 'the Pearl of the Danube.'

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Budapest used to be three separate towns — Buda, Óbuda, and Pest — until they joined together in 1873.
  • 2More than 100 natural hot springs bubble up under the city, making Budapest one of the few capital cities in the world built on thermal water.
  • 3The stone lions guarding the Chain Bridge are famous for supposedly having no tongues — a fun legend, even if it isn't quite true.
  • 4The Fisherman's Bastion has seven fairy-tale towers, one for each of the seven Magyar tribes who first settled Hungary.
  • 5The Hungarian Parliament Building is one of the largest parliament buildings on Earth, and it guards Hungary's thousand-year-old royal crown.
  • 6At Széchenyi Baths, you can swim in a giant outdoor thermal pool even in the middle of winter, with steam rising around falling snow.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Chain Bridge

The very first permanent bridge to link Buda and Pest, finished in 1849. Two giant stone lions guard each end — see if you can spot whether they really have tongues!

📍 Fisherman's Bastion

White towers and archways that look straight out of a fairy tale, built for the view rather than for real battles. Its seven turrets stand for the seven tribes who first founded Hungary, and the view of the Danube from here is unforgettable.

📍 Széchenyi Thermal Bath

One of the largest thermal bath complexes in Europe, filled with steaming water straight from underground hot springs. Locals play chess on floating boards right in the warm outdoor pool — even when snow is falling!

📍 Hungarian Parliament Building

A huge, lacy stone palace on the riverbank, once one of the biggest parliament buildings in the world. Deep inside sits the thousand-year-old Holy Crown of Hungary, guarded day and night.
