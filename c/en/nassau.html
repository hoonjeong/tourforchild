🇧🇸 Bahamas

🐬 Nassau

Once a pirate hideout, now home to giant water slides, swimming pigs, and dolphins

📖 The Story

About 300 years ago, Nassau had almost no laws at all, and that made it the perfect hideout for pirates. Real, famous pirates — like the fearsome Blackbeard — sailed right into this harbor and called it home. For a few wild years, so many pirates ruled the town that people started calling it the 'Republic of Pirates.'

Eventually, the pirates were chased away, but the town kept building. Up on a hill, workers carved 65 steps straight down out of solid rock by hand — no machines at all — creating a stairway now called the Queen's Staircase. People still climb it today, brushing their hands along walls carved more than 200 years ago.

Much more recently, someone had a wild idea: build a resort around the legend of a lost underwater city called Atlantis. Now, giant water slides send riders zooming through clear tunnels that pass straight through a lagoon — and real sharks glide by on the other side of the glass.

But maybe the strangest wonder is out on a quiet nearby island, where wild pigs live right on the beach — and swim! When a boat arrives, the pigs paddle out through the waves to say hello, hoping for a snack. Nobody knows for certain how they got there, but everybody agrees: swimming pigs are about as surprising as the Caribbean gets.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Queen's Staircase

These 65 steps were carved straight out of solid rock by hand over 200 years ago. Run your hand along the walls and you're touching history carved without a single power tool.

📍 Pirates of Nassau Museum

Step inside to see how real pirates lived in Nassau's wild days as the "Republic of Pirates" — complete with a life-size replica pirate ship you can climb aboard.

📍 Atlantis Paradise Island

Named for the legendary lost city, this resort's water slides shoot riders through clear tunnels passing straight through a lagoon where real sharks glide by just beyond the glass.

📍 Pig Beach, Big Major Cay

On this uninhabited island, wild pigs live right on the sand — and paddle out to greet arriving boats, hoping visitors brought a snack.
