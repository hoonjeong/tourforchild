🧇 Brussels

Where a tiny boy became a hero and a giant atom touches the sky

📖 The Story

Welcome to Brussels, the capital of Belgium — and also the "capital of Europe," where leaders from many countries meet. But long before presidents arrived, Brussels was already famous for something much smaller: a tiny bronze boy, barely taller than a fire hydrant, who has become one of the most beloved statues on Earth.

His name is Manneken Pis, and legend says he once saved the whole city. Long ago, when enemies tried to blow up Brussels' walls, a clever little boy supposedly found the burning fuse and put it out the only way a boy could — by peeing on it! Whether the legend is true or not, Brussels loved the story so much that people still dress the little statue in tiny costumes, from superhero capes to soccer jerseys, hundreds of times a year.

Just a short walk away shines the Grand Place, a square so dazzling with gold trim and carved stone that people call it one of the most beautiful squares in the world. Long ago, rich trade guilds — bakers, boatmen, brewers — competed to build the fanciest guildhall here, and their gold decorations still sparkle in the sun today. It's no wonder Brussels also became famous for its rich chocolate and crispy waffles, treats sweet enough to match the square.

And then there's the Atomium, a giant silver structure that looks like it fell from outer space. Built in 1958, it's actually a model of a single iron atom — blown up 165 billion times its real size! Escalators and tunnels connect its huge shiny spheres, and from the top ball, you can see the whole city spread out below, tiny cars and rooftops and all.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Manneken Pis owns more than 1,000 tiny costumes, and volunteers dress him in a new outfit several times a week!
  • 2The real Manneken Pis statue is only about 60 cm tall — smaller than most kindergartners.
  • 3Grand Place is covered with an enormous 'flower carpet' made of hundreds of thousands of begonia flowers every two years.
  • 4Brussels calls itself the birthplace of the modern comic strip — giant painted murals of characters like Tintin cover walls all over downtown.
  • 5The praline, a chocolate filled with sweet cream, was invented in Brussels in 1912 — and Belgians still take their chocolate very seriously.
  • 6The Atomium's nine shiny spheres are each 18 meters wide, big enough that one of them is a restaurant with a view over the whole city.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Manneken Pis

This tiny bronze boy is the star of Brussels' funniest legend — the one who supposedly saved the city with clever thinking. Come see his latest costume; he might be wearing anything from a spacesuit to a chef's hat!

📍 Grand Place

One of the most beautiful squares in the world, ringed by golden guildhalls that rich merchants built to outshine each other centuries ago. At night the whole square glows like something out of a fairy tale.

📍 Atomium

A giant model of an iron atom, blown up 165 billion times and built for a World's Fair in 1958. Ride the escalators through its shiny silver spheres and look out over the whole city from the top.

📍 Comic Strip Route

Brussels loves comic strips so much that giant murals of characters climb up the sides of buildings all over downtown. Walk the Comic Strip Route and spot your favorites hiding on the walls.
