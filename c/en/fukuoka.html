🇯🇵 Japan

🍜 Fukuoka

The ancient port where Japan first opened its door to Asia

📖 The Story

Long before Tokyo was even a small village, a port town on the western tip of Japan called Hakata was already busy welcoming ships from China and Korea. For over two thousand years, boats loaded with silk, books, and new ideas sailed into its harbor, making this corner of Japan the country's front door to the rest of Asia.

One day long ago, a farmer digging in a rice paddy on tiny Shikanoshima Island found something strange glinting in the dirt: a small solid-gold seal, no bigger than a fingertip. Carved on it were tiny Chinese characters saying it was a gift from a Chinese emperor to the king of a land called 'Na' — sent all the way across the sea nearly 2,000 years ago!

Centuries later, in the 1200s, powerful Mongol warships crossed the sea to invade Japan — and Hakata was right in their path. The people of the town raced to build a long stone wall along the beach to block the invaders' horses, and twice, just as the ships approached, huge typhoons roared in and swept the fleets away. People later called those storms the 'kamikaze,' or divine wind.

Today Hakata's old fighting spirit and love of good food live on every single night, when hundreds of little food carts called 'yatai' pop open along the streets, filling the air with steam and laughter. This is also the very city where creamy, rich tonkotsu ramen was born — so a bowl here tastes like a taste of Fukuoka's whole, wonderful story.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Kushida Shrine

Hakata's own guardian shrine, lovingly called 'Okushida-san' by locals. Every July, teams hoist giant decorated floats called yamakasa onto their shoulders and race through the dawn streets right past this shrine. The tradition is nearly 800 years old and shows off Hakata's fearless, festive spirit.

📍 Fukuoka Castle Ruins (Maizuru Park)

This is where a huge castle once stood, built by the lord who ruled this region over 400 years ago. The buildings are gone now, but the massive stone walls and foundations remain, hinting at just how grand it once was. In spring, cherry blossoms turn the whole park pink.

📍 Shikanoshima Island (Kin-in Park)

A small island floating in Hakata Bay, connected to the mainland by a bridge. It was in a field right here that the famous solid-gold seal was dug up. Stand in Kin-in Park, built at the discovery spot, and imagine that special gift arriving across the sea 2,000 years ago.

📍 Genko Borui (Mongol Invasion Wall)

The remains of a long stone wall built along Hakata's beach to hold back Mongol invaders. Even though it was built in a hurry, it was so sturdy that pieces of it still stand more than 700 years later. Touch the stones and you can almost feel how urgent that day must have been.
