🏛️ Washington, D.C.

America's capital, belonging to no state, filled with soaring marble monuments

📖 The Story

Long ago, after America won its freedom from Britain, its leaders needed a brand-new capital that belonged to no single state. George Washington picked a marshy spot beside the Potomac River, and people named the city after him. A French-born designer, Pierre L'Enfant, drew grand wide avenues spreading out like the spokes of a wheel.

In 1814, during a war with Britain, British soldiers marched into the young capital and set fire to the president's house. When workers rebuilt it, they painted right over the scorched stone walls with bright white paint—and that's how the building got its new name: the White House!

Over the next 200 years, tall marble monuments rose across the city. A giant stone tower was built to honor the first president, but construction stopped for 20 years during the Civil War—look closely today and you can still spot where the marble color changes partway up! Later came the Lincoln Memorial, where a huge marble Lincoln sits in a chair, gazing over a long reflecting pool.

Today, the wide National Mall between the monuments is lined with free museums called the Smithsonian. Kids can stand beneath a real Wright brothers airplane or touch a rock brought back from the Moon. Every spring, thousands of cherry trees gifted by Japan burst into pink bloom, and every step feels like walking straight through American history.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Washington, D.C. isn't part of any state—it's its own special federal district.
  • 2The Washington Monument was built in two phases decades apart; look closely and you can see the marble change color partway up.
  • 3After British soldiers burned the president's house in 1814, it was repainted white to cover the scorch marks—giving us the 'White House.'
  • 4No building in Washington is allowed to be taller than the Capitol dome, so the skyline stays low and open.
  • 5In 1912, Japan gave Washington thousands of cherry blossom trees as a gift of friendship; they still bloom bright pink every spring.
  • 6The Smithsonian museums are all free, and one holds a real Apollo 11 capsule that flew to the Moon plus the actual Wright brothers airplane.

📍 Places to visit

📍 The White House

The president's home and office, rebuilt and painted white after British soldiers burned it in 1814. It has 132 rooms, a bowling alley, and even a chocolate-making kitchen!

📍 Lincoln Memorial

A giant marble Lincoln sits here, watching over a long reflecting pool. In 1963, Martin Luther King Jr. stood on its steps and gave his famous 'I Have a Dream' speech to a quarter-million people.

📍 Washington Monument

The tallest stone obelisk in the world, built to honor George Washington. Construction paused for 20 years during the Civil War—look closely and you can still spot where the marble color changes partway up!

📍 Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum

Home to real space and flying machines—the actual Wright brothers airplane and the Apollo 11 capsule that carried astronauts to the Moon. Best of all, it's completely free to visit!
