🎿 Innsbruck

Where the Alps rise right behind the rooftops—snowy peaks just 20 minutes from downtown

📖 The Story

High in the heart of the Austrian Alps sits a small city with big mountains right behind its rooftops. Its name, Innsbruck, means "bridge over the Inn"—the blue-green river that rushes right through downtown. Snowy peaks rise so close that on a clear day it looks like you could touch them from the old town square.

Hundreds of years ago, an emperor named Maximilian I fell in love with this mountain town and made it one of his favorite homes. To celebrate his wedding, he built a balcony above the main square and covered its roof with thousands of gleaming copper tiles dipped in real gold. From up there, he and his family could watch jousting tournaments and festivals in the square below—still glowing gold today.

Because the mountains crowd so close, Innsbruck grew into one of the world's great winter-sports cities. It hosted the Winter Olympics not just once but twice, and later welcomed young athletes from all over the globe for the Winter Youth Olympics. Skiers still soar off a giant ski jump on a hill right at the edge of downtown, landing to the roar of the crowd.

Here's the best part: hop into a cable car in the middle of the city, and in under twenty minutes you're standing on a mountain peak above the clouds. Down below, church spires and colorful rooftops look tiny, while up top there's crunchy snow even in summer. Few cities anywhere let you leap from cobblestone streets to alpine wilderness so fast.

📍 Places to visit

📍 The Golden Roof (Goldenes Dachl)

This gleaming balcony was built by Emperor Maximilian I to watch festivals in the square below. Its roof is covered in 2,657 fire-gilded copper tiles that still sparkle in the sun. It's the symbol of Innsbruck's old town.

📍 Nordkette Cable Car (Seegrubenbahn)

Step into a sleek cable car right in the city, and glide up over rooftops, forests, and cliffs to a station high above the clouds. In under 20 minutes you go from cobblestone streets to alpine snow and dizzying mountain views.

📍 Bergisel Ski Jump

This giant ramp on a hill at the edge of the city hosted Olympic ski jumping twice. Climb up (or take the elevator) to the top platform, and you'll feel your stomach flip just imagining the jumpers' flight over the rooftops below.

📍 Ambras Castle

A Renaissance archduke turned this hilltop castle into one of the world's first "wonder rooms," packed with armor, art, and oddities from around the globe. Wander its towers and gardens and imagine the strange treasures once gathered inside.
