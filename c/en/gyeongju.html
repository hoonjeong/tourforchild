👑 Gyeongju

Silla's 'thousand-year capital'—the first to unite Korea, a whole city that's a museum

📖 The Story

Long ago, several kingdoms on the Korean peninsula were competing with one another. The capital of one of them, called Silla, was Gyeongju. Silla started small but slowly grew stronger, and finally became the first kingdom ever to unite the Korean peninsula into one.

Here's the amazing part: Silla lasted almost 1,000 years! From 57 BC to 935 AD—very few kingdoms in the whole world lasted that long. All through those centuries, Gyeongju was a great city full of grand palaces, temples, and busy markets.

Gyeongju is special for another reason too. All over the city there are big hills poking up from the ground—but they're actually the tombs of Silla's kings and queens. Several sparkling golden crowns were found inside them, which is why Silla was called 'the kingdom of gold.'

Even today, Gyeongju still has Cheomseongdae, a tower for watching the stars, and Bulguksa Temple and Seokguram Grotto, both over a thousand years old. That's why people call Gyeongju a 'museum without a roof.' The whole city is like a living history book!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Cheomseongdae Observatory

A 1,400-year-old tower for watching the stars. Even the number of stones is said to stand for things like the days of a year. It's one of the oldest observatories in the world.

📍 Daereungwon Tomb Complex

A field of hill-like royal tombs. You can step right inside Cheonmachong tomb, where a painting of a flying horse and golden treasures await.

📍 Bulguksa Temple

A beautiful temple over a thousand years old. Look for its stone stairway-bridges and two famous but very different pagodas (Dabotap and Seokgatap).

📍 Seokguram Grotto

A great stone Buddha sits inside a mountain grotto, made to gaze at the sunrise over the East Sea. It's so finely made it's hard to believe it's 1,200 years old.
