🐪 Cairo

The city the Nile built, home of pyramids and pharaohs

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, beside the Nile River in Egypt, there grew a great city that would one day become Cairo. Every year the Nile flooded and left rich black soil along its banks, and people planted wheat and barley in it. Egyptians believed the Nile was a gift from the gods, and thanks to this river, a magnificent civilization thrived here for thousands of years.

Just outside the city stand giant stone pyramids, built as tombs for kings called pharaohs. The Great Pyramid of Giza was stacked from about 2.3 million blocks of stone, and it stayed the tallest building on Earth for almost 3,800 years! Beside it crouches the Great Sphinx, a huge statue with a lion's body and a human face, guarding the pyramids for thousands of years.

Egyptians believed a pharaoh became a god after death, so they wrapped his body carefully into a mummy and filled his tomb with golden treasures and picture-writing called hieroglyphs. No one could read these pictures for a very long time—until soldiers found a stone slab called the Rosetta Stone, carved with the same message in three scripts, and about 200 years ago its secret was finally unlocked!

Today Cairo is a busy modern city of nearly 20 million people. The treasures the pharaohs left behind are kept safe in the Egyptian Museum, including the dazzling gold mask of the boy king Tutankhamun. Wander into the old market Khan el-Khalili, and you'll still hear merchants calling out and smell spices in the air, just like people have for 600 years.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The Great Pyramid of Giza was the tallest building on Earth for almost 3,800 years, until England's Lincoln Cathedral was finally built taller.
  • 2The Great Pyramid is stacked from about 2.3 million stone blocks—some as heavy as an elephant!
  • 3The Great Sphinx was carved from one single giant block of limestone, so its body and face are all one piece of rock.
  • 4Egyptians didn't just mummify pharaohs—they mummified cats too! Cats were considered sacred animals.
  • 5Nobody could read hieroglyphs for over 1,400 years, until the Rosetta Stone helped crack the code in 1822.
  • 6The ancient Nile flooded so reliably each year that Egyptians used its pattern to create one of the world's first 365-day calendars.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Great Pyramid of Giza

Egypt's most famous pyramid, built as a tomb for the pharaoh Khufu. About 2.3 million blocks of stone were stacked entirely by hand. Even today, its size is jaw-dropping.

📍 Great Sphinx of Giza

A giant statue with a lion's body and a human face. It has guarded the pyramids like a silent protector for nearly 4,500 years.

📍 Egyptian Museum

A museum overflowing with pharaohs' treasures. Here you can see the golden mask and treasures of the boy king Tutankhamun with your very own eyes.

📍 Khan el-Khalili Bazaar

A bustling market over 600 years old. Its narrow lanes are packed with shops selling spices, lamps, and scarves—just walking through feels like an adventure.
