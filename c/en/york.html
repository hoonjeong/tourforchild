🏰 York

The English city with three names: Roman fortress, Viking port, Gothic cathedral town

📖 The Story

Two thousand years ago, Roman soldiers marched north into Britain and built a fortress where two rivers met. They called it Eboracum, and it became one of the most important bases in the whole Roman Empire. Emperors even visited — one, Constantine the Great, was declared ruler of Rome right there in the fortress.

Centuries later, fierce sailors called Vikings rowed up the river and took the city for themselves. They renamed it Jorvik and turned it into a busy trading town, packed with merchants selling silk, silver, and amber from lands far away. Long after the Vikings left, archaeologists dug deep under the streets and found an entire Viking neighborhood, perfectly preserved in the mud — houses, toys, even old shoes.

Then, in the Middle Ages, people built a ring of stone walls around the whole city — walls so strong that most of them still stand today. Right in the center, they raised a giant cathedral called York Minster, one of the largest Gothic churches in all of Northern Europe. It took more than 250 years to finish, and its enormous stained-glass windows glow like jewels when the sun shines through.

Today, the city still carries all its old names hidden inside it, layer upon layer like a cake. Walk down the narrow, crooked street called the Shambles, and you're strolling through a real medieval lane so magical it helped inspire Diagon Alley in Harry Potter. Roman, Viking, medieval — in York, you can visit three different worlds without ever leaving town!

📍 Places to visit

📍 York Minster

One of the largest Gothic cathedrals in Northern Europe, it took over 250 years to build. Sunlight pours through its huge stained-glass windows in every color of the rainbow. Down in its undercroft, you can still see the remains of the old Roman fortress underneath.

📍 JORVIK Viking Centre

Built right on top of the real site where archaeologists dug up a Viking neighborhood. Ride a little time-travel car through Jorvik as it looked in the year 960 — sounds, sights, and even smells included!

📍 The Shambles

A real, narrow, crooked medieval lane once lined with butcher shops. The upper floors of the buildings lean so close together that neighbors across the street could almost touch hands. It's famous for helping inspire Diagon Alley in Harry Potter.

📍 Clifford's Tower

A round, ancient stone tower standing on top of a mound. It's the last remaining piece of the castle Norman kings built to rule the city. Climb to the top and you can see all of York and its old walls spread out below.
