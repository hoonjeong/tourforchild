🕌 Delhi

The city rebuilt again and again for 1,000 years — and an iron pillar that refuses to rust

📖 The Story

Long ago, ancient Indians believed a legendary city called Indraprastha once stood exactly where Delhi stands today — home to the five hero brothers of India's great epic, the Mahabharata. Nobody knows for sure if that first city was real. But one thing is certain: for over a thousand years, people have kept building cities, again and again, right on this very spot.

Historians count at least seven or eight different cities that rose up on Delhi's land over time, one civilization stacking its walls and towers right on top of the last. Sultans, and later Mughal emperors, all wanted this same patch of ground, so every time an empire fell, a new one built its capital right here.

The most dazzling builder of all was Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan — yes, the very same emperor who built the Taj Mahal! He raised a massive red sandstone fortress called the Red Fort, with gates so grand a king's parade could pass beneath them, plus a giant mosque where thousands could pray together at once.

Today Delhi is India's capital, yet it still wears every layer of its past like rings on a tree. Ancient towers, Mughal domes, and modern government buildings all sit within a short drive of one another. Walking through Delhi feels like riding a time machine, hopping from one era to the next.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Red Fort (Lal Qila)

A massive fortress Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan built from red sandstone. Its walls tower 33 meters high, and every year India's Prime Minister raises the flag right above its gate on Independence Day.

📍 Qutub Minar & the Iron Pillar

The world's tallest brick minaret, over 800 years old, with five stories each carved in a different pattern — just looking up at it is dazzling. Right beside it stands the mysterious Iron Pillar, which has barely rusted in over 1,600 years.

📍 Humayun's Tomb

A beautiful garden tomb Empress Bega Begum built for Emperor Humayun. Its blend of red sandstone and white marble later inspired the design of the world-famous Taj Mahal.

📍 India Gate

A grand 42-meter arch built to remember soldiers who served their country. At night it lights up, becoming one of Delhi's most sparkling spots to visit.
