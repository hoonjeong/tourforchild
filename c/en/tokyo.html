🗼 Tokyo

From a sleepy fishing village to a dazzling city of the future

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, before Tokyo was even called Tokyo, it was just a small, quiet fishing village by a bay named Edo. Then in 1603, a powerful warlord named Tokugawa Ieyasu made Edo his home base and built a giant castle there. Soldiers, merchants, and craftspeople flooded in, and the sleepy village began to grow and grow.

Within just a hundred years, Edo had grown into one of the biggest cities on Earth—bigger than London or Paris at the time! With over a million people, it had bustling fish markets, kabuki theaters, and streets crowded with samurai, merchants, and street-food carts, all buzzing with life.

In 1868, a new emperor moved into the old shogun's castle and gave the city a brand-new name: Tokyo, meaning 'Eastern Capital.' But Tokyo's story wasn't always easy—a huge earthquake shook the city in 1923, and it was heavily damaged again during World War II. Each time, the people rebuilt it, bigger and shinier than before.

Today Tokyo is one of the most amazing cities on the planet, mixing ancient temples with sky-high towers, bullet trains, and robots. It has hosted the Olympics not once but twice, and every day millions of people cross its famous Shibuya intersection like a giant human wave. Somewhere underneath all those neon lights, the humble spirit of old Edo is still there.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Senso-ji Temple

Tokyo's oldest temple, first built in the year 628 after two fishermen found a tiny golden statue in the river. Walk through the giant red lantern of the Thunder Gate and down a lively street full of snacks to reach it.

📍 Shibuya Crossing

One of the busiest crossings on Earth—when the light turns green, up to 3,000 people flood across from every direction at once, like a human wave. Giant video screens tower overhead, watching the whole show.

📍 Tokyo Tower

Built in 1958 and inspired by the Eiffel Tower, this bright orange-and-white tower stands about 3 meters taller than its Paris cousin. Ride up to the observation deck and you can see almost all of Tokyo spread out below.

📍 Imperial Palace (former Edo Castle)

This huge park in the middle of the city sits on the very spot where Tokugawa Ieyasu built his castle back in 1603. Wide moats and stone walls still guard the grounds, where Japan's emperor lives today.
