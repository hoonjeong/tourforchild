🏜️ Petra

The rose-red city carved by hand — lost for centuries, found again

📖 The Story

Deep in the desert of Jordan, hidden between towering cliffs, sits a city unlike any other. Its buildings weren't built brick by brick — they were carved straight into rose-red rock. Long ago, a clever desert people called the Nabataeans made this place their home, and they called it Petra.

To reach Petra, you don't just walk in the front door. You squeeze through the Siq, a narrow, winding canyon more than a kilometer long, with rock walls soaring higher than a ten-story building on either side. Just when the canyon feels like it will never end, it opens — and suddenly, glowing pink in a sliver of sunlight, stands the Treasury, Petra's most famous building, carved from a single cliff from the top down.

The Nabataeans weren't just brilliant carvers — they were water wizards too. In a desert where rain barely ever falls, they built hidden channels, pipes, and giant tanks to catch every drop of rainwater and flash floods, and stored it for dry days. That clever trick let Petra grow rich as a resting stop for camel caravans carrying spices and incense across the desert.

But over time, earthquakes shook the cliffs and trade routes changed, and people slowly left. Petra sat quiet and hidden for hundreds of years, known only to local Bedouin families, until a curious explorer in disguise found his way through the Siq in 1812 and told the world. Today, Petra is one of the New Seven Wonders of the World — a whole city, waiting patiently in the rock, for anyone brave enough to walk through the Siq and find it.

📍 Places to visit

📍 The Siq

This narrow, winding canyon is the secret front door to Petra. Walk between cliff walls taller than a ten-story building for over a kilometer, watching the sliver of sky above grow narrower and narrower — until the Treasury suddenly appears.

📍 Al-Khazneh (The Treasury)

Petra's most famous carving glows pink and gold in the morning sun. Carved from the top down straight out of the cliff, it may once have held royal tombs or sacred treasures.

📍 The Royal Tombs

A row of grand tomb facades carved into a cliff, built for Nabataean kings and important families. Climb up for one of the best views over the whole valley of Petra.

📍 Ad-Deir (The Monastery)

Even bigger than the Treasury, this giant carved building sits high on the mountain, reached only by climbing about 800 rock-cut steps. Fewer visitors make the climb, so it feels like your own secret discovery.
