♨️ Bath

The English city built around a magic hot spring that never runs cold

📖 The Story

Long ago, in the misty hills of western England, legend says a prince named Bladud caught a terrible skin disease and was sent away from home. He became a poor swineherd, and one day noticed his pigs kept wallowing in a steaming, muddy puddle. When he tried the warm mud himself, his skin cleared up! Overjoyed, he built a town right on that magic warm spring — and folks say that town became Bath.

Real history begins with the Romans, who arrived about 2,000 years ago and found the same strange thing: hot water bubbling straight out of the ground, all on its own, with no fire needed. They believed a goddess named Sulis lived in the spring, so they built her a temple and a grand bathing hall, calling the whole place 'Aquae Sulis' — the waters of Sulis.

For hundreds of years afterward, Bath's magic pool was almost forgotten. Then, about 300 years ago, rich visitors rediscovered the healing water and turned Bath into the most fashionable spa town in the country. Elegant golden-stone houses curved around whole streets, like the famous Royal Crescent, and writers like Jane Austen strolled its squares and wrote it into their books.

Here's the wow: that same hot water is still flowing today, through the very same Roman pipes, into the very same pool. Every drop that bubbles up actually fell as rain on nearby hills about 10,000 years ago, then journeyed deep underground before rising back hot to greet you. Stand by the water in Bath, and you're touching rain older than almost anything else on Earth's surface.

📍 Places to visit

📍 The Roman Baths

Step down into the actual pool the Romans built almost 2,000 years ago, still filled with steaming green spring water. You can see the original stone floor, ancient lead pipes, and even coins tourists once tossed in for luck.

📍 Bath Abbey

This towering stone church stands right on the spot where an even older Saxon church once stood, next door to the ancient hot spring. Look up at its west front and you'll see stone angels carved climbing ladders straight up to heaven.

📍 Royal Crescent

Thirty grand houses curve together in one giant, perfect crescent, all built from the same golden Bath stone. It's one of the finest examples of Georgian architecture anywhere in the world, and Jane Austen loved to walk past it.

📍 Pulteney Bridge

This is one of only a handful of bridges in the whole world with little shops built all along both sides, so crossing it feels just like walking down a tiny street. Underneath, the River Avon tumbles over a wide, curved weir.
