⛰️ Zhangjiajie

Where 3,000 stone pillars float above the clouds—a real place stranger than the movies

📖 The Story

Deep in the mountains of southern China stands a forest of more than 3,000 stone pillars, all shooting straight up like giant fingers pointing at the sky. Some are as tall as a 65-story building. Over hundreds of millions of years, wind, rain, and tree roots wore away everything softer, leaving only these towering columns of hard stone behind.

When mist and clouds drift slowly between the pillars, the peaks seem to float right up in the air. This dreamlike sight inspired the floating 'Hallelujah Mountains' in the movie Avatar. After the film came out, one real pillar was even officially renamed 'Avatar Hallelujah Mountain.'

People have built amazing things here too. A glass bridge clings to a cliff, stretching as long as four soccer fields laid end to end and hanging 300 meters above the ground—crossing it feels like walking on air. There's also an elevator built right into the cliff face, whisking visitors up to the top in under two minutes.

Sometimes clouds wrap only around the middle of the pillars, leaving their tops and bottoms clear, so the mountains look like they're wearing a jade belt. Walking through this strange forest of stone giants feels like landing on another planet.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Yuanjiajie (Avatar Hallelujah Mountain)

This is the real place that inspired the floating mountains in Avatar. Come on a misty day, and the peaks truly look like they're drifting in the sky.

📍 Zhangjiajie Grand Canyon Glass Bridge

A bridge with a see-through glass floor, 430 meters long and 300 meters high—one of the longest, highest glass bridges on Earth. The whole way across, you can see straight down into the canyon below!

📍 Bailong Elevator

An elevator built right inside a cliff, standing 326 meters tall—one of the tallest outdoor elevators in the world. It zips you up to the mountaintop in less than two minutes.

📍 Tianmen Mountain (Heaven's Gate Cave)

This mountain has a giant hole clean through its middle, called Tianmen Cave, or 'Heaven's Gate.' You can also walk a glass skywalk clinging to the cliff and ride up a winding road with 99 hairpin turns.
