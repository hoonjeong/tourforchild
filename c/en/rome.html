🏛️ Rome

The Eternal City a legendary wolf-raised twin built—still buzzing 2,000 years later

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, legend says two baby twins, Romulus and Remus, were left all alone by a river. A mother wolf found them and raised them like her own cubs! When the twins grew up, Romulus decided to build a city on one of seven hills by the river. He named it after himself—Rome—and that was around 2,700 years ago.

Rome grew and grew, until it ruled an enormous empire stretching across three continents—from chilly Britain all the way to sunny Egypt. Roman engineers built roads so wide and sturdy that people still say, 'All roads lead to Rome.' They even invented a super-strong concrete, mixed with volcanic ash, that could hold up giant domes and bridges for thousands of years.

In the middle of the city stood the Colosseum, a colossal stone stadium where up to 50,000 people cheered for gladiators, chariot races, and even pretend sea battles—yes, they flooded the arena with real water and sailed in tiny ships! Beneath the arena floor, a maze of tunnels and elevators hid actors and animals, popping them up right in front of the roaring crowd.

Empires rise and empires fall, but Rome never really disappeared. Today you can stroll past 2,000-year-old ruins on your way to buy gelato, then toss a coin into the splashing Trevi Fountain and make a wish. That's why people call Rome 'the Eternal City'—it just keeps living on, layer after layer, story after story.

📍 Places to visit

📍 The Colosseum

This giant stone stadium once held up to 50,000 roaring fans watching gladiators and chariot races. Hidden tunnels and elevators beneath the floor could make lions and fighters pop up like a magic trick. Today you can walk right where ancient Romans once cheered.

📍 The Roman Forum

This was ancient Rome's busy downtown—part market, part courthouse, part town square. Emperors gave speeches here, and merchants sold everything from spices to silk. Now it's a beautiful field of ruins where you can imagine the crowds bustling by.

📍 The Pantheon

Built almost 1,900 years ago, this temple has the largest dome made of unreinforced concrete in the world. A round hole at the top, called the oculus, lets in sunlight and rain! Ancient Romans built it so well that it's still standing strong today.

📍 Trevi Fountain

This sparkling fountain is famous for one fun tradition: toss a coin over your shoulder into the water, and legend says you'll return to Rome one day. Every year, almost a million euros of coins are collected and given to charity.
