🌇 Kaohsiung

The harbor city where you walk into a dragon's mouth and out a tiger's

📖 The Story

Long ago, on the southern tip of Taiwan, a small fishing village grew up by the sea. People fenced off their harbor with bamboo and spent their days catching fish. The local Indigenous people called the place something like 'Takau,' and that old name became the seed of today's Kaohsiung.

Years passed, and the tiny fishing village kept growing into a real port town. Long ago, when Japan ruled Taiwan, officials thought 'Takau' sounded like a place name back in Japan, so they renamed the city 'Kaohsiung.' After that, ships from all over the world began crowding into its harbor to load and unload cargo.

Today Kaohsiung is Taiwan's biggest harbor city — and it's also turned itself into a giant playground of wonder. Old dusty warehouses along the water became a rainbow-colored art district, and one underground train station even has a swirling glass ceiling that glows brighter than a rainbow.

On top of that, wild monkeys hop along a mountain right in the middle of downtown, and giant dragon and tiger statues let you 'walk into their mouths' for good luck. Up on a nearby hill, a bronze Buddha as tall as a 30-story building quietly watches over the whole city. Kaohsiung mixes legend, art, and the sea into one big adventure.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Lotus Pond & Dragon and Tiger Pagodas

Two towers rise over a wide lotus pond—one shaped like a dragon's mouth, the other a tiger's. Legend says walking in through the dragon and out through the tiger washes away bad luck and leaves you with only the good kind.

📍 Cijin Island

A five-minute ferry ride whisks you to this long, narrow sandbar island. A white lighthouse and an old fort look out over the harbor, and its beach lets you feel real black sand under your feet.

📍 Pier-2 Art Center

Old dockside warehouses were transformed into a rainbow-bright art park. Giant robot sculptures and bike paths now fill a place that once only carried cargo—today it echoes with laughter instead.

📍 Formosa Boulevard Station (Dome of Light)

A colossal glass ceiling stretches over the metro station like an underground sky. It's the largest glass artwork on Earth, and standing beneath it feels like standing under a sky full of colorful, glowing stars.
