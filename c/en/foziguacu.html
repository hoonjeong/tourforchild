💦 Foz do Iguaçu

Where Brazil, Argentina, and Paraguay meet — a town where 275 waterfalls roar together

📖 The Story

In the south of Brazil, right where Brazil, Argentina, and Paraguay all touch, there's a town called Foz do Iguaçu. Its name means "the mouth of the Iguaçu River" — and that river is hiding one of the loudest, biggest surprises on Earth. Just outside town, the land suddenly drops away, and the whole river tumbles down in not one, not two, but about 275 waterfalls at once. Mist rises so high you can see rainbows arching over the jungle any time the sun peeks out.

Long before anyone built a town here, the Guaraní people who lived along this river told a legend about how the falls were born. They say a river serpent-god named Boi loved a beautiful young woman named Naipí, but Naipí loved a brave warrior named Tarobá instead. When the two lovers tried to escape together by canoe down the river, the furious serpent-god slashed the riverbed with one great blow, splitting it into a thousand pieces and creating a churning, roaring canyon. Naipí was swept over the edge and turned into a rock in the river; Tarobá became a palm tree on the bank, forever watching her from across the falls.

The real history is almost as wild. In 1541, a Spanish explorer named Cabeza de Vaca became the first European to stumble onto these falls while hacking through the jungle, and he was so stunned he named them after a saint. It took nearly 400 more years before Brazil and Argentina turned the area into national parks to protect it, and today it's a UNESCO World Heritage site, wilder and better guarded than ever.

Foz do Iguaçu is full of more wonders than just the falls. Raccoon-like coatis wander the park paths looking for snacks (don't feed them — they're wild!), toucans and hundreds of butterflies flit through the trees, and just up the river sits Itaipu, one of the biggest dams on the planet, turning all that rushing water into electricity for millions of homes. Stand on the walkway at the Devil's Throat, feel the spray on your face, and you'll understand why people say Iguaçu makes Niagara Falls look small.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Cataratas do Iguaçu (Falls Trail)

A walkway that hugs the cliffside so you can look straight across at nearly 275 waterfalls crashing down at once. Rainbows hang in the mist almost all day, and by the end you're standing close enough to feel the spray on your skin.

📍 Devil's Throat (Garganta do Diabo)

The wildest, loudest part of the whole falls — a U-shaped canyon where 14 waterfalls pour together into one thundering wall of water and mist. A long metal walkway takes you out over the river to stand right above the roar.

📍 Parque das Aves (Bird Park)

Right next to the falls, this park lets you walk through giant net-covered forests full of free-flying toucans, parrots, and flamingos. Some birds are so used to visitors they'll land right next to you for a closer look!

📍 Itaipu Dam

One of the largest hydroelectric dams on the planet, built where the Paraná River rushes between Brazil and Paraguay. It's so massive that its power plant alone can light up whole countries — you can even walk across the top and feel the ground hum.
