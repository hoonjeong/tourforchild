⚜️ Montreal

The island city with a whole other city hiding underground

📖 The Story

Long before any street or building stood here, the Mohawk and other Indigenous peoples lived on this leafy island in a great river. In 1535, a French explorer named Jacques Cartier climbed the big hill in the middle of the island and looked out at the endless forest below. He was so amazed that he named the hill 'Mont Royal'—Royal Mountain—and centuries later, the whole city took its name from that mountain: Montreal.

About a hundred years later, in 1642, a small group of French settlers built a tiny village right where the river curled around the island, calling it Ville-Marie. The island sat exactly where two rivers met, so canoes loaded with furs, traders, and adventurers passed through constantly. That tiny village grew and grew until it became one of the busiest, most important cities in all of North America.

Montreal's winters can be freezing and snowy, so long ago people came up with an amazing idea: build a whole city UNDER the city! Today, more than 30 kilometers of tunnels link subway stations, shops, movie theaters, and even universities underground, so people can walk everywhere across downtown without ever putting on a coat. It's called RESO, and it's the largest underground city on the planet.

Above ground, Montreal still hums with two languages, French and English, spoken side by side on every street corner. Fireworks light up the summer sky, jazz spills out of every open window during the festival, and church bells ring over cobblestone streets in the old part of town. Whether you're riding a bike along the river or climbing to the top of Mont Royal for a view of the whole city, Montreal always feels like it's throwing a party just for you.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Mont Royal, the big hill at the heart of the city, was long thought to be an old volcano—but scientists later discovered it never actually erupted at all!
  • 2Beneath the streets, 32+ km of tunnels called 'RESO' link metro stations, shops, and movie theaters—the largest underground city on Earth.
  • 3Montreal is built entirely on an island, surrounded by the mighty St. Lawrence River.
  • 4It's the second-largest French-speaking city in the world after Paris—many street signs and songs mix French and English.
  • 5Habitat 67, the strange stack of concrete boxes by the river, was designed by a 23-year-old university student as his final school project!

📍 Places to visit

📍 Mont Royal Park

The very hill Jacques Cartier climbed in 1535 and named 'Royal Mountain.' Hike to the top for a giant lookout over the whole city, then find the huge lit-up cross that glows at night.

📍 Notre-Dame Basilica

Step inside Old Montreal's most famous church and look up—the ceiling glows deep blue and gold, covered in thousands of tiny stars. It looks almost like stepping inside the night sky.

📍 Habitat 67

A wild stack of concrete boxes piled up like giant building blocks along the river. A 23-year-old architecture student designed it for the 1967 World's Fair, and people still live inside it today!

📍 Biosphère

A giant silver dome floating on an island in the river, built for the same World's Fair as Habitat 67. It once wore a clear plastic skin—until a fire burned it off, leaving the bare, beautiful skeleton you see today.
