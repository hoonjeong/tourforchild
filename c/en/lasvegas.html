🎰 Las Vegas

The quiet desert springs that turned into a city of lights bright enough to see from space

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, before there was a single glowing light, this place was just quiet, dry desert in the American Southwest called the Mojave Desert. Hundreds of years ago, Native American peoples such as the Paiute lived here, finding water hidden in springs beneath the sand. One day, Spanish traders traveling the Old Spanish Trail stumbled upon those surprising green, grassy springs bubbling up in the middle of the dry desert. They gave the spot a name: 'Las Vegas'—Spanish for 'the meadows.'

For a long time, Las Vegas was just a tiny, dusty stop where trains refueled with water on their way across the desert. In 1905, workers auctioned off little plots of land, and a small railroad town was born. Back then, nobody could have guessed that this sleepy desert town would one day become one of the most famous cities on Earth.

Everything changed in the 1930s. Nearby, thousands of workers built an enormous dam across the Colorado River—Hoover Dam—one of the biggest, boldest engineering projects ever attempted. It tamed the mighty river, made electricity, and brought water and jobs to the desert. Around the same time, Nevada made gambling legal, and glittering hotels and casinos began popping up along a road that would soon become known as 'the Strip.'

Today, Las Vegas glows so brightly with neon signs and lights that you can spot it from an airplane window—even from space! Giant fountains dance to music in front of hotels, a volcano erupts right on cue, and roller coasters loop through the sky above the street. What began as a few quiet desert springs grew into a dazzling city that never seems to sleep.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1'Las Vegas' is Spanish for 'the meadows'—named for real green springs bubbling in the desert long before any lights showed up.
  • 2Building nearby Hoover Dam used so much concrete that there was enough to pave a two-lane highway all the way across the United States.
  • 3The famous fountains at the Bellagio shoot water more than 140 meters (460 feet) high, timed perfectly to the beat of the music.
  • 4Las Vegas glows so brightly that astronauts looking down at Earth at night can actually spot its lights from space.
  • 5The Neon Museum is a 'boneyard' where more than 800 giant retired neon signs rest—including old cowboys and a genie's lamp.
  • 6The famous 'Welcome to Fabulous Las Vegas' sign has been glowing since 1959, making it more than 65 years old.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Fremont Street Experience

The original street where Las Vegas was born. It's now covered by a giant screen-canopy overhead, so at night a dazzling light show plays right above your head.

📍 Bellagio Fountains

Hundreds of fountains rise high above a huge lake in front of the hotel, dancing perfectly in time with music. At night, colorful lights make the water look like it's actually singing.

📍 Hoover Dam

A massive dam built in the 1930s to hold back the powerful Colorado River. It's the real hero that brought Las Vegas its water and electricity, and it's still an awe-inspiring feat of engineering today.

📍 The Neon Museum

The place where old neon signs from Las Vegas's earlier hotels and casinos come to retire and rest. Walk among giant cowboys and sparkling crowns and meet the city's earlier stories face to face.
