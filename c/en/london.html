🎡 London

The 2,000-year-old river town that burned down—and rose again, ravens and all

📖 The Story

Around 2,000 years ago, Roman soldiers crossed a wide river in southern Britain and built a small trading town they called Londinium. It sat right where ships could sail in from the sea, so goods, food, and people poured in from all over the Roman world. That little riverside town grew and grew—today we call it London.

For hundreds of years London kept growing, its narrow streets packed with wooden houses built right up against each other. Then one night in September 1666, a spark in a bakery on Pudding Lane grew into a monster: the Great Fire of London. It roared through the city for four whole days, gobbling up thousands of homes—yet almost everyone escaped, and only a handful of people were lost.

Out of the ashes, Londoners rebuilt bigger and grander than before. A brilliant architect named Christopher Wren designed a magnificent new cathedral with a dome so huge it still rules the skyline today. Brick and stone replaced wood, so the next fire could never spread the same way again.

Now ancient and modern stand side by side all over London. A thousand-year-old fortress guards royal jewels, a giant bell booms the hour over Parliament, and double-decker buses zoom past buildings older than most countries. Walk down any street, and you're really walking through 2,000 years of stories all at once.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Tower of London

This thousand-year-old fortress has been a royal palace, a prison, and now guards the dazzling Crown Jewels. Look for the six famous ravens strutting the grounds—and the Beefeater guards in their bright red uniforms.

📍 Elizabeth Tower (Big Ben)

This clock tower stands beside the Houses of Parliament, where England's laws are made. Its giant bell, nicknamed Big Ben, has been booming out the hour since 1859—you can hear it ring across the whole city.

📍 St Paul's Cathedral

Rebuilt after the Great Fire, this cathedral's giant dome was once the tallest point in London. Whisper into the wall of its Whispering Gallery, and someone far across the dome can hear you perfectly!

📍 Buckingham Palace

This is where the King lives and works today. Watch the Changing of the Guard out front, and if the royal flag is flying, it means the King is home!
