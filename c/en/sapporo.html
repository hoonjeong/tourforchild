🇯🇵 Japan

❄️ Sapporo

Japan's young snow capital, drawn from scratch on a blank map

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, the northern island of Hokkaido was covered in deep forests and wild rivers, and only the Ainu people called this land home. They named a spot where a river ran wide and clear 'Sat poro pet'—a dry, big river. Long after, when settlers built a brand-new city right there, they picked that very name for it: Sapporo.

Unlike Japan's ancient capitals such as Kyoto or Nara, Sapporo was born on a blank map. About 150 years ago, planners drew arrow-straight streets in a perfect grid, like graph paper, and even invited an American teacher named William Clark to help build schools and farms. Before he sailed home, he told his students, 'Boys, be ambitious!'—words Sapporo still remembers today.

Every winter, Sapporo turns into a wonderland of snow. So much snow falls that in 1950, a group of students piled it into six giant snow statues in a park downtown. People loved it so much that this small snow pile grew into the world-famous Sapporo Snow Festival, with sculptures as tall as buildings.

Today Sapporo is Japan's fifth-largest city, but it still remembers its pioneer days. You can hear the same clock tower bell settlers once heard, sip fizzy drinks from Japan's very first beer brewery, and watch millions of snowflakes glitter under festival lights. It's a city built by dreamers—one straight street, and one snowflake, at a time.

  • 2Downtown Sapporo is laid out in a neat grid—so tidy that you can find a spot just by its numbers instead of a street name.
  • 3In some winters, Sapporo can pile up more than 5 meters (16+ feet) of snow—among the snowiest big cities on Earth!
  • 4The Sapporo Snow Festival began in 1950 with just six snow statues built by local students—now over two million visitors come to see the giant sculptures every year.
  • 5Sapporo Beer, Japan's oldest brewery, still uses a red star—Hokkaido's pioneer symbol—as its logo.
  • 6American teacher William Clark's parting words, 'Boys, be ambitious!' became one of Japan's most famous quotes, and his statue still watches over the city.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Sapporo Clock Tower

Built in 1878 as a school hall, it's now the symbol of the whole city. The very same bell still rings out today, just as it did back then.

📍 Odori Park

A long, narrow park that slices right through the city center. In winter it fills with the Snow Festival's giant snow sculptures, and in summer with bright, colorful flowers.

📍 Sapporo Beer Museum

An old red-brick brewery building. It's Japan's only beer museum, and beer has been fizzing away here since 1876.

📍 Mt. Moiwa Ropeway

Ride a cable car up the mountain, and you'll look down on sparkling Sapporo—a view ranked among Japan's three best night views.
