🕊️ Hiroshima

The city that turned its saddest day into the world's biggest wish for peace

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, Hiroshima grew up beside six rivers that spread out like fingers into the sea, so people nicknamed it the 'City of Water.' Boats glided under more than a hundred bridges, and a proud castle watched over the busy streets. It was a cheerful town full of markets, gardens, and children playing along the riverbanks.

But one day, during a terrible war, something awful happened — a single bomb fell from the sky and hurt the city more than almost any other place on Earth has ever been hurt. Nearly everything was gone in a flash, and for a while people wondered if anything could ever grow there again.

Yet the people of Hiroshima did something brave: instead of staying sad forever, they decided their city would become a messenger for peace. They planted thousands of trees, built a beautiful Peace Park, and kept one broken building standing just as it was — so nobody would forget how important peace is. A little girl named Sadako folded paper cranes hoping to get well, and now children all over the world fold cranes and send them to Hiroshima to wish for peace.

Today Hiroshima is green, lively, and full of hope again. Boats still cross the water, a giant orange gate seems to float on the sea nearby, and friendly deer wander a magical island close by. It's a city that turned its saddest day into the world's biggest wish for peace.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Hiroshima is nicknamed the 'City of Water' because six rivers spread through it, crossed by more than 100 bridges.
  • 2One bombed building was kept standing exactly as it was — the Atomic Bomb Dome — and it's now a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a symbol of peace.
  • 3A little girl named Sadako folded paper cranes wishing to get well. Today, over 10 million paper cranes are sent to Hiroshima from around the world every year.
  • 4Nearby Miyajima island has a giant orange gate that seems to float on the sea at high tide — it's one of Japan's 'Three Most Scenic Views.'
  • 5Gentle wild deer wander freely around Miyajima; they're considered sacred messengers and happily pose for photos.
  • 6Hiroshima invented its own style of okonomiyaki — a savory pancake stacked with cabbage, noodles, and egg — first made cheaply to fill hungry bellies after the war.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Peace Memorial Park & Atomic Bomb Dome

This green park sits exactly where the city was hurt worst, and the broken dome nearby was kept just as it was that day. Kids visit to fold paper cranes and hang them near the Children's Peace Monument, adding their own wish for a peaceful world.

📍 Itsukushima Shrine (Miyajima's Floating Torii)

At high tide, this giant orange gate looks like it's floating right on the sea — at low tide, you can walk out and touch it! It's one of Japan's most famous views, and gentle deer roam the island all around it.

📍 Hiroshima Castle

Nicknamed 'Carp Castle,' this black castle once watched over the busy river town. It was rebuilt after the war using old photos and drawings, so today kids can climb inside and imagine what life was like when samurai walked these grounds.

📍 Okonomimura (Okonomiyaki Food Hall)

This whole building is packed with tiny okonomiyaki grills where cooks flip savory pancakes right in front of you. Hiroshima-style stacks cabbage, noodles, and egg high — watching it sizzle is half the fun!
