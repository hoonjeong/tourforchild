🏜️ Grand Canyon

A river spent six million years carving Earth's grandest canyon

📖 The Story

Long before it had a name, a mighty river began carving its way through the high desert of northern Arizona. Drop by drop, year after year, the Colorado River sliced deeper and deeper into the rock. It took millions of years, but the river never stopped — and slowly, an enormous canyon began to open up in the earth.

Today that canyon is almost too big to believe. It stretches 277 miles long, gapes up to 18 miles wide, and plunges more than a mile straight down. Stand at the edge and the far wall looks impossibly far away — some say it's like staring across a whole ocean made of rock and sky.

The walls themselves are like a giant history book stacked in stripes of red, orange, and gray. Each layer was laid down at a different time, and the oldest rocks at the very bottom are almost 2 billion years old — some of the oldest rock ever found on our planet. For thousands of years before anyone wrote any of this down, Native peoples such as the Havasupai, Hualapai, Navajo, and Hopi made their homes in and around the canyon, and some still do today.

Now people come from all over the world just to peek over the rim, hike down winding trails, or even walk out onto a glass bridge that seems to float in thin air. Condors with wings as wide as a car soar on the warm air rising from the canyon below. It's a place that reminds everyone how patient, powerful, and astonishing plain old water and time can be.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1The Grand Canyon is 277 miles (446 km) long, up to 18 miles (29 km) wide, and over a mile (1.6 km) deep — big enough to fit several mountains inside it.
  • 2The Colorado River carved this whole canyon over about 6 million years, using nothing but flowing water, sand, and rock.
  • 3The oldest rocks at the very bottom are almost 2 billion years old — nearly half the age of Earth itself!
  • 4The Havasupai people still live inside the canyon, near a hidden waterfall so blue-green it looks painted.
  • 5California condors, the biggest flying birds in North America with wings nearly 10 feet (3 m) wide, glide over the canyon using rising warm air.
  • 6At Grand Canyon West, you can walk out onto a horseshoe-shaped glass bridge called the Skywalk, floating 4,000 feet above the canyon floor.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Mather Point (South Rim)

This famous overlook is many visitors' very first view of the canyon — and it never fails to make people gasp. From here you can see layer upon layer of colorful rock stretching for miles, with the Colorado River glinting far below.

📍 Bright Angel Trail

This zig-zagging trail winds from the rim all the way down toward the river, dropping through millions of years of rock in just a few hours of walking. Mule trains still carry riders and supplies down the very same switchbacks used over a century ago.

📍 Colorado River & Phantom Ranch

Down at the very bottom of the canyon runs the mighty Colorado River — the very river that carved this whole place, drop by drop, over millions of years. Near here sits tiny Phantom Ranch, one of the only places in the U.S. you can only reach by foot, mule, or raft.

📍 Grand Canyon Skywalk

On the western edge of the canyon, a horseshoe-shaped glass bridge juts out over open air. Walk to its edge and you're standing on nothing but glass, staring straight down 4,000 feet to the canyon floor below.
