🏯 Beijing

The walled city where emperors lived like Sons of Heaven, guarded by a stone dragon of a wall that never ends

📖 The Story

Long ago, Beijing was the beating capital of Chinese emperors for more than five hundred years. Right in the very center of the city stood an enormous palace called the Forbidden City. Its walls were so tall and its gates so heavily guarded that ordinary people were never, ever allowed inside. Only the emperor—called the 'Son of Heaven'—and his family and servants could live and walk within those red walls.

Here's a wonderful legend: the Forbidden City is said to have exactly 9,999 and a half rooms. Why not a nice round 10,000? Because in old Chinese belief, only the Jade Emperor's palace in heaven could have a full 10,000 rooms—so the earthly emperor's home had to be just a little smaller, out of respect. Golden dragons curled along the rooftops, and every roof tile gleamed a special yellow, a color only the emperor was allowed to use.

But Beijing hides another jaw-dropping wonder just north of the city: a colossal stone wall that snakes over mountain after mountain, farther than the eye can see. Emperors and kings kept building and rebuilding this wall for more than two thousand years to guard their land from invaders. Workers even mixed sticky rice into the mortar to make some sections stronger—and it worked so well those walls are still standing today!

Today, the Forbidden City has become the Palace Museum, and any kid in the world can walk through gates that once turned everyone away. A short trip north, you can climb the Great Wall yourself, watching it curl over the hilltops like a sleeping stone dragon. Standing there, you can almost feel a thousand years of history rising up around you.

📍 Places to visit

📍 The Forbidden City

This is the very palace with the legendary 9,999-and-a-half rooms, home to emperors for nearly 500 years. Wander through its golden-roofed halls and giant courtyards, and imagine being the only kid ever allowed past those red gates.

📍 The Great Wall (Mutianyu)

This is one of the best-preserved stretches of the Great Wall, snaking over green mountains as far as you can see. You can climb its steep stone steps and stand where soldiers once watched for enemies two thousand years ago.

📍 Temple of Heaven

Emperors came here once a year to pray to the sky for a good harvest for everyone in the land. Its round main hall was built entirely without a single nail—just wood pieces locked together like a giant puzzle.

📍 Summer Palace

A shimmering lake and a hilltop palace where emperors escaped the summer heat to relax and stroll. A long covered walkway winds along the water, painted with thousands of tiny storybook scenes.
