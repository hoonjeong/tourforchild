🇩🇪 Germany

🏙️ Frankfurt

The crowning city of kings that turned itself into glittering 'Mainhattan'

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, a tribe called the Franks discovered a shallow spot where they could cross the river Main on foot — a 'ford.' They built a settlement right there and named it Franconofurd, which means 'the Franks' ford.' Over time, the name became shorter and easier to say: Frankfurt.

The little river-crossing town grew important fast. For more than 500 years, starting in the 1300s, German kings and emperors traveled to Frankfurt to be chosen and crowned right in its great cathedral, in the very heart of the city. Bells rang, trumpets blared, and Frankfurt's town hall — a building called the Römer, with three pointy roofs in a row — became the place where new rulers celebrated with a giant feast.

But this old, beautiful town nearly vanished forever. During World War II, bombs fell on Frankfurt and destroyed almost all of its ancient, colorful, crooked houses. For decades afterward, people rebuilt what they could. Then, not so long ago, city planners tried something amazing: using centuries-old paintings and photographs like puzzle pieces, they rebuilt a whole neighborhood of crooked medieval houses right next to the Römer — finishing the job only in 2018!

Today, Frankfurt is a city of two faces at once. Walk one way and you're back among timber-framed houses from hundreds of years ago. Turn around, and shiny glass skyscrapers tower overhead — so many that people nicknamed the city 'Mainhattan,' mixing the river Main with Manhattan. Old and new, crowned kings and stock-market towers, all standing together on the same riverbank.

📍 Places to visit

📍 The Römer (Frankfurt's Old City Hall)

This city hall's three pointy gables have watched over Frankfurt's main square for centuries. Inside is the Kaisersaal, the 'Emperor's Hall,' where new rulers once celebrated with a giant feast after their coronation.

📍 Frankfurt Cathedral (Kaiserdom)

For centuries, this tall red-sandstone church was where German kings were elected and crowned emperor. Climb its tower today and you can see the whole city — old timber houses on one side, glass skyscrapers on the other.

📍 Goethe House

This is the very house where the famous writer Johann Wolfgang von Goethe was born and grew up. You can still walk through the rooms and see the desk where he began writing some of his first stories.

📍 Main Tower

One of the tallest skyscrapers in Frankfurt's 'Mainhattan' skyline, with an observation deck near the top. Look down and you'll spot the old crooked rooftops of the Römer standing right beside the glass towers.
