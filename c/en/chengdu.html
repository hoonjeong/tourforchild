🐼 Chengdu

Home of giant pandas and a 2,000-year-old river trick that still works today

📖 The Story

In the middle of China sits a green, misty city called Chengdu — nicknamed the 'Land of Abundance' because its soil never runs thin and its rivers never run dry. It's most famous today as the hometown of giant pandas, but long before pandas made it famous, Chengdu was already keeping an amazing secret hidden underground.

More than 2,000 years ago, a river kept flooding Chengdu's fields every summer and drying them out every winter. So a clever engineer named Li Bing had an idea: instead of building a wall to block the river, he split it into two channels using stone and bamboo — one for floodwater, one for farmland. It worked so well that the same system, called Dujiangyan, is still watering Chengdu's fields today, over 2,260 years later — with no big dam needed at all!

Chengdu holds another surprise. Under a quiet neighborhood, workers once dug up a tiny gold ornament thinner than a coin, showing a sun with four birds flying around it. It was made about 3,000 years ago by a mysterious civilization, and nobody is completely sure what it meant — maybe the sun traveling across the sky, carried by magical birds. Today that little gold sun is Chengdu's most famous symbol.

These days, Chengdu is best loved for its giant pandas, who lounge around eating bamboo all day at a huge panda park in the hills. Locals relax the same way — sipping tea for hours in outdoor teahouses and slurping fiery, tongue-tingling hot pot. Between ancient rivers, golden birds, and sleepy pandas, Chengdu has always known how to take it easy.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding

This green hillside park is home to dozens of giant pandas, from wobbly newborn cubs to lazy grown-ups munching bamboo all day. It's the best place in the world to see Chengdu's favorite animal up close.

📍 Dujiangyan Irrigation System

Built over 2,260 years ago, this clever water system still splits the river to stop floods and water farms — without a single big dam. Standing beside it, you can watch the ancient trick still working today.

📍 Jinsha Site Museum

This museum sits right on top of the spot where the tiny gold sun-and-birds ornament was dug out of the ground. Walk through and see ivory, jade, and gold treasures left by a mysterious 3,000-year-old civilization.

📍 Wuhou Shrine

This peaceful shrine honors Zhuge Liang, the clever strategist from the Three Kingdoms story, and the kings of the ancient Shu kingdom that once ruled from Chengdu. Its red walls and bamboo paths make it one of the most famous history spots in the city.
