🌮 Mexico City

A floating Aztec capital still sleeping right beneath a modern megacity

📖 The Story

Long, long ago, wandering people called the Aztecs were searching for a sign. Their gods had told them: build your city where you see an eagle perched on a cactus, eating a snake. One day, on a swampy little island in the middle of a lake, they finally saw it! So right there, in the middle of the water, they began building a city called Tenochtitlan.

Building on water didn't stop them—it made them clever. They wove reed rafts, piled them with mud and plants, and grew crops on floating gardens called chinampas. Canals crisscrossed the city like watery streets, and boats glided everywhere instead of carts. By the 1500s, Tenochtitlan was bigger than Paris or London, with towering temples and a bustling market that amazed everyone who saw it.

Then came big changes, and eventually the lake was drained and a new city was built right on top of the old one, stone by stone. For hundreds of years, people forgot exactly where the great Aztec temple once stood—until 1978, when electrical workers digging near the main square hit something huge: a giant carved stone disc, part of the buried Templo Mayor.

Today, Mexico City sits directly on top of ancient Tenochtitlan. Walk through the main square and you can peek down into excavated Aztec ruins right next to a towering cathedral. And if you ride a colorful boat through the canals of Xochimilco, you're floating on one of the last pieces of the Aztecs' amazing water city.

📍 Places to visit

📍 The Zócalo

One of the largest public squares in the world, and the very heart of the city since Aztec times. Aztec temples once stood here, and today a giant Mexican flag flies where their sacred center used to be.

📍 Templo Mayor

The excavated ruins of the Aztecs' greatest temple, hidden under the city for centuries until workers found it by accident in 1978. Twin shrines once topped it, and today you can walk right past stone serpent carvings from that lost world.

📍 Xochimilco Canals

The last living piece of the Aztecs' floating gardens, where farmers still grow flowers and vegetables on man-made islands. Ride a brightly painted trajinera boat down the canals, just as families did hundreds of years ago.

📍 Chapultepec Castle

A hilltop that Aztec rulers once used, later crowned with a grand castle—the only royal castle in North America. Climb up for sweeping views over the whole valley where Tenochtitlan once floated.
