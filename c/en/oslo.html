🎿 Oslo

The Viking city where forest meets fjord — and the world's Peace Prize is given away

📖 The Story

At the very top of a long, calm fjord, surrounded by pine forest and rocky hills, sits Oslo, the capital of Norway. People have lived here for more than a thousand years, ever since Viking families built wooden houses and busy harbors along this water. Even today, ships glide in and out of the fjord just as they did back then.

Some Vikings loved their ships so much that when a great chief or queen died, her ship was buried with her, tucked into a mound of earth like a giant treasure box, filled with beautifully carved wood, sleds, and even a fancy cart.

For over a thousand years that ship slept quietly under a grassy hill, until farmers digging nearby in the early 1900s hit something hard and wooden. It was the Oseberg ship! It was so well preserved in the thick blue clay that you can still see the swirling dragon carvings on its bow today.

Oslo still loves that mix of old and new adventure. Kids here can hop off a tram and ski straight into deep forest in winter, while down at the harbor, sturdy explorer ships once sailed off toward the North and South Poles. And every December, world leaders travel to Oslo's City Hall to hand someone the Nobel Peace Prize, for making the world a little kinder, just like this city always has.

🤩 Did you know?

  • 1Oslo is over 1,000 years old, one of the oldest capital cities in the Nordic countries.
  • 2A real Viking ship called the Oseberg ship was buried in a hill for about 1,200 years before farmers dug it up, carvings and all, in the early 1900s.
  • 3The word 'ski' is Norwegian, and Oslo is home to Holmenkollen, one of the world's most famous ski jumps, still used for competitions after more than 100 years.
  • 4Nearly two-thirds of Oslo is forest, lakes, and hills, so kids really can hop off a tram and ski home through the woods.
  • 5Every December, the Nobel Peace Prize is handed out in Oslo's City Hall, the only Nobel Prize given here instead of in Sweden.
  • 6The wooden ship Fram sailed farther north and farther south than any other wooden ship ever has, carrying explorers Nansen and Amundsen toward both the North and South Poles.

📍 Places to visit

📍 Viking Ship Museum

This museum holds the actual 1,200-year-old Oseberg ship, dug whole out of a burial mound. Look closely at the bow and you can see swirling carvings made entirely by hand, long before anyone had power tools.

📍 Vigeland Sculpture Park

Inside Frogner Park stand over 200 statues by sculptor Gustav Vigeland, showing people laughing, wrestling, and even a famous little bronze boy stamping his foot in anger. Kids love hunting for their favorite one.

📍 Akershus Fortress

This stone castle has guarded Oslo's harbor for more than 700 years. Climb its old walls and you can look out over the very same fjord Viking ships once sailed.

📍 Holmenkollen Ski Jump

One of the world's most famous ski jumps towers above the forest outside Oslo. Skiers have soared off it for over 100 years, and today you can ride to the top and imagine flying through the air just like they do.
